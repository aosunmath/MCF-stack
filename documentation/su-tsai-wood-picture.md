# Su–Tsai–Wood infinite-time neck illustration

The source is Su, Tsai and Wood, *Infinite-Time Singularities of the Lagrangian Mean Curvature Flow*, [arXiv:2401.02228v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02228v3). Reviewed for this drawing: the Main Theorem, §1.3 and equation (1.1), §2.3 and Proposition 2.13, Assumption 3.1 and Remark 3.2, Definitions 3.3–3.4, Theorem 9.1, Proposition 9.5 and Corollary 9.6. Figures 1–2 were consulted for the desingularization and global topology. No source figure, mesh, or PDF is redistributed.

## Scope

The actual smooth flow is an embedded compact m-dimensional connected sum of two m-tori in a flat complex torus of real dimension 2m, for m ≥ 3. Its codimension is m. The example has one shrinking neck, not two. There are no ends on the compact manifold. The two ends of the local Lawlor model attach to the punctured tori. The limiting tori intersect at exactly one point. Their transverse tangent planes are distinct; this is not a multiplicity-two plane limit.

The picture uses an equal-angle local model, with characteristic angles π/m. Remark 3.2 permits such tangent planes by choosing the lattice generated by bases of the two planes. This does not assert rotational symmetry of the compact flow.

## Exact profile and normalization

Up to a common unitary rotation and with unit waist,

```math
\gamma(s)=(1+is)^{1/m},\qquad s\in\mathbb R,
\qquad L=\{\gamma(s)\omega:\omega\in S^{m-1}\subset\mathbb R^m\}.
```

Use the principal root. Then `Re(gamma^m)=1`, `|gamma|≥1`, and the two asymptotic phase angles are ±π/(2m). This is the equal-parameter member of the Lawlor family in §2.3, reparametrized and rotated. The radial tangent and spherical tangents have holomorphic volume proportional to `gamma^(m−1) gamma' = i/m`, proving constant phase in the Im Ω convention; the symplectic pairings vanish because tangent vectors to the real unit sphere are orthogonal to its radius. These are direct checks, not a numerical PDE computation.

The signed profiles ±γ depict the same family of spherical fibers: the antipodal map on S^(m−1) identifies the two signs. The pair of branches is not a pair of connected components. The gold points select opposite representatives of the waist sphere, whose radius is ε in the scaled model εL. The dashed radius ruler is an annotation, not a segment of L.

For sampling, set s = −sinh(v). Then r = ε cosh(v)^(1/m) and the phase is `(atan2(1,sinh(v))−π/2)/m`. This distributes points near the waist even at small ε. The fixed spatial crop is independent of model time. All open profile ends are display truncations.

## Time and the two limits

Equation (1.1) has the form `d(ε²)/dt = −c ε^m + o(ε^m)`. Dropping the remainder gives

```math
\varepsilon_{\rm model}/\varepsilon_0=(1+\tau)^{-1/(m-2)},
\qquad \tau=\tfrac c2(m-2)\varepsilon_0^{m-2}t.
```

The displayed times are dimensionless model times, with lengths in units ε₀. The static plate selects m=3, τ=0,1,5 and the infinite-time limit. The viewer also allows m=4,5,6. No absolute gluing scale or numerical value of c is assigned, and no chosen ε₀ is certified to meet the theorem's smallness threshold.

The left view approaches the signed profiles of two distinct transverse tangent planes. The right view magnifies by ε₀/ε and keeps the local neck at unit waist. At the limit button it shows the rescaled limit of Proposition 9.5; the code does not divide by zero. The global topology statement refers to the theorem, not a reconstructed torus embedding.

Every Lawlor neck is minimal. A changing dilation of it is not itself MCF. The true solution includes cutoff matching to the compact tori, a time-dependent Hamiltonian graph correction, and a correction to the leading scale law. None of these are solved by the illustration.
