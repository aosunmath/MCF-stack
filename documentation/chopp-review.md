# Chopp's mean curvature flow computations

Review date: 2026-11-07. This addition records five numerical shrinker candidates from David L. Chopp's 1994 paper. They are not marked as proved constructions or identified with later rigorous families. All five lie in the three-dimensional ambient group of the existing Shrinkers chapter. Their permanent example labels remain aliases of their complete section pages.

The 2005 Torres–Chopp–Walsh paper, [*Level set methods to compute minimal surfaces in a medium with exclusions (voids)*](https://doi.org/10.4171/IFB/119), was screened at the publisher abstract. Its algorithms evolve surfaces with orthogonality conditions on exclusion boundaries and a weighted-flow formulation. It is not recorded as a reviewed construction of a complete boundaryless Euclidean soliton. The author list also includes Gauss-curvature flow, intrinsic-Laplacian-of-curvature flow, and prescribed constant-mean-curvature surfaces in hyperbolic space; those are different equations or constrained problems. This is a source search for the requested special-solution addition, not an exhaustive bibliography of numerical interface motion.

## Added numerical candidates

All have intrinsic dimension two and codimension one in Euclidean three-space. Topology and compactness below describe the intended surfaces in the numerical construction. They are not existence conclusions for exact shrinkers.

The diagnostic is a finite-grid self-similarity check. For the noncompact candidates, boundary samples are excluded. These numbers are neither entropy nor Gaussian area, and are not certified error bounds. The metadata leaves those geometric quantities unrecorded. Embeddedness is also `not-recorded`, with an explanation that the target is embedded but an exact embedded solution has not been established by the computation. The candidate status appears in every case name, the notes, the manuscript, and the illustration caption; it must not disappear in a property search.

The normalization is `H = <X,nu>/2`, equivalently `vec H = -X^perp/2`, for the slice at time -1. Its associated flow, if an exact profile is obtained, is `M_t = sqrt(-t) Sigma`, `t < 0`. Chopp's test equation (3.1) uses an extinction scale `T`: if `H_S = <X,nu>/(2T)`, then `Sigma = T^(-1/2) S`. The fixed-volume display is not automatically this normalized slice. None of the five candidates has an explicit closed formula in the reviewed sources.

The two-ended surfaces have nonrotational observed cone links. The reported minimum openings are approximately 0.739 and 1.2627 radians, respectively. For the four-handle candidate, AIC95 compares approximately 72.3 degrees with its numerical critical rotational-expander aperture 66.04 degrees. The comparison and Assertion 1 underlie the conditional fattening argument. Uniqueness of the level-set evolution is consistent with fattening and distinct thin continuations. No unconditional fattening theorem for Chopp's specific computed mesh is claimed.

## Scope and deduplication

- The Angenent torus is a benchmark, already catalogued; it is not a new Chopp family.
- The four-handled compact dumbbell in Section 4.5 fails the test (`E = 0.584`). It is not added as a self-shrinker. Semiregular polyhedra and a trefoil in Section 5 are future-work proposals, not further constructions.
- The 1993 fixed-boundary soap films include circular and square catenoid-type tests, offset circles, three rings, a Scherk-type piece, and a twisted strip. These do not supply new complete boundaryless soliton families. The exact classical catenoid profile `r(z) = a cosh(z/a)` on `[-b,b]`, with boundary radius `R = a cosh(b/a)`, is recorded in the existing stationary entry. The quoted benchmark is `a = 0.4`, `b ≈ 0.277259`, `R ≈ 0.5`.
- Multiarmed dumbbells, pillows, and periodic tube lattices in Chopp–Sethian are initial-value numerical experiments, not constructions of ancient, eternal, or self-similar solutions. No such time extension is inferred. Geodesic curvature on a prescribed surface belongs to a different ambient problem, and forced prescribed-curvature flow is distinguished from unforced MCF.
- Later Platonic, one-ended, and Ilmanen–White constructions have their own sources and entries. Related symmetry or topology is not sufficient to merge them with a numerical candidate. The four-handle candidate is one entry covering both the 1994 and 1995 papers.

## Original illustrations and verification

`scripts/generate_chopp_schematics.py` generates five fixed-topology meshes and standalone SVG drawings. They are original schematics; no source image, mesh, or private research file is copied into the publication. Their field equations and arbitrary display scales are drawing devices, not exact formulas for Chopp's surfaces or numerical shrinker solvers.

The closed models reuse the project's concentric-sheet-and-neck drawing routine, with openings along the six coordinate rays or the eight diagonal rays. Connecting the two punctured sheets by `n` necks gives genus `n-1`. The saddle reuses the one-ended cubic-field drawing routine at genus one, rotated in the horizontal plane by pi/4 to give the stated reflection pattern. Its field before rotation is `z(x²+y²+z²-4) - A Im((x+iy)²) sqrt(x²+y²+1)`, with `A = 1.35 / 2^1.65`. The two-ended drawings reuse the paired-graph field with respectively two and four necks, at genus one and three. The former is stretched vertically by a factor of 2.5. The exact arbitrary drawing functions are in `generate_hmw_rays_geometry.py`, `generate_bns_geometry.py`, and `generate_iw_geometry.py`; this reuse makes no mathematical identification of their source families.

The interactive controls rotate, zoom, reveal a half-space cutaway, and restore the complete drawing. Blue and gold distinguish the two sides. The outer rims of noncompact models and the cutaway edges are display boundaries. The schematic does not preserve the source's numerical cone angle or certify asymptotic decay. No geometric parameter slider suggests a proved family where the source only reports one candidate.

Regeneration requires NumPy, SciPy, Pillow, scikit-image, and fast-simplification, then `python scripts/generate_chopp_schematics.py`. The generated `chopp-audit.json` checks connectedness, edge incidence, boundary loops, and Euler characteristic. The repository tests independently recompute topology from the committed mesh data and test clipping. These checks establish properties of the drawings, not the shrinker equation, geometric embeddedness, or source assertions. The ordinary Python and JavaScript suites, full build with link checks, and desktop/mobile reader checks are required before publication.
