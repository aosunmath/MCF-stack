Visual sources and individual credits

The credits below identify documented visual influences and parametrization sources. Where a paper credits an individual illustrator, that credit is retained separately from the paper's authorship. Newly generated schematic geometry is not the source author's numerical mesh.

Höskuldur P. Halldórsson

The planar classification, Sections 2–5 and Figures 4–19, supplies the ODEs and visual references for the annulus-dense shrinkers, rotators, expanding spirals and rotating shrinking spirals. The helicoidal paper, equations (3.3), (4.1) and (5.3), and Figures 1–4, supplies the helicoidal translator ODE and the classical minimal-surface formula attributed there to Wunderlich.

All displayed geometry is newly generated by scripts/generate_halldorsson_geometry.py; no paper figures or source meshes are redistributed. SciPy DOP853 integrates the arclength systems. A scalar Brent solve targets the irrational turning condition; finite precision does not certify irrationality. A comet preset uses finite-radius asymptotic shooting and is explicitly an approximation to the special orbit. Refinement and equation diagnostics are recorded in the geometry audit, without rigorous error bounds. Minimal helicoids are sampled from the exact formula. The viewers apply exact similarity motions to finite patches, and use equal spatial scales. Dashed circles mark accumulation sets, not extra flow components.

David L. Chopp

Chopp's Computation of Self-Similar Solutions for Mean Curvature Flow, Experimental Mathematics 3 (1994), 1–15 supplies the numerical candidates and visual references for the five Chopp entries: Figures 8–12 (cubical), 13–15 (octahedral), 16–19 (saddle), 20–23 (two-handled cone), and 26–28 (four-handled cone). The latter also appears in Angenent–Ilmanen–Chopp, A Computed Example of Nonuniqueness of Mean Curvature Flow in R3 (1995), p. 1943.

Chopp is also credited for Figure 1 in Ilmanen–White, Fattening in Mean Curvature Flow, which informs our two-ended topology drawing, and Figure 2 in Hoffman–Martín–White, a visual reference for the cubical silhouette. Our corresponding models are schematics, not recomputations of Chopp's numerical surfaces. Source review and drawing provenance.

Tom Ilmanen

The conjectural genus-eight picture credited to Ilmanen in Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller, Mean curvature self-shrinkers of high genus: Non-compact examples, Figure 1, is a visual reference for the sphere–plane configuration. Our KKM drawing uses the paper's Scherk equation and a project-specific drawing map; it is not a solved shrinker or the paper's matched initial surface. Drawing provenance.

O. Michael Melko

Melko's Visualizing Minimal Surfaces (2010), equations (16)–(19), provides the stationary Costa parametrization used for the ancient-Costa illustration. The project evaluates this parametrization and adds a truncated linearized normal mode. Those perturbed frames are not a numerical construction of the nonlinear ancient flow. Implementation and limitations.

Lee–Wang quadric flow

The shrinker time plate and circle-action viewer are original drawings from Lee and Wang, Hamiltonian stationary cones and self-similar solutions in higher dimension, Theorem 1.1 and Sections 2–3. They sample the exact unit-weight coordinates in C³; no PDE is solved. The displayed phase leaves and projected orbit curves are labeled as such. The SVG and browser canvas use the same coordinate generator, with a fixed ambient crop and scale across times. No figure, mesh, or software from the paper is reproduced.

The companion expander illustration samples the exact real slices x₃ = ±√(x₁² + x₂² + 2t) at positive times and the singular cone at t = 0. The comparison keeps one spatial scale and a fixed radial display cutoff. Its circle-orbit diagram is drawn in the exact two-plane spanned by x and i(x₁,x₂,−x₃) in R⁶; changing the circle phase keeps flow time fixed. These real slices are not themselves mean curvature flows in R³. See the expander geometry and verification notes.

Mario B. Schulz

The illustration for the work of Reto Buzano, Huy The Nguyen, and Mario B. Schulz follows the qualitative geometry of Section 4, Figures 3–4 of Noncompact self-shrinkers for mean curvature flow with arbitrary genus, version 2. These are visual references as well as mathematical source material. The authors produced their simulations with Kenneth Brakke's Surface Evolver. Our mesh instead samples an illustrative implicit field. BNS drawing provenance.

Schulz is individually credited for Figure 5, the genus-five numerical picture in Hoffman–Martín–White, Generating Shrinkers by Mean Curvature Flow, version 3. It informs our sphere–plane drawing and its interior view, together with Torralbo's figures below.

Schulz's Geometric Analysis Gallery, especially its self-shrinker collection, is an additional resource for these visualizations. The documented sources used for our drawings are the paper figures identified above; the gallery link does not assert that the website itself was consulted during the original implementation, or that its images or meshes were reused.

Drawing methods and their scope

  • Formula sampling and projection. Parametrizations are sampled into curves or triangles. Revolving a profile, taking a slice, or projecting from a higher-dimensional ambient space is identified in the caption. The mesh is a finite representation of the formula.
  • Numerical ODE profiles. SciPy integration and, where required, Brent root finding produce profiles with closing or matching conditions. DOP853 is used in several generators. The interactive Abresch–Langer viewer also has a custom period quadrature and bisection implementation. Solver settings and residuals are recorded in the relevant code and audit.
  • Numerical PDE and flow models. Selected generators use finite differences, sparse linear solves, or truncated linearized evolution. Their specific discretizations and limitations accompany the figures. A finite-domain calculation or linearized perturbation is not automatically a computed complete soliton or nonlinear ancient flow.
  • Implicit schematic meshes. Marching cubes extracts a surface from a sampled drawing field; quadric simplification reduces its triangle count. Connectivity, boundary loops, and Euler characteristic are checked where recorded. These checks describe the displayed mesh and do not establish the shrinker equation.
  • Rendering. Custom Python and JavaScript code produces SVG plates and Canvas/WebGL viewers with projection, shading, rotation, zoom, and cutaways. The supplied Chu–Sun replay pages additionally use three.js. Pillow supplies raster panels in selected plates; trimesh supplies selected mesh operations. Cutaway edges and finite display rims are explained in the captions.

Code, provenance, and attribution

The figure-generation scripts, viewer code and geometry assets, and figure provenance notes distinguish source formulas from drawing choices and record numerical settings where available. The figure registry connects these assets to the catalogue. Public links point to original publications; supplied PDFs and private research files are not redistributed.

For attribution, retain the named visual source and figure locator, the numerical method or schematic construction, and the implementation credit. “Original” in older captions refers to newly generated geometry or rendering and should not be read as an absence of visual influence. A shared shape or topology does not identify surfaces produced by different mathematical constructions.
