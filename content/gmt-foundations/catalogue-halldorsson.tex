\chapter{Nonclosed shrinking curves dense in an annulus}\label{sec:catalogue-halldorsson-dense-shrinkers}
\begin{example}[Nonclosed shrinking curves dense in an annulus]\label{ex:catalogue-halldorsson-dense-shrinkers}
The nonclosed branch of the planar shrinking-curve classification is recorded in
\cite{CatHalldorsson2012}, Theorem~5.1 and Section~5, PDF pp.~11--13.
These are smooth complete immersed curves $X:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2$ of
codimension one, in the normalization $\vec H=-X^\perp/2$.
With arclength $s$, left normal $N=iT$, and signed curvature $k$, solve
\[
 u'=uv,\qquad v'=\frac12-u^2,\qquad \theta'=u,\qquad
 X(s)=-2i e^{i\theta(s)}(u(s)+iv(s)).
\]
Here $u(0)=r_{\min}/2$, $v(0)=0$, $\theta(0)=0$ and
$0<r_{\min}<\sqrt2$. The phase orbit $(u,v)$ is periodic; write $P$ for its
period and $\alpha=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^P u(s)\,ds$.
Choose $r_{\min}$ so that $\alpha$ is irrational. The period analysis gives
$1/2<\alpha<1/\sqrt2$, and every irrational value in this interval occurs.
This is an ODE construction with an irrational turning condition, not a closed formula.

The image is dense in the annulus $r_{\min}\leq |X|\leq r_{\max}$, where
\[
 r_{\min}e^{-r_{\min}^2/4}=r_{\max}e^{-r_{\max}^2/4},
 \qquad r_{\max}>\sqrt2.
\]
Successive radial excursions are congruent by rotation through $2\pi\alpha$.
The curvature is positive and periodic, with
$k e^{-|X|^2/4}$ constant: local strict convexity does not imply global embeddedness.
The curve is nonembedded and nonproper. Its parameter manifold is the
noncompact line, with two intrinsic ends, despite its bounded image; neither end
escapes to spatial infinity. Completeness follows from the global unit-speed
parametrization. The annulus is the image closure, not a two-dimensional flow.

The exact evolution from this ODE-defined profile is
$M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,X(\mathbb R)$, $-\infty<t<0$, interpreted as a parametrized
immersion. At every regular time its Gaussian area and entropy are infinite,
with arclength counted on the parameter domain: an infinite-length curve lies
in a bounded set, so every fixed Gaussian weight is bounded below there.
This elementary consequence is separate from the source classification.
No finite-entropy or stability theorem is claimed for this branch.
For rational $\alpha=m/\ell$, the curve closes and is already recorded in
Example~\ref{ex:catalogue-abresch-langer}; the circle and stationary line are
Examples~\ref{ex:catalogue-round-sphere} and~\ref{ex:catalogue-plane-shrinker}.
The drawing numerically shoots for $\alpha=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ and shows finitely many
excursions. Finite precision cannot certify irrationality or depict the full dense image.
\end{example}

\chapter{Halldorsson helicoidal rotating translators}\label{sec:catalogue-halldorsson-helicoidal-translators}
\begin{example}[Halldorsson helicoidal rotating translators]\label{ex:catalogue-halldorsson-helicoidal-translators}
For every pitch $h>0$, Halld\'orsson constructs a one-parameter family of
complete immersed helicoidal surfaces in $\mathbb R^3$ that rotate at unit
angular speed and, equivalently, translate with velocity $-he_3$.
Here pitch means vertical displacement per radian, so one full screw turn
rises by $2\pi h$. Source: \cite{CatHalldorsson2013}, Theorem~4.1,
equations~(3.1)--(3.4), (4.1)--(4.2), PDF pp.~4--9.

Let $s\in\mathbb R$ be arclength on a planar generating curve and solve
\[
 k=\frac{\tau(\tau^2+h^2)-\nu}{\tau^2+\nu^2+h^2},\qquad
 \tau'=1+k\nu,\qquad \nu'=-k\tau,\qquad \theta'=k,
 \qquad X=(\tau+i\nu)e^{i\theta}.
\]
Initial data $(\tau,\nu,\theta)(0)=(0,d,0)$, $d\geq0$, select its minimum
radius, up to the rotations and orientation changes of the construction.
The surface and its eternal evolution are
\[
 F(s,\phi)=(e^{i\phi}X(s),h\phi),\quad(s,\phi)\in\mathbb R^2,
 \qquad M_t=F(\mathbb R^2)-ht e_3,\quad t\in\mathbb R.
\]
The same evolving images are $(e^{it}F_{\mathrm{horizontal}},F_3)$;
their parametrizations differ by the screw symmetry. With
\[
 n=\frac{(h e^{i\phi}i e^{i\theta},-\tau)}{\sqrt{\tau^2+h^2}},
 \qquad H=-\frac{h\tau}{\sqrt{\tau^2+h^2}},
\]
one has $\vec H=(-he_3)^\perp$. Thus these belong to Translators even though
the source also describes them as rotating surfaces. The profiles are
ODE-defined, and are different from the helicoid-like translators in
Example~\ref{ex:catalogue-translating-helicoids}.

Each parameter surface is a complete proper immersion of $\mathbb R^2$,
of dimension two, codimension one, genus zero, and one intrinsic end.
Properness follows from $|X(s)|\to\infty$ as $s\to\pm\infty$ and $F_3=h\phi$.
The generating curve has two radially escaping, individually embedded arms;
these are not two topological ends of the surface. Both arms wind infinitely,
have curvature tending to zero and infinite total absolute curvature, and
satisfy $rT/X\to\pm i$. Arms can intersect one another. The centrally
symmetric $d=0$ member is embedded; in general the surface is embedded
exactly when its planar generating curve is, since its height determines
$\phi$. The source exhibits crossings but gives no complete numerical
threshold in $(h,d)$ for embeddedness. The displayed normal gives changing
sign of $H$, so the surfaces are not mean convex or convex.

For fixed nonzero $d$ and fixed initial tangent, as $h\to0$ the generating
curves converge on compact arclength intervals to
$X_0(s)=id e^{-is/d}$; this is Corollary~4.12, PDF p.~12. It is a limit of
profiles: the $h=0$ surface parametrization degenerates and does not define
a stationary cylindrical translator. As $h\to\infty$, the profile equation
becomes $k=\tau$, giving the rotating curves of
Example~\ref{ex:catalogue-rotating-curves} and their product surfaces.
No Gaussian-area, entropy or general perturbative stability conclusion is
recorded in the cited passages. The viewer shows finite numerical ODE
patches, with pitch, profile and time choices; display edges are not boundaries
of the complete surfaces.
\end{example}

\chapter{Helicoidal minimal surfaces as stationary flows}\label{sec:catalogue-halldorsson-minimal-helicoids}
\begin{example}[Helicoidal minimal surfaces as stationary flows]\label{ex:catalogue-halldorsson-minimal-helicoids}
Section~5 of \cite{CatHalldorsson2013}, PDF pp.~12--14, derives the classical
helicoidal minimal surfaces and attributes them to Wunderlich.
For $h>0$, $a\in\mathbb R$, and $(u,\phi)\in\mathbb R^2$, an exact formula is
\[
 X(u)=(h\sinh(hu)+ia\cosh(hu))e^{-iau},\qquad
 F(u,\phi)=(e^{i\phi}X(u),h\phi).
\]
A constant horizontal rotation may be included. The profile parameter $u$
is not arclength; in the notation of the source,
$\tau=h\sinh(hu)$ and $s=(a^2+h^2)\tau/h^2$.
These codimension-one minimal immersions in $\mathbb R^3$ give exact eternal
stationary flows $M_t=F(\mathbb R^2)$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$.

For $a=0$, changing variable $r=h\sinh(hu)$ gives the usual embedded
helicoid $(r\cos\phi,r\sin\phi,h\phi)$, $r,\phi\in\mathbb R$.
For $a\ne0$, the profile has infinitely many self-intersections, which give
self-intersections of the surface at the same height. In each case the
parameter manifold is a complete proper noncompact plane, of genus zero
and one end. Properness follows directly from $|X(u)|\to\infty$ and
$F_3=h\phi$; completeness also follows from properness of the immersion.
The screw symmetry has pitch $h$, with vertical period $2\pi h$ as a
symmetry of the image. Genus and ends here describe the full parameter
plane, not a quotient by that vertical period.

For $a\ne0$, the profile has minimum radius $|a|$, reflection symmetry,
constant-sign curvature tending to zero on both arms, and
$rT/X\to\pm(h-ia)/\sqrt{h^2+a^2}$. These are properties of the generating
curve, not convexity of the minimal surface. For fixed $a\ne0$, its
arclength parametrization tends locally smoothly to the circle
$ia e^{-is/a}$ as $h\to0$. Another scaling gives the limiting profile
\[
 X_0(s)=\frac{s^{1-ib}}{1-ib},\qquad s>0,\quad b\in\mathbb R,
 \qquad s^{1-ib}=s e^{-ib\log s}.
\]
This logarithmic spiral is a limiting generating curve in equation~(5.4),
not itself a claimed planar curve-shortening soliton; the zero-pitch
surface parametrization is degenerate. No entropy, Gaussian-area or
stability classification is recorded here.

Section~6 studies helicoidal surfaces of nonzero constant mean curvature
for comparison. Constant mean curvature alone does not make them
stationary or self-similar solutions of ordinary MCF, so that comparison
family is not added as a new special flow. Its round cylindrical case is
already represented by Example~\ref{ex:catalogue-round-cylinder}.
The figures sample the exact formula, with parameters $h$ and $a$ and
interactive rotation and zoom.
\end{example}
