\chapter{Round shrinking spheres}\label{sec:catalogue-round-sphere}
Throughout this collection, the normalized self-shrinker equation is
$\vec H=-X^\perp/2$, and a normalized shrinker $\Sigma$ generates
$M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma$, $-\infty<t<0$.
The standard examples appear in \cite{CM12}, pp.~757--759, and
\cite{CatDLN2018}, Section~3.3, PDF p.~8.

\begin{example}[Round shrinking spheres]\label{ex:catalogue-round-sphere}
For an integer $n\geq1$, an extinction time $T\in\mathbb R$, and a center
$a\in\mathbb R^{n+1}$, the exact family is
\[
 M_t=\{a+\sqrt{2n(T-t)}\,\omega:\omega\in S^n(1)\},
 \qquad -\infty<t<T.
\]
The centered normalized slice is $S^n(\sqrt{2n})$ at $t=-1$.
\begin{itemize}
\item These are smooth compact embedded hypersurfaces without boundary,
complete and proper, with topology $S^n$ and full rotational symmetry.
The case $n=1$ is the shrinking circle in the plane.
\item With outward normal and $H=\operatorname{div}\nu$, all principal
curvatures are $1/\sqrt{2n(T-t)}$. Thus the flow is strictly convex,
$H=\sqrt{n/(2(T-t))}$ and $|A|^2=1/(2(T-t))$.
It becomes extinct at $a$ in a Type~I singularity at time $T$.
\item The round sphere is entropy stable and $F$-stable, where $F$-stability
allows the Gaussian center and scale to vary. These are the stability
notions in \cite{CM12}, Theorems~0.12 and~0.16; stability of Gaussian
area with center and scale fixed is a different question.
\item The normalized Gaussian integral gives
\[
 \lambda(S^n(\sqrt{2n}))
 =(4\pi)^{-n/2}(2n)^{n/2}e^{-n/2}|S^n(1)|.
\]
In particular, a two-sphere has entropy $4/e$.
\end{itemize}
The three-dimensional picture is the exact surface for $n=2$; for other
dimensions a round profile represents the symmetry reduction.
\end{example}

\chapter{Generalized shrinking cylinders}\label{sec:catalogue-round-cylinder}
The cylinder family is recorded in \cite{CM12}, pp.~758--759.
The spherical factor and the number of Euclidean directions are independent
discrete parameters.

\begin{example}[Generalized shrinking cylinders]\label{ex:catalogue-round-cylinder}
Let $n\geq2$ and $1\leq k<n$. In
$\mathbb R^{k+1}\times\mathbb R^{n-k}=\mathbb R^{n+1}$ the exact flow is
\[
 M_t=S^k(\sqrt{2k(-t)})\times\mathbb R^{n-k},\qquad -\infty<t<0.
\]
Equivalently, $(\omega,z)\mapsto(\sqrt{2k(-t)}\,\omega,z)$,
with $\omega\in S^k(1)$ and $z\in\mathbb R^{n-k}$, is a normal
parametrization of the flow.
\begin{itemize}
\item These are complete, proper, noncompact embedded hypersurfaces without
boundary, with topology $S^k\times\mathbb R^{n-k}$ and polynomial area
growth. Their symmetries include $O(k+1)$ on the spherical factor and all
Euclidean isometries in the axial factor.
\item There are $k$ positive principal curvatures
$1/\sqrt{2k(-t)}$ and $n-k$ zero principal curvatures. Hence they are
mean convex and weakly convex, and $|A|^2=1/(2(-t))$.
At time zero the spherical factor collapses onto the axial subspace.
\item Topologically there are two ends if $n-k=1$, and one end if
$n-k\geq2$. Each end has exactly the displayed product geometry.
\item They are entropy stable among the complete embedded hypersurfaces
considered by \cite{CM12}, but are not $F$-stable when $1\leq k<n$;
see Theorems~0.12 and~0.16. The Euclidean factor leaves entropy unchanged:
$\lambda(M_{-1})=\lambda(S^k(\sqrt{2k}))$.
For the round cylinder in $\mathbb R^3$, this is $\sqrt{2\pi/e}$.
\end{itemize}
For $n=2$, $k=1$, an interactive picture displays an exact finite window of
the infinite cylinder. End caps are not part of the solution.
\end{example}

\chapter{Planes through the shrinker center}\label{sec:catalogue-plane-shrinker}
Planes are stationary solutions that also satisfy the shrinker equation;
see \cite{CatDLN2018}, Sections~2.5 and~3.3, and \cite{CM12},
Theorem~0.16.

\begin{example}[Planes through the shrinker center]\label{ex:catalogue-plane-shrinker}
Let $P$ be an $n$-dimensional linear subspace of $\mathbb R^N$, where
$1\leq n<N$. Then
\[
 M_t=P,\qquad -\infty<t<\infty,
 \qquad \vec H=0,\quad X^\perp=0.
\]
Thus $P$ is both a stationary flow and a normalized self-shrinker.
An affine plane is a shrinker centered at any point of that plane; it
satisfies the centered equation at the origin only if it contains the origin.
\begin{itemize}
\item The inclusion is complete, proper, embedded, flat, noncompact and
boundaryless, with topology $\mathbb R^n$ and Euclidean volume growth.
It has two ends for $n=1$ and one end for $n\geq2$.
\item The solution has no finite singular time. The identity
$\sqrt{-t}\,P=P$ does not describe a geometric collapse.
Its entropy is exactly one.
\item In codimension one it is entropy stable and $F$-stable under the
notions in \cite{CM12}; no conclusion about a different constrained
variation problem is intended.
\end{itemize}
The picture shows a bounded patch of a plane in $\mathbb R^3$. Its boundary
is a viewing boundary, not a boundary of the flow.
\end{example}

\chapter{Abresch--Langer closed shrinking curves}\label{sec:catalogue-abresch-langer}
The closed planar curves are classified by \cite{CatAL1986}.
The supplied sources record the curvature conservation laws in
\cite{CatDLN2018}, Theorem~1, PDF pp.~5--6, and the rotation-index
description in \cite{CatCMComplexity2020}, Section~6.3, supplied PDF p.~22.

\begin{example}[Abresch--Langer closed shrinking curves]\label{ex:catalogue-abresch-langer}
Besides the round circle, there are noncircular closed immersed curves
$\gamma_{m,\ell}\subset\mathbb R^2$, indexed primitively by coprime
positive integers satisfying
\[
 \frac12<\frac m\ell<\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]
Here $m$ is the rotation index and $\ell$ is the number of periods of the
curvature. Their flows are $\sqrt{-t}\,\gamma_{m,\ell}$ for $t<0$.
An ODE description in the normalization of this collection is
\[
 \kappa_{\theta\theta}+\kappa=\frac1{2\kappa},\qquad
 \kappa>0,\qquad
 \kappa_\theta^2+\kappa^2-\log\kappa=E.
\]
Choose the nonconstant periodic solution with curvature period
$2\pi m/\ell$. With
$T(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and
$N(\theta)=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, reconstruction gives
\[
 X(\theta)=2\kappa_\theta T(\theta)-2\kappa N(\theta),
 \qquad \theta\in\mathbb R/(2\pi m\mathbb Z).
\]
This is an ODE construction with a closing condition, not a closed
elementary formula for $\kappa$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The domain is $S^1$. These are compact, complete, proper immersions
with self-intersections. Their curvature is positive after orienting the
curve, so they are locally strictly convex; their images do not bound
convex embedded domains.
\item The round circle is the only embedded closed shrinking curve.
The noncircular curves have finite entropy strictly greater than two,
as recorded in \cite{CatCMComplexity2020}, Section~6.3.
\item The conservation law $\kappa e^{-|X|^2/4}=C>0$ relates curvature
to distance from the shrinking center. Compactness makes the shrinking
flow a Type~I flow becoming extinct at the origin.
\end{itemize}
The interactive figure starts with $(m,\ell)=(2,3)$. Enter other coprime
indices in the stated range, or use the example buttons, to compute more
numerical profiles. The viewer supports $m,\ell\leq100$ and reports the
numerical closing gap. This display limit is not a restriction of the
classification theorem. The static drawing and printed figure show
$(2,3)$. These computations illustrate the closing condition; their
residuals are not certified error bounds.
\end{example}

\chapter{Angenent's shrinking torus}\label{sec:catalogue-angenent-torus}
Angenent constructs an embedded rotational self-shrinker with topology
$S^1\times S^{n-1}$ for each $n\geq2$ in \cite{CatAngenent1992}.
We use the normalized profile equation from \cite{CatDLN2018},
Section~3.3 and equation~(5.2), PDF pp.~8 and~14.

\begin{example}[Angenent's shrinking torus]\label{ex:catalogue-angenent-torus}
There is a simple closed profile $s\mapsto(x(s),r(s))$ in $r>0$ whose
rotation gives
\[
 X(s,\omega)=(x(s),r(s)\omega)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n,
 \qquad \omega\in S^{n-1}(1).
\]
For Euclidean arclength $s$ and tangent angle $\alpha$, the profile solves
\[
 x'=\cos\alpha,\qquad r'=\sin\alpha,\qquad
 \alpha'=\left(\frac{n-1}{r}-\frac r2\right)\cos\alpha
                +\frac x2\sin\alpha.
\]
Equivalently, it is a closed geodesic of
$r^{2(n-1)}e^{-(x^2+r^2)/2}(dx^2+dr^2)$ on $r>0$.
The theorem selects a closed orbit by shooting; the profile is not given
by a closed formula. Its flow is $\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma$, $t<0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The hypersurface is compact, embedded, complete and proper, with
topology $S^1\times S^{n-1}$, rotational $O(n)$ symmetry and reflection
symmetry across $x=0$. When $n=2$ it is a torus in $\mathbb R^3$.
\item It is not mean convex. Indeed, the compact embedded mean-convex
classification in \cite{CM12}, Theorem~0.17, would force a sphere.
It shrinks to the origin with a Type~I singularity.
\item It is entropy unstable by \cite{CM12}, Theorem~0.12,
which applies to this compact embedded non-spherical hypersurface.
No exact entropy value or global uniqueness assertion is supplied here. Berchenko--Kogan's numerical calculation estimates its entropy as approximately $1.85122$ (\cite{CatBerchenkoKogan2021}, Section 5, PDF pp.~10--12). This estimate has no certified error enclosure; Section 6, Question 6.3, leaves rigorous error bounds open. The proved bound for the separately constructed variational torus is recorded in Example \ref{ex:catalogue-drugan-nguyen-torus}.
\end{itemize}
The interactive figure revolves a numerically closed solution of this
ODE; its caption records the shooting residual. The profile is not circular.
The variational torus in \cite{CatDLN2018}, Theorem~2, is a separate
existence construction; that survey does not identify it with Angenent's torus.
\end{example}

\chapter{Drugan's immersed shrinking spheres}\label{sec:catalogue-drugan-sphere}
\cite{CatDrugan2015}, Theorem~1.1, p.~3139, proves the existence of a
nonembedded immersed $S^2$ self-shrinker in $\mathbb R^3$.
\cite{CatDLN2018}, Theorem~3, PDF pp.~14--15, gives the corresponding
existence statement for $S^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}$, $n\geq2$.

\begin{example}[Drugan's immersed shrinking spheres]\label{ex:catalogue-drugan-sphere}
For each $n\geq2$, there is a smooth rotational immersion
$X:S^n\to\mathbb R^{n+1}$ satisfying $\vec H=-X^\perp/2$ whose image
is not embedded. The associated immersed flow is
$X_t=\sqrt{-t}\,X$, $-\infty<t<0$.
In the three-dimensional construction, a graphical piece of the profile,
written as axial height $z=\gamma(r)$ for $r>0$, solves
\[
 \frac{\gamma''}{1+(\gamma')^2}
   =\left(\frac r2-\frac1r\right)\gamma'-\frac\gamma2.
\]
This is equation~(1.2) of \cite{CatDrugan2015}, p.~3140; the paper's
radial variable $x$ is denoted here by $r$. Equation~(1.3) describes the
same curve as a radius depending on height.
Shooting and continuation through profile turning points produce the
closed immersed sphere; a single graphical piece does not describe the
whole surface.
\begin{itemize}
\item The parameter domain is the compact sphere, so the induced metric
is complete and the immersion is proper. The image has self-intersections
and is not the round sphere.
\item The construction has rotational symmetry and reflection symmetry
across a hyperplane perpendicular to the rotation axis. It has no ends.
The homothetic flow contracts to the origin with Type~I curvature growth.
\item The cited existence argument supplies neither an elementary
parametrization nor uniqueness or an exact entropy value. Embedded
classification and embedded stability theorems cannot be applied to this
immersion by dropping their embeddedness hypothesis.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Numerical picture.}
The picture computes the rotational shooting construction in
\cite{CatDrugan2015}, Sections~2--5, and can be compared with Figure~1
on p.~3141. It shows the two-dimensional immersed sphere in
$\mathbb R^3$, not a higher-dimensional projection.
Let $s$ be arc length along the meridian and let $\theta$ be its tangent
angle measured from the positive radial direction. Substituting
$\gamma'=\tan\theta$ into the graph equation gives
\[
 \frac{dr}{ds}=\cos\theta,\qquad
 \frac{dz}{ds}=\sin\theta,\qquad
 \frac{d\theta}{ds}
 =\left(\frac r2-\frac1r\right)\sin\theta-\frac z2\cos\theta.
\]
This system continues through the vertical tangents where the graph
equation becomes unsuitable for numerical integration. At the upper
pole $(r,z)=(0,b)$, smoothness requires $\theta=0$. The even expansion
from the graph equation, consistent with Section~6 of the paper, is
\[
 z(r)=b-\frac b8r^2-\frac{b(b^2+4)}{1024}r^4+O(r^6).
\]
We start at $r=10^{-4}$ using this expansion and shoot in $b$ until
$z=0$ at the second radial turning point, where
$\theta=-3\pi/2$. This is the orthogonal reflection condition in
Section~5, pp.~3154--3155. The computation gives
\[
 b\simeq0.39345656,\qquad r_{\mathrm{reflection}}\simeq0.83684239.
\]
Reflection across $z=0$, with the order of traversal reversed, completes
the meridian from $(0,b)$ to $(0,-b)$. Revolving it gives
\[
 X(s,\varphi)=(r(s)\cos\varphi,r(s)\sin\varphi,z(s)),
 \qquad 0\leq\varphi<2\pi,
\]
with the angular circles collapsed at the two poles. This is a numerical
parametrization at time $t=-1$; the corresponding flow is
$X_t=\sqrt{-t}\,X$ for $t<0$.

The meridian view shows the self-intersections directly. The revolution
angle control reveals interior sheets by removing angular sectors;
the resulting cut edges are display boundaries, not boundaries of the
immersed sphere. The two colors distinguish the shot half from its
reflected continuation. The shooting height residual is below $10^{-10}$
before rounding the exported mesh. Halving the initial radius and maximum
step and tightening the integration tolerances changes the computed pole
height and reflection radius by less than $10^{-9}$. These are
floating-point convergence diagnostics, not certified error bounds.
In particular, numerical shooting does not prove existence, uniqueness,
or that this root is the supremum used in Drugan's proof.
\end{example}

\chapter{The Clifford shrinking torus in four-space}\label{sec:catalogue-clifford-torus}
We follow the supplied June~2020 version of \cite{CatELS2020}; its
Sections~2, 4 and~5 distinguish exact geometry, Hamiltonian stability,
flow instability and local rigidity.

\begin{example}[The Clifford shrinking torus in four-space]\label{ex:catalogue-clifford-torus}
The exact normalized immersion is
\[
 X(\theta_1,\theta_2)=\sqrt2
 (\cos\theta_1,\sin\theta_1,\cos\theta_2,\sin\theta_2),
 \qquad (\theta_1,\theta_2)\in(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
It lies in $\mathbb R^4=\mathbb C^2$, and
$X_t=\sqrt{-t}\,X$ gives a smooth flow for $t<0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item This is a compact embedded Lagrangian torus of dimension two and
codimension two, complete and proper. It is minimal in $S^3(2)$ and has
the product rotation symmetry $U(1)\times U(1)$.
\item The induced metric is $2(d\theta_1^2+d\theta_2^2)$; it is
intrinsically flat, has area $8\pi^2$, $|\vec H|=1$, and
$\vec H=-X/2$. Its entropy is exactly $2\pi/e$
(\cite{CatELS2020}, Section~2 and Lemma~2.4, PDF pp.~3--6).
\item It is $F$-unstable under Lagrangian variations but $F$-stable under
Hamiltonian variations after accounting for translations. It is locally
volume minimizing under Hamiltonian variations (Lemmas~4.1--4.2 and
Theorem~4.3, PDF p.~13).
\item Nevertheless it is not a local entropy minimum, even within
Hamiltonian variations. For every $\varepsilon>0$ and $k\in\mathbb N$
there is a compact embedded Lagrangian torus Hamiltonian isotopic and
$\varepsilon$-close in $C^k$ whose first finite-time singularity has a
blow-up outside the $U(2)$-orbit of the Clifford torus
(Corollary~4.8 and Theorem~4.9, PDF p.~16).
\item A compact embedded two-dimensional self-shrinker in $\mathbb C^2$
sufficiently $C^{2,\alpha}$-close, for $0<\alpha<1$, is the Clifford
torus up to translation, dilation and rotation (Theorem~5.7, PDF p.~29).
This is a local rigidity statement.
\end{itemize}
The interactive picture rotates the second and fourth coordinates in
$\mathbb R^4$ and then projects onto the first three coordinates. Apparent intersections in a
projection do not mean that the original torus is immersed nonembedded.
\end{example}

\chapter{Products of closed shrinking curves}\label{sec:catalogue-curve-products}
\cite{CatSmoczyk2005}, equation~(1.4), p.~2984, describes these
Lagrangian products. That paper uses $\vec H=-X^\perp$; spatial dilation
by $\sqrt2$ converts its examples to the convention used here.

\begin{example}[Products of closed shrinking curves]\label{ex:catalogue-curve-products}
Let $m\geq2$ and let each
$\gamma_j:S^1\to\mathbb R^2$ be a normalized closed Abresch--Langer
shrinker, including the round circle if desired. Then
\[
 X(u_1,\ldots,u_m)
   =(\gamma_1(u_1),\ldots,\gamma_m(u_m))\in\mathbb R^{2m},
 \qquad (u_1,\ldots,u_m)\in(S^1)^m
\]
satisfies $\vec H=-X^\perp/2$, and $X_t=\sqrt{-t}\,X$ for $t<0$.
The product formula is exact, but any noncircular factor still requires
the curvature ODE and closing condition.
\begin{itemize}
\item The domain is the compact $m$-torus. These are complete proper
immersions of dimension and codimension $m$, Lagrangian in $\mathbb C^m$,
with flat normal bundle and nowhere vanishing mean-curvature vector.
The product metric is intrinsically flat.
\item A product is embedded when every factor is an embedded circle.
A noncircular factor has self-intersections, and so does the product
immersion. Multiple covers must not be treated as embeddings.
\item Unless all factor images are circles, the image is not contained
in a sphere. The all-circle product in $\mathbb R^4$ is the Clifford
torus described separately above.
\item These compact products have no ends and finite entropy. The centered Gaussian integral factors, so
$\lambda(\gamma_1\times\cdots\times\gamma_m)=\prod_j\lambda(\gamma_j)$,
with the immersion multiplicity counted. They have a Type~I
homothetic extinction at the origin. No general stability or uniqueness
claim for this family is taken from the cited product construction.
\end{itemize}
The figure displays the planar factors and identifies the ambient
dimension of their product.
\end{example}

\chapter{Higher-codimension spherical shrinkers}\label{sec:catalogue-spherical-minimal}
Minimal submanifolds of a round sphere give Euclidean self-shrinkers.
This correspondence, and a converse with hypotheses, appear in
\cite{CatSmoczyk2005}, p.~2985 and Theorem~1.1. We use the
$\vec H=-X^\perp/2$ scaling.

\begin{example}[Higher-codimension spherical shrinkers]\label{ex:catalogue-spherical-minimal}
Let $n\geq2$, $N\geq n+2$, and let $F:M^n\to S^{N-1}(\sqrt{2n})$ be a smooth
compact minimal immersion. Viewed in $\mathbb R^N$, it satisfies
\[
 \vec H=-\frac12F,
 \qquad F_t=\sqrt{-t}\,F,\quad -\infty<t<0.
\]
An explicit family is obtained for integers $p,q\geq1$, $n=p+q$, by
\[
 F(u,v)=(\sqrt{2p}\,u,\sqrt{2q}\,v),
 \quad (u,v)\in S^p(1)\times S^q(1),
 \quad N=p+q+2.
\]
To avoid repeating the Clifford torus entry, one may take $p+q\geq3$
in this explicit family.
\begin{itemize}
\item For a general compact minimal immersion, the resulting shrinker is
complete and proper; it is embedded when the original immersion is
embedded. Topology, symmetry and further curvature properties depend
on the chosen minimal submanifold and are not fixed by this construction.
\item Its mean-curvature vector has constant length $\sqrt{n/2}$, and
the principal normal $\vec H/|\vec H|$ is parallel in the normal bundle.
Conversely, a compact self-shrinker of dimension $n\geq2$ is spherical
if and only if its mean-curvature vector is nowhere zero and its
principal normal is parallel (Smoczyk's Theorem~1.1).
\item The displayed product family is compact embedded of Euclidean
codimension two, with topology $S^p\times S^q$ and
$O(p+1)\times O(q+1)$ symmetry. It has $|A|^2=1$ and is minimal
inside $S^{n+1}(\sqrt{2n})$; these identities follow directly from its
two round factors.
\item For these explicit products, Gaussian factorization gives
$\lambda(S^p(\sqrt{2p})\times S^q(\sqrt{2q}))=\lambda(S^p(\sqrt{2p}))\lambda(S^q(\sqrt{2q}))$.
For a general compact minimal immersion $M^n\subset S^{N-1}(\sqrt{2n})$,
$F_{0,1}(M)=(4\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-n/2}\operatorname{Area}(M)$ by the constant radius.
\item The explicit spherical products are locally rigid: for fixed $p,q\geq1$ and $0<\alpha<1$, a normalized shrinker in $\mathbb R^N$, $N\geq p+q+2$, sufficiently close in $C^{2,\alpha}$ to the product is an ambient rotation of it. This is \cite{CatSunZhu2025}, Theorems 1.1--1.2, PDF p.~1. It is a local statement with center and scale fixed, not global uniqueness.
\end{itemize}
The formula determines the product geometry exactly. The figure is a
factor diagram with the full ambient dimension stated.
\end{example}

\chapter{Møller closed shrinkers of large even genus}\label{sec:catalogue-moller-genus}
M{\o}ller's construction is stated in \cite{CatMoller2014}, Theorem~1,
and summarized in the supplied \cite{CatDLN2018}, Section~3.3,
PDF pp.~10--11.

\begin{example}[Møller closed shrinkers of large even genus]\label{ex:catalogue-moller-genus}
There is an integer $g_0$ such that for every even $g\geq g_0$ there
exists a compact smooth embedded orientable surface
$\Sigma_g\subset\mathbb R^3$ of genus $g$ satisfying
$\vec H=-X^\perp/2$. Its flow is
$M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma_g$, $t<0$.
This is an existence construction by desingularization, not an explicit
formula and not an existence assertion for every positive genus.
\begin{itemize}
\item Each surface is complete and proper, has no ends, and is invariant
under a dihedral group of order $2g$.
\item Along the constructed family as $g\to\infty$, the surfaces
converge in Hausdorff distance to the union of a round shrinking sphere
and a rotational shrinking torus. Convergence is locally smooth away
from their two intersection circles (\cite{CatMoller2014}, pp.~1--2).
The limiting union is singular and is not one of the smooth members.
\item These surfaces are not mean convex and are entropy unstable:
apply \cite{CM12}, Theorems~0.17 and~0.12, to the compact
embedded non-spherical surfaces. Their homothetic flows have Type~I
extinction at the origin.
\item The cited construction supplies no exact coordinates or exact
entropy value and makes no uniqueness assertion for a specified genus.
\end{itemize}
The illustration follows Figures~1--2 of \cite{CatMoller2014}: a sphere
of radius $2$ intersects a non-circular rotational shrinking torus along
two circles. In cylindrical coordinates $(r,\theta,z)$, the displayed
numerical torus meets the sphere near $r=1.783$, $z=\pm0.906$,
consistent with Proposition~2.1. These are numerical coordinates, not
exact formulas. Rotation, a quarter cutaway, and a meridian view expose
both sheets and the circles where the desingularization takes place.
The removed sector is only a viewing aid. As in the paper's Figure~1,
this is the singular configuration before handle insertion, not a
computed smooth surface $\Sigma_g$.

The second illustration shows how the two intersection circles can be
replaced by handles, using gluing schematics of genera $8$, $16$, and $24$.
Their mesh topology is checked, but these models do not solve the
self-shrinker equation and are not certified approximations to
$\Sigma_g$. The chosen genera illustrate the topology; the cited theorem
does not give an explicit $g_0$ that would establish these particular
values as members of its constructed family.
\end{example}

\chapter{Kapouleas--Kleene--Møller shrinkers with one end}\label{sec:catalogue-kkm-one-end}
The original construction is \cite{CatKKM2018}, Theorem~1.1, arXiv
version~3, PDF p.~2. It is among the examples cited in the introduction
of \cite{CatSunWang2020}.

\begin{example}[Kapouleas--Kleene--Møller shrinkers with one end]\label{ex:catalogue-kkm-one-end}
There is an integer $g_0$ such that for every integer $g\geq g_0$ there
exists a smooth complete embedded orientable self-shrinker
$\Sigma_g\subset\mathbb R^3$ of genus $g$, with
\[
 \vec H=-\frac12X^\perp,\qquad
 M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma_g,\quad -\infty<t<0.
\]
The construction desingularizes the intersection circle of a plane
through the origin and $S^2(2)$. It is a gluing existence theorem;
no explicit parametrization of $\Sigma_g$ is provided.
\begin{itemize}
\item The surface has dimension two and codimension one, is noncompact,
has exactly one end, and separates $\mathbb R^3$ into two connected
components. Its topology is an orientable closed genus-$g$ surface with
one point removed. The compact core and graphical end give properness.
\item It is invariant under the dihedral symmetry group of order $4g+4$
specified in the construction.
\item Outside a ball the end is a graph over a plane, asymptotic to a
nonplanar regular cone whose link is a nonzero smooth vertical graph over
a great circle of $S^2$. The end has asymptotic height
$u(\rho,\theta)=\rho\sigma(\theta)+O(\rho^{-1})$;
the function $\sigma$ is determined by the construction, not an explicit
formula. See equation~(1.2), PDF p.~4.
\item As $g\to\infty$, the surfaces converge locally in Hausdorff distance
to $S^2(2)\cup P$. The source gives an $O(R/g)$ Hausdorff bound inside
$B_R(0)$; convergence is locally smooth away from the intersection circle.
The limiting union is singular, and is not one of the smooth shrinkers.
\item The conical geometry gives quadratic area growth and finite entropy.
The source supplies no exact entropy value or numerical entropy bound
for individual $g$. It records entropy instability on PDF p.~4.
No uniqueness or identification with another construction is asserted.
\end{itemize}
The picture resolves the joining circle using a Scherk-based construction
model. The local ingredient in the paper is the singly periodic minimal
surface
\[
 \sinh u\,\sinh v=\sin w,\qquad (u,v,w)\in\mathbb R^3,
\]
with period $2\pi$ in $w$; see equation~(5.1), PDF p.~9. Section~5 curls
$k=g+1$ periods around the equator and attaches the four wings to the
upper cap, lower cap, inner disk, and outer sheet. The genus relation is
in Remark~5.12, PDF p.~12. The illustration uses a simplified coordinate
map and an adjustable conical deformation, not the paper's matched
initial surface or the nonlinear correction that solves the shrinker
equation. Its cutaway exposes the inner disk and the joining belt.

\chapter{Drugan--Nguyen variational shrinking tori}\label{sec:catalogue-drugan-nguyen-torus}

\begin{example}[Drugan--Nguyen variational shrinking tori]\label{ex:catalogue-drugan-nguyen-torus}
For every integer $n\geq2$, the weighted half-plane $\{(r,x):r>0\}$
with metric
\[
 g_n=r^{2(n-1)}e^{-(r^2+x^2)/2}(dr^2+dx^2)
\]
contains a simple closed geodesic $\gamma_n=(r,x)$ whose weighted
length satisfies
\[
 L_{g_n}(\gamma_n)<2\int_0^\infty s^{n-1}e^{-s^2/4}\,ds.
\]
Revolving this curve gives a normalized self-shrinker
\[
 X(u,\omega)=(r(u)\omega,x(u)),\qquad
 (u,\omega)\in S^1\times S^{n-1}(1),
 \qquad \vec H=-X^\perp/2.
\]
The profile is obtained by a variational and modified curve-flow
argument, not by a closed formula. The smooth MCF is
$\sqrt{-t}\,X$, $t<0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item This is a compact embedded hypersurface of dimension $n$ in
$\mathbb R^{n+1}$, with topology $S^1\times S^{n-1}$ and rotational
$O(n)$ symmetry. It is complete, proper, boundaryless and has no ends.
For $n=2$ it is an embedded genus-one torus in $\mathbb R^3$.
\item The rotational Gaussian integral and the length bound give
\[
 \lambda(\mathcal T_n)=F_{0,1}(\mathcal T_n)
 =(4\pi)^{-n/2}|S^{n-1}(1)|L_{g_n}(\gamma_n)<2.
\]
Thus this family has a proved entropy upper bound in every stated
dimension. The theorem does not provide an exact entropy value.
\item In dimension two, this is the shrinking doughnut $\mathcal T$
used by \cite{CatChuSun2025}, Theorem~1.3 and Corollary~1.7,
to produce genus-one singularities and an embedded genus-one shrinker
of strictly lower entropy. That conclusion does not identify the
lower-entropy model with a particular previously constructed family.
\item The asserted bound is attached to this variationally constructed
torus. A numerical value for the Angenent shooting profile is a
different type of evidence and does not establish that the profiles
coincide.
\end{itemize}
The illustration shows the weighted half-plane construction and a
clearly labelled torus topology schematic. Reusing a numerical
Angenent mesh as an exact model of this construction would assume the
unproved identification.
\end{example}

\chapter{Ketover self-shrinking doubled planes}\label{sec:catalogue-ketover-doubled-plane}
The following is \cite{CatKetover2024}, Theorem~1.1, arXiv version~2,

\begin{example}[Ketover self-shrinking doubled planes]\label{ex:catalogue-ketover-doubled-plane}
For every integer $g\geq1$ there exists an embedded self-shrinker
$\Sigma_g\subset\mathbb R^3$ of genus $g$ such that
\[
 \lambda(\Sigma_g)=F_{0,1}(\Sigma_g)<2,
 \qquad \lim_{g\to\infty}\lambda(\Sigma_g)=2.
\]
The normalized smooth flow is $M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma_g$, $t<0$.
There is no explicit formula for $\Sigma_g$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The construction gives a smooth complete properly embedded
boundaryless surface of dimension two and codimension one. It is
invariant under the prismatic group $D_{g+1}\times\mathbb Z_2$,
including reflection across the horizontal plane $P=\{z=0\}$.
The group has order $4(g+1)$.
\item For all sufficiently large $g$, the surface is noncompact with
exactly two asymptotically conical ends: one in $z>0$ and one in
$z<0$, each graphical over the exterior of a disk in $P$. Their cone
links converge in $C^0$ to the equator as $g\to\infty$.
For these $g$, the topology is a closed orientable genus-$g$ surface
with two points removed.
\item For the small-genus members, this theorem does not establish
noncompactness or an exact number of ends. Numerical evidence for
two ends already at $g=1$ is stated separately in Remark~1.2.
\item As $g\to\infty$, $\Sigma_g\to2P$ as varifolds. From every
subsequence one can extract a further subsequence and find a radius
$r>0$ such that the surfaces are two normal graphs converging smoothly
on each compact subset of $P\setminus C_r$, where
$C_r=\{(x,y,0):x^2+y^2=r^2\}$. The radius is part of the subsequential
conclusion, not a prescribed geometric parameter.
\item For sufficiently large $g$, the level set flow starting from the
smooth surface $\Sigma_g$ fattens (Theorem~1.3, PDF p.~4). The
homothetic flow reaches its asymptotic cone at time zero; this statement
concerns weak continuation beyond that singular time.
\item Entropy minimization among genus-$g$ shrinkers is conjectural
(Remark~1.2). Neither uniqueness nor a particular value below two is
claimed.
\end{itemize}
The interactive picture is an original topology schematic informed by
Proposition~7.4 (PDF pp.~36--37) and Section~8. In the subsequential
large-genus regime of that proposition, $\Sigma_g\cap P\cap B_R(0)$

plane is a varifold limit rather than a smooth member of the family.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Angenent torus cylinders]\label{ex:catalogue-angenent-torus-cylinders}

 \Sigma_k=T^2\times\mathbb R^k\subset\mathbb R^{k+3}
\]
is a smooth embedded shrinker of dimension $k+2$ and codimension one. If $X_T(p)$ parametrizes the torus, the exact product and flow formulas are
\[
 X(p,z)=(X_T(p),z),\qquad
 \Sigma_k(t)=\sqrt{-t}\,T^2\times\mathbb R^k,\quad t<0.
\]
The torus factor itself is specified by its closed weighted geodesic or rotational ODE, not by a closed elementary formula. Since the Euclidean factor is tangent to the product,
\[
 \mathbf H_{\Sigma_k}=(\mathbf H_T,0)=-\tfrac12(X_T^\perp,0)
 =-\tfrac12X^\perp.
\]
This verifies the product construction directly.
\begin{itemize}
\item Each product is connected, complete, proper, noncompact, and without boundary, with topology $T^2\times\mathbb R^k$. It has two topological ends when $k=1$ and one when $k\geq2$. Its intrinsic dimension is at least three, so a surface genus is not assigned; genus one describes the torus factor only.
\item The product inherits the torus's axial rotational symmetry and is invariant under translations and orthogonal transformations in the Euclidean factor. The two ends of $\Sigma_1\subset\mathbb R^4$ have the fixed torus as cross-section.
\item Gaussian integration in the Euclidean factor gives, for $a\in\mathbb R^3$, $b\in\mathbb R^k$, and $\tau>0$,
\[
 F_{(a,b),\tau}(\Sigma_k)=F_{a,\tau}(T^2),\qquad
 \lambda(\Sigma_k)=\lambda(T^2)=F_{0,1}(\Sigma_k).
\]
Indeed, the extra factor is $(4\pi\tau)^{-k/2}\int_{\mathbb R^k}e^{-|z-b|^2/(4\tau)}\,dz=1$. Thus the numerical estimate $1.85122$ for the torus also estimates these products, with the same lack of a certified error enclosure; the product identity itself is exact.
\item As Euclidean products of a closed nonflat embedded shrinker, these examples fall within the realization theorem of \cite{CatCLSZ2026}, Theorem~1.2, PDF p.~2: each can occur with multiplicity one as a first-singularity tangent flow of a closed embedded hypersurface in the corresponding Euclidean space. The theorem does not assert that the initial closed hypersurface is this noncompact product.
\end{itemize}
The factor is constructed in \cite{CatAngenent1992}; the numerical entropy estimate is \cite{CatBerchenkoKogan2021}, Section~5, with its error-bound limitation in Section~6. The interactive picture shows only three $z$-slices of $\Sigma_1$ after projection from $\mathbb R^4$ to $\mathbb R^3$. It uses the catalogue's numerical torus mesh. Apparent intersections are projection artifacts; the three slices do not represent three components or the entire three-dimensional hypersurface.
\end{example}

\item No identification with the Buzano--Nguyen--Schulz min-max examples or the Kapouleas--Kleene--Moller and Nguyen gluing examples is proved. The shared topology and limiting configuration do not establish that they coincide or are distinct.
\end{itemize}
See \cite{CatHMW2026}, version~3: Section~5 (PDF pp.~16--17), Theorems~40--42 (pp.~26--31), Section~9 (p.~40), and Lemma~64 (pp.~45--46). The source uses $\leq\delta$ in Theorem~40; the strict bound above is its refined choice in Theorem~41. The picture shows the sphere--plane limit and prescribed symmetry, not a computed shrinker.
\end{example}

\chapter{Flow-generated three-ended shrinkers}\label{sec:catalogue-hmw-three-ended}
\begin{example}[Hoffman--Martin--White three-ended family]\label{ex:catalogue-hmw-three-ended}
For every sufficiently large integer $g$, Hoffman--Martin--White construct a smooth embedded shrinker $\Sigma_g\subset\mathbb R^3$ with genus $2g$ and exactly three ends. It has the normalization and smooth evolution
\[
 \vec H=-X^\perp/2,\qquad M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma_g,\quad t<0.
\]
The construction obtains the surface as a multiplicity-one tangent shrinker of a flow whose initial surface is compact and embedded in $S^2\times\mathbb R$ with its product metric. The shrinker lives in the Euclidean tangent space. There is no explicit parametrization.
\begin{itemize}
\item It is a connected, orientable, complete, proper noncompact surface of dimension two and codimension one. Its topology is a closed genus-$2g$ surface with three punctures; the three ends are annular and asymptotically conical.
\item It has the group $G_g$ and the union of lines $Q_g$ specified in Example~\ref{ex:catalogue-hmw-one-ended}. In particular, $\Sigma_g\cap\{z=0\}=Q_g$ and $\Sigma_g\setminus\{z=0\}$ has genus zero. These are the symmetries of the genus-$g$ Costa--Hoffman--Meeks minimal surface, but the shrinker has genus $2g$.
\item As $g\to\infty$, the surfaces converge to the horizontal plane with multiplicity three. After passage to a subsequence, convergence is smooth away from one circle in that plane. The circle is part of the limiting analysis, not an independently prescribed radius.
\item The entropy has a bound $\lambda(\Sigma_g)\leq\Lambda<\infty$ independent of $g$, and $F_{0,1}(\Sigma_g)=\lambda(\Sigma_g)$. The paper does not specify a numerical value of $\Lambda$ in this theorem. This entry therefore does not assert $\lambda<3$ or an exact entropy limit.
\item For each small $g$ the preceding construction produces a shrinker with genus $g$ or $2g$ and a uniform entropy bound; the assertions of genus $2g$ and three ends require $g$ sufficiently large. The paper's genus-two picture is explicitly conjectural and is not a numerical approximation. No uniqueness or identification with Shao--Zou's tripling construction is proved.
\end{itemize}
See \cite{CatHMW2026}, version~3: Theorem~43 (PDF p.~31), Theorem~55 and Conjecture~56 (p.~38), Theorem~59 (p.~39), Proposition~62 and Lemma~64 (pp.~45--46). The schematic depicts the three-sheet limit and topological data only; it does not assert existence for the small number of handles drawn.
\end{example}

\chapter{Ketover shrinkers with Platonic symmetry}\label{sec:catalogue-ketover-platonic}
\cite{CatKetover2016}, Theorem~0.1, constructs five families by
equivariant Gaussian min-max. The compact doubled solids used in the
sweepout describe the genus and symmetry of the construction; compactness
of the limiting shrinker is not proved.

\begin{example}[Ketover shrinkers with Platonic symmetry]\label{ex:catalogue-ketover-platonic}
There are smooth connected embedded self-shrinking surfaces
$\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3$ with the following pairs of genus and
rotational symmetry group:
\[
 (g,G)=(3,T_{12}),\quad(5,O_{24}),\quad(7,O_{24}),
 \quad(11,I_{60}),\quad(19,I_{60}).
\]
Here the subscripts give the orders of the tetrahedral, octahedral,
and icosahedral rotation groups. The normalization is
$\vec H=-X^\perp/2$, and $M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma$ for $t<0$.
No explicit parametrization is supplied.
\begin{itemize}
\item The sweepout joins two spherical sheets by one neck for each
face of a tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, or icosahedron,
respectively. The numbers of necks are $4,6,8,12,20$; the first neck
connects the components, so the genus is one less than this number.
This is a construction description, not an exact metric model.
\item The min-max limit is multiplicity one. It is complete and proper,
has no boundary, and retains the stated genus and symmetry. The
construction controls the genus but does not determine compactness,
the number of ends, or end asymptotics. In particular, a compact
picture suggested by numerical work must not be used to assign zero
ends to these proved examples.
\item The proof gives a useful strict Gaussian-area bound:
\[
 \frac4e < F_{0,1}(\Sigma)=\lambda(\Sigma)<\frac8e.
\]
For the doubled cube, the first equivariant width is $4/e$, the
second is strictly below twice the first by Lemma~2.4, and the equality
of the two widths is excluded in Remark~2.7. Remark~2.5 gives the same
construction for the other four symmetry types. These are bounds,
not computed entropy values.
\item No uniqueness or stability classification is asserted. The
two-parameter equivariant construction alone does not give an index
bound for unrestricted variations. Later constructions with similar
symmetries need not be different surfaces, and no identification is
assumed here.
\end{itemize}
Sources: \cite{CatKetover2016}, Theorem~0.1 and the compactness
discussion, PDF pp.~2--3; Theorems~1.1 and~1.3, p.~5;
Lemma~2.4 and multiplicity-one argument, p.~12;
Remark~2.5 and Remark~2.7, pp.~14--15.
The illustration shows the neck counts and symmetry of the sweepout,
not a numerical solution or a proof of compactness of the limit.
\end{example}

\chapter{Sun--Wang--Zhou shrinkers of arbitrarily large entropy}\label{sec:catalogue-swz-large-entropy}
The Gaussian-width construction of \cite{CatSunWangZhou2024}
produces shrinkers whose entropy is unbounded, in contrast with the
uniform upper bounds for several gluing and low-parameter min-max
families.

\begin{example}[Sun--Wang--Zhou Gaussian-width shrinkers]\label{ex:catalogue-swz-large-entropy}
For every $2\leq n\leq6$ there is a sequence of smooth, connected,
embedded self-shrinking hypersurfaces $\Sigma_k^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}$.
They are normalized by $\vec H=-X^\perp/2$, with associated smooth
flows $M_t=\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma_k$ for $t<0$. The construction is
variational and does not give explicit parametrizations.
\begin{itemize}
\item Each shrinker is complete, proper, boundaryless, and has finite
entropy. The construction is a multiplicity-one limit of free-boundary
min-max hypersurfaces in exhausting balls with metrics approaching the
Gaussian metric. The final shrinker has no free boundary.
\item Write $G_n=(4\pi)^{-1}e^{-|x|^2/(2n)}g_{\mathrm{Euc}}$ and
let $\omega_k(\mathbb R^{n+1},G_n)$ be its Gaussian $k$-width,
defined by exhaustion. Then
\[
 F_{0,1}(\Sigma_k)=\lambda(\Sigma_k)
 =\omega_k(\mathbb R^{n+1},G_n),\qquad
 \lambda(\Sigma_k)\asymp k^{1/(n+1)}.
\]
The growth statement means comparison up to positive constants
depending on $n$, and in particular $\lambda(\Sigma_k)\to\infty$.
It is not a closed formula for an individual width or a plotted list
of computed values. The unbounded entropies ensure infinitely many
different examples, without requiring consecutive widths to be
strictly different.
\item The Gaussian Morse index for compactly supported normal
variations satisfies $\mathrm{index}(\Sigma_k)\leq k$.
This is an upper bound, not an equality or an entropy-stability claim.
\item The theorem does not specify a genus, a number of ends, end
asymptotics, compactness, or a symmetry group for each member.
In dimension two the genus is therefore left unrecorded in the
property browser, rather than inferred from the min-max parameter.
\item In every dimension $n\geq2$ and for every $\Lambda>0$, there
is a smooth embedded shrinker with
$\Lambda<\lambda(\Sigma)<\infty$ and finite Gaussian index.
For $n\geq7$ this follows by taking a lower-dimensional example
and a Euclidean product. For example,
$\Sigma^6\times\mathbb R^{n-6}\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}$ has the
same entropy as $\Sigma^6$, and Theorem~5.3 preserves finiteness
of index under each line product. These higher-dimensional product
examples are noncompact; this extension does not assert the
$n$-dimensional Gaussian-width growth law above beyond $n=6$.
\end{itemize}
Sources: \cite{CatSunWangZhou2024}, Theorem~1.6 and Corollary~1.7,
arXiv version~1, PDF p.~4; Definition~5.1 and the proof of
Theorem~1.6, pp.~39--42; Definition~5.2, p.~40;
Theorem~5.3 and its proof, pp.~42--44. The illustration is a diagram of the
width-growth conclusion, not numerical entropy data or a surface model.
\end{example}

\chapter{Chu--Sun genus-one shrinkers below the shrinking doughnut}\label{sec:catalogue-chu-sun-genus-one}
The flow construction in \cite{CatChuSun2025} gives an embedded
genus-one shrinker with strictly less entropy than the
Drugan--Nguyen shrinking doughnut. It does not identify the
resulting surface with any particular previously constructed example.

\begin{example}[Chu--Sun lower-entropy genus-one shrinkers]\label{ex:catalogue-chu-sun-genus-one}
Let $\mathcal T\subset\mathbb R^3$ be the rotationally symmetric
compact genus-one shrinker constructed by Drugan--Nguyen
(Example~\ref{ex:catalogue-drugan-nguyen-torus}). There is a smooth
embedded genus-one shrinker $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3$ satisfying
\[
 F_{0,1}(\Sigma)=\lambda(\Sigma)
 <\lambda(\mathcal T)<2.
\]
It has dimension two and codimension one, is normalized by
$\vec H=-X^\perp/2$, and yields the smooth self-similar flow
$\sqrt{-t}\,\Sigma$ for $t<0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The surface is a multiplicity-one tangent-flow model at a
genus-one singularity of an ancient level-set flow which is
asymptotic, after rescaling as $t\to-\infty$, to $\mathcal T$.
The ancient flow is allowed to pass through cylindrical and spherical
singularities before the genus-one singularity. Thus the theorem does
not assert that this is the first singularity of a smooth ancient flow.
\item The resulting shrinker is complete, proper, and boundaryless.
Its compactness, end count, end asymptotics, and symmetry are not
determined by this existence statement. No explicit parametrization,
exact entropy value, or numerical shape is supplied.
\item Strictly smaller entropy rules out congruence with the chosen
shrinking doughnut $\mathcal T$. It does not prove that $\Sigma$
differs from the Buzano--Nguyen--Schulz genus-one example.
Remark~1.5 discusses that example and its numerical evidence,
without proving the identification. It also does not identify
$\mathcal T$ with Angenent's shooting torus.
\item For a shrinker minimizing entropy among all embedded genus-one
shrinkers, Theorem~1.4 gives a further alternative: it is noncompact
or has Gaussian Morse index exactly $5$. This statement applies to
an entropy minimizer, not to every shrinker obtained by the
interpolation construction. No uniqueness is asserted.
\end{itemize}
Sources: \cite{CatChuSun2025}, Theorems~1.4 and~1.6,
Remark~1.5 and Corollary~1.7, arXiv version~3, PDF pp.~3--4;
proofs in Sections~5.2--5.5, pp.~33--34.
The diagram records the flow construction and strict entropy drop;
the final genus-one shrinker is left abstract because its compactness
and ends are not specified.
\end{example}
