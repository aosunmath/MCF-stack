§ 5.28 Bi-rotational stationary minimal hypersurfaces209060F5D0F3

(Stationary profiles in the fattening construction) Let $p,q\geq 2$, $n=p+q$, and write $(X,Y)\in \mathbb R^ p\times \mathbb R^ q$, $x=|X|$, $y=|Y|$. Angenent–Ilmanen–Velázquez analyze complete minimal hypersurfaces invariant under $O(p)\times O(q)$ in [AIV2002], Section 2, pp. 4–7. For each $a>0$, the axis-regular profile is the increasing solution

\[ \frac{U''}{1+(U')^2}+\frac{p-1}{x}U'-\frac{q-1}{U}=0, \qquad U(0)=a,\quad U'(0)=0,\quad 0\leq x<\infty . \]

Its smooth extension at the axis has $U''(0)=(q-1)/(pa)$. The hypersurface

\[ \Sigma _ a=\{ (X,U(|X|)\omega ):X\in \mathbb R^ p,\ \omega \in S^{q-1}\} \]

is an embedded noncompact hypersurface of dimension $n-1$ and codimension one. It is proper and complete, has topology $\mathbb R^ p\times S^{q-1}$ and one end, and gives the eternal stationary flow $M_ t=\Sigma _ a$, $t\in \mathbb R$. These global properties follow from the positive global radial graph and its conical end. Interchanging the two blocks gives the other cap, with topology $S^{p-1}\times \mathbb R^ q$. Scaling is explicit, $U_ a(x)=aU_1(x/a)$, but $U_1$ is an ODE solution, not a closed formula. The nonflat hypersurfaces are not convex; their principal curvatures have cancelling signs because their mean curvature vanishes.

Both caps are asymptotic to the minimal cone

\[ C_{p,q}=\{ (X,Y):|Y|=A_*|X|\} ,\qquad A_*=\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}. \]

The cone is smooth away from its vertex; it is not itself one of the smooth caps. For $4\leq n\leq 7$ the profiles cross the cone infinitely often. The characteristic exponents in logarithmic radius are

\[ \lambda _\pm =\frac{-(n-1)\pm \sqrt{(n-5)^2-8}}2, \]

so the approach oscillates in those dimensions. At $n=8$ the exponents are real. Theorem 4, p. 7, records the minimizing cones for $(p,q)=(3,5),(4,4),(5,3)$ and the one-sided minimizing property of the Simoes cone $C_{2,6}$. In the latter case the cone minimizes on $|Y|\leq \sqrt5|X|$ but not on the other side. Appendix A, pp. 30–32, supplies a barrier computation. This is a statement about the cone; it is not a stability classification of every smooth cap.

The asymptotically conical geometry gives finite Gaussian area and entropy for each cap, but no value is recorded here, and the two quantities are not identified. The figures integrate the displayed minimal ODE and compare both caps with their cone, using orbit-space radii rather than a three-dimensional rendering of an $n$-dimensional ambient space. Cone crossings are intersections of different hypersurfaces, not self-intersections of a cap.
