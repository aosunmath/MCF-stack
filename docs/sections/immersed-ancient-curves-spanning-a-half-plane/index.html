§ 5.8 Immersed ancient curves spanning a half-planeDCA2FD176411

DCA2FD176411
4B3B50761BB9

Ma constructs a compact immersed ancient curve-shortening flow in $\mathbb R^2$ whose time-slice convex hulls together fill a half-plane. Compactness refers to each individual immersed closed curve, not to a uniform spatial bound over ancient time. The construction joins increasingly complicated grim-reaper configurations and takes an ancient limit; it is not a homothetic curve or an explicit parametrization. The curves can have crossings at early times and become embedded later, then shrink to a round point. Hence embeddedness is time dependent. Smooth compact slices are complete and proper as immersions, have no ends, and have finite Gaussian area and entropy at each time; no uniform ancient-time bound is claimed. The source's sequence of disappearing fingers is a guide to the construction, not a formula for singular events of the final ancient limit. Our diagram separates early immersed stages from the later embedded stage and labels them as qualitative stages rather than numerically computed MCF slices.

Sources: [Ma2021].
