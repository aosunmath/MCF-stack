§ 5.11 Haslhofer–Hershkovits ancient ovals12F33FD70B5C

12F33FD70B5C
56D296501918

For $n\geq 2$ and every $1\leq j\leq n-1$, Haslhofer–Hershkovits construct an ancient hypersurface flow $M_ t\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $-\infty <t<0$, with compact strictly convex embedded slices. Each slice is diffeomorphic to $S^ n$, hence complete and proper with no ends. The construction has $O(j+1)\times O(n-j)$ symmetry and is uniformly Andrews noncollapsed. It is not self-similar and becomes round at extinction.

Under backward parabolic blow-down,

\[ \lambda ^{-1}M_{\lambda ^2t}\longrightarrow S^ j(\sqrt{2j|t|})\times \mathbb R^{n-j}\quad (\lambda \to \infty ). \]

The tip rescalings selected in Claim 3.5 of [HH13] approach $\mathrm{Bowl}^{j+1}\times \mathbb R^{n-j-1}$, with the bowl normalized to unit tip curvature. Rescaled diameter is unbounded backward in time and curvature decay is type II. No closed formula or uniqueness follows from this construction. Gaussian area and entropy are finite for each compact slice; no fixed slice value is evaluated here.

Source: [HH13], Theorem 1.1 and Section 3. Huisken–Sinestrari's Theorem 3.2 in [HS14] shows why the hypothesis $h_{ij}\geq \varepsilon H g_{ij}$ with one $\varepsilon >0$ for all ancient times cannot hold for these nonspherical ovals: a closed convex ancient flow with that uniform pinching is a shrinking sphere.

The illustration specializes to $n=2$, $j=1$. It numerically evolves the boundary of the Minkowski sum of a unit ball and an ellipsoid with semiaxes $(8,1/2,1/2)$, using rotational mean curvature flow. This is a finite smooth convex seed inspired by the long capped cylinders in Section 3, not a closed formula for the ancient oval. Extinction is shifted to $t=0$, and parabolic scaling makes the axial-to-equatorial radius ratio equal to $2$ at $t=-1$. The slider samples $-1000\leq t\leq -0.001$.

Original size shows contraction at a fixed spatial scale. The default view shows $(-t)^{-1/2}M_ t$: the backward central limit is the cylinder of radius $\sqrt2$, while the extinction limit is the sphere of radius $2$. The right-tip view instead translates the tip to the origin and multiplies lengths by its mean curvature; the dashed comparison is a numerically integrated unit-speed bowl. These are different rescalings. Mesh refinement, time-tolerance refinement, and a shorter seed give consistent sampled profiles, but they do not certify an error to the ancient solution.
