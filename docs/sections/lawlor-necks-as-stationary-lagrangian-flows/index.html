§ 5.31 Lawlor necks as stationary Lagrangian flows380EC54D3A30

380EC54D3A30 Lawlor necks are the stationary endpoint of the Lagrangian neck construction.
263868E061E7

(Stationary special Lagrangian necks) For $n\geq 2$, $a_ j>0$, and phases $\psi _ j\in \mathbb R$, define

\[ P_0(y)=\frac{\prod _{j=1}^ n(1+a_ jy^2)-1}{y^2},\quad P_0(0)=\sum _ ja_ j,\quad \phi _ j(y)=\psi _ j+\int _0^ y\frac{dt}{(a_ j^{-1}+t^2)\sqrt{P_0(t)}}. \]

The map

\[ X(x,y)=\left(x_ j\sqrt{a_ j^{-1}+y^2}e^{i\phi _ j(y)}\right)_{j=1}^ n, \qquad (x,y)\in \mathbb S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R, \]

parametrizes a complete proper embedded special Lagrangian of dimension and codimension $n$. Its constant flow $L_ t=X(\mathbb S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R)$ is eternal, $t\in \mathbb R$, since $\vec H=0$. This is the $\alpha =0$ case of [JoyceLeeTsui2010], Theorems C–D, credited there to Lawlor.

It has two planar ends and topology $\mathbb S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R$; in $\mathbb C^2$ it is an annulus. The positive characteristic angles sum to $\pi $. They determine a neck up to scale, and equal $a_ j$ give the rotational subfamily after a unitary change of coordinates. Calibration makes the neck area-minimizing for compactly supported competitors with the same boundary, hence stable for compactly supported variations.

For $n\geq 3$, [ImagiJoyceOliveira2016], Theorem 1.1, classifies closed-as-subsets, embedded, exact, asymptotically conical special Lagrangians with two transverse planar asymptotes and rate $\rho <0$, up to the specified special unitary rotation. The source's weaker rate $\rho <2$ additionally permits translation. These are conditional uniqueness statements. The drawing projects the exact $n=2$, $a_1=a_2=1$ map, whose common complex factor is $\sqrt{1+y^2/2}+iy/\sqrt2$.
