§ 5.20 Ancient flows from unstable minimal hypersurfaces (Mramor–Payne, 2019; Choi–Huang–Lee, 2024)C21B5E7431CB

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Melko — 2010 (publication year). Background only: Parametrization of the stationary Costa surface used for the illustration, not the ancient flow constructed from it. Background: The Costa-surface illustration supplies a stationary reference shape, not the ancient-flow construction. No title credit is assigned. Passage: Catalogue illustration credit.
  2. Mramor–Payne — 2019-04-17 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Constructs ancient flows converging from either side to unstable minimal hypersurfaces under the stated geometry assumptions. Passage: Theorem 1.1.
  3. Choi–Huang–Lee — 2024-05-02 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Constructs index-parameter families with exponential space-time convergence and a mean-convex subfamily; it is not merely citing the earlier construction. Passage: Theorems 1.1–1.2; Theorems 4.4, 5.1 and 5.3.

Let $M^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $n\geq 2$, be two-sided, properly embedded and minimal, unstable on a bounded domain, with bounded curvature and a uniform embedded tubular neighborhood. Assume additionally that it is asymptotically flat, periodic with compact fundamental domain, or periodic and asymptotically flat within its fundamental domain. Mramor–Payne produce two embedded ancient flows approaching $M$ smoothly and uniformly from opposite sides as $t\to -\infty $. Each has a sign of mean curvature, is nonconvex, and is not a soliton.

For a concrete three-dimensional example, use the classical Costa minimal surface. For sufficiently negative times the normal-graph construction retains its genus one and three ends. These slices are complete and proper; these conclusions follow from the bounded normal graph over the complete proper initial model. We record that early ancient interval $(-\infty ,T)$, without asserting an eternal continuation for the Costa case. The theorem also applies to other Costa–Hoffman–Meeks surfaces and periodic minimal surfaces, but their differing topology is not collapsed into this case.

Source: [MP19], Theorem 1.1 and its following examples, pp. 3–4, and the normal-graph construction in Section 3. There is no closed time-dependent formula, uniqueness, or quantitative entropy value supplied. The distinct rotational eternal continuation from a catenoid is recorded separately in 5.21. The picture uses a numerical sampling of the classical Costa parametrization in [Melko2010], equation (17), with square lattice $\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z$ and no additional spatial dilation. Its three ends are truncated for display.

To illustrate different early times, the viewer uses the linearization of mean curvature flow over the minimal surface. With $H=\operatorname {div}\nu $ and mean curvature vector $-H\nu $, a normal height satisfies

\[ \partial _ t u=(\Delta _ M+|A|^2)u \]

to first order. On a truncated Costa domain, let $\phi _ D>0$ be the numerically computed first Dirichlet mode, normalized by $\max \phi _ D=1$, with $(\Delta _ M+|A|^2)\phi _ D=\lambda _ D\phi _ D$ and $\lambda _ D>0$. The displayed model is

\[ X_{\mathrm{model}}^{\pm }(p,\tau ) =X_ M(p)\pm 0.11e^\tau \phi _ D(p)\nu _ M(p), \qquad \tau =\lambda _ D t\in [-5,0]. \]

The numerical value $\lambda _ D\approx 1.511$ depends on this scale, truncation and discretization; it is not a claimed exact eigenvalue of the complete Costa surface. The height decays backward and increases forward on either normal side. These are linearized model times, not computed nonlinear ancient slices or an asymptotic expansion proved for the flows in Theorem 1.1. The default display magnifies the normal displacement by three; the model-scale option removes that magnification. The endpoint $\tau =0$ is only a chosen small model amplitude, not a singular time. The two signed models illustrate the two sides; no reflection identity between the nonlinear flows is asserted.

Choi–Huang–Lee strengthen the finite-total-curvature construction: if $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $n\ge 2$, is complete, two-sided, unstable, embedded and minimal with finite total curvature and Morse index $I$, there is an $I$-parameter family of ancient flows backward approaching $\Sigma $ exponentially in space-time. For sufficiently negative time they are entire normal graphs over $\Sigma $, have finite total curvature and finite mass drop from $\Sigma $. A one-parameter branch is mean convex. These estimates and parameter count require the stated finite-total-curvature hypotheses; they are not assertions for every minimal hypersurface. See [ChoiHuangLee2025].
