§ 2.21 One-ended high-genus shrinkers from sphere–plane gluing (X. H. Nguyen, 2011; Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller, 2011)2285B0C987A6

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. X. H. Nguyen — 2011-06-26 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Independently constructs the same class of embedded sphere–plane desingularizations, with genus m−1 and one conical end for sufficiently large m. Multiply space by √2 to match the catalogue normalization. The acknowledgments discuss corrections to the original fixed-point setup and acknowledge the similar KKM result; first-posting order is bibliographic, not a claim about priority of a completed proof. No identification of the two constructed families is asserted. Passage: arXiv:1106.5272v3, Theorem 1 and topology paragraph (PDF p. 2); acknowledgments (p. 5); Section 4.3 (p. 13); Definition 21 and Lemma 22 (pp. 20–21); Section 7 (pp. 24–25). Reviewed text.
  2. Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller — 2011-06-27 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Constructs noncompact embedded high-genus shrinkers with one conical end by sphere–plane desingularization. The introduction acknowledges Nguyen’s similar result. The detailed symmetry and quantitative estimates on this page are those of the KKM construction. Passage: Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2); final paragraph of the introduction (p. 5).
  3. Sun–Zhichao Wang — 2018-11-13 (first arXiv submission). Analysis: Explicitly credits Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller and Nguyen for the constructions; proves fixed-genus compactness and consequences for ends and entropy. Passage: Introduction, Theorem 1.1.
2285B0C987A6

This class has independent constructions by Xuan Hien Nguyen [NguyenShrinker2014], Theorem 1, and by Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller [KKM2018], Theorem 1.1. Both desingularize the intersection of a sphere and a plane using a singly periodic Scherk surface and solve the nonlinear shrinker equation. Nguyen proves existence in Theorem 1 (arXiv version 3, PDF p. 2), with the nonlinear fixed-point argument in Section 7, pp. 24–25. Her surfaces have genus $m-1$ for every sufficiently large integer $m$ and one asymptotically conical end. Her convention is $\mathbf H=-X^\perp $; multiplying space by $\sqrt2$ gives the normalization used here. The details of that construction are recorded in Example 2.22.

Both papers acknowledge the other construction: see Nguyen's acknowledgments on PDF p. 5 and the end of the KKM introduction on PDF p. 5. Neither cited passage identifies the resulting surfaces as congruent. The precise symmetry, asymptotic estimates, and illustration below describe the KKM construction. Both constructions are also cited in the introduction of [SunWang2020].

A515DF7C7C60

There is an integer $g_0$ such that for every integer $g\geq g_0$ there exists a smooth complete embedded orientable self-shrinker $\Sigma _ g\subset \mathbb R^3$ of genus $g$, with

\[ \vec H=-\frac12X^\perp ,\qquad M_ t=\sqrt{-t}\, \Sigma _ g,\quad -\infty <t<0. \]

The construction desingularizes the intersection circle of a plane through the origin and $S^2(2)$. It is a gluing existence theorem; no explicit parametrization of $\Sigma _ g$ is provided.

  • The surface has dimension two and codimension one, is noncompact, has exactly one end, and separates $\mathbb R^3$ into two connected components. Its topology is an orientable closed genus-$g$ surface with one point removed. The compact core and graphical end give properness.

  • It is invariant under the dihedral symmetry group of order $4g+4$ specified in the construction.

  • Outside a ball the end is a graph over a plane, asymptotic to a nonplanar regular cone whose link is a nonzero smooth vertical graph over a great circle of $S^2$. The end has asymptotic height $u(\rho ,\theta )=\rho \sigma (\theta )+O(\rho ^{-1})$; the function $\sigma $ is determined by the construction, not an explicit formula. See equation (1.2), PDF p. 4.

  • As $g\to \infty $, the surfaces converge locally in Hausdorff distance to $S^2(2)\cup P$. The source gives an $O(R/g)$ Hausdorff bound inside $B_ R(0)$; convergence is locally smooth away from the intersection circle. The limiting union is singular, and is not one of the smooth shrinkers.

  • The conical geometry gives quadratic area growth and finite entropy. The source supplies no exact entropy value or numerical entropy bound for individual $g$. It records entropy instability on PDF p. 4. No uniqueness or identification with another construction is asserted.

The picture resolves the joining circle using a Scherk-based construction model. The local ingredient in the paper is the singly periodic minimal surface

\[ \sinh u\, \sinh v=\sin w,\qquad (u,v,w)\in \mathbb R^3, \]

with period $2\pi $ in $w$; see equation (5.1), PDF p. 9. Section 5 curls $k=g+1$ periods around the equator and attaches the four wings to the upper cap, lower cap, inner disk, and outer sheet. The genus relation is in Remark 5.12, PDF p. 12. The illustration uses a simplified coordinate map and an adjustable conical deformation, not the paper's matched initial surface or the nonlinear correction that solves the shrinker equation. Its cutaway exposes the inner disk and the joining belt. The displayed values $k=6,9,12$ illustrate the topology and do not assert that these genera lie in the theorem's existence range. The outer edge is a display truncation; the waviness is not the computed function $\sigma $. Figure 1 of the paper, PDF p. 3, is itself captioned as a conjectural genus-eight shrinker.

Ambient spaces: ℝ³
Ambient space, topology, embeddedness and entropy

Nguyen and Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller independently construct this class by sphere–plane desingularization. The detailed estimates and Scherk-based illustration on this page concern KKM; Nguyen’s construction has a linked detail page. No geometric identification or noncongruence is asserted. Existence is proved for every sufficiently large genus, with no explicit threshold. The Scherk-based interactive picture is a construction schematic; its period counts and cone profiles are not computed KKM solutions or entropy data.
