§ 4.13 Bi-rotational expanding hypersurfaces (Angenent–Ilmanen–Velázquez, 2002; Neto, 2026)6BCE5569C7F9

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Angenent–Ilmanen–Velázquez — 2002 (preprint year). Construction: Earlier construction of the axis-starting subfamily; uniqueness fixes axis radius, not the asymptotic cone. Passage: Theorem 18; Sections 4–5.1, pp. 17–21. Reviewed text.
  2. Neto — 2026-10-19 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Retains the previously recorded general bi-rotational construction. Passage: Existing source review on this reading page.

Fix integers $p,q\ge 1$. In the 2026 preprint of Silva Neto, an $O(p+1)\times O(q+1)$-invariant expander in $\mathbb R^{p+q+2}$ is generated by

\[ F(s,u,v)=(x(s)u,y(s)v),\quad (u,v)\in S^ p\times S^ q, \]
\[ x'=\cos \sigma ,\quad y'=\sin \sigma ,\quad \sigma '=-\left(\frac px+\frac x2\right)\sin \sigma + \left(\frac qy+\frac y2\right)\cos \sigma . \]

The maximal nonconical profiles give complete proper noncompact hypersurfaces. Axis-starting profiles close smoothly with topology $\mathbb R^{p+1}\times S^ q$ or $\mathbb R^{q+1}\times S^ p$ and are embedded. The two-ended family has topology $\mathbb R\times S^ p\times S^ q$; each point of the open quadrant prescribes its closest point to the origin, up to orientation. Its profile has at most one loop, so embeddedness must be checked for the chosen member. Every end is asymptotically conical, with Euclidean volume growth. The Clifford minimal cone is excluded. The first ambient dimension is four, not three. The time evolution is $\sqrt t\, F$, $t>0$. The numerical meridian and projected torus orbits illustrate a selected $(p,q)=(1,1)$ solution of the stated ODE. The global assertions are attributed to the preprint, not independently verified here.

The Angenent–Ilmanen–Velázquez axis-starting family. The earlier construction in [AIV2002], Theorem 18 and Section 5.1, pp. 17–21, uses block dimensions $P,Q\geq 2$, so $P=p+1$, $Q=q+1$ in the notation above. For each $a>0$ there is a unique smooth axis-regular solution on $0\leq x<\infty $ of

\[ \frac{U''}{1+(U')^2}+\left(\frac{P-1}{x}+\frac x2\right)U' -\frac U2-\frac{Q-1}{U}=0,\qquad U(0)=a,\quad U'(0)=0. \]

It is strictly increasing for $x>0$, with $U''(0)=((Q-1)/a+a/2)/P$, and $U(x;a)/x\to B_ a>0$. The embedding is $(X,\sqrt t\, U(|X|/\sqrt t;a)\omega )$ for $X\in \mathbb R^ P$, $\omega \in S^{Q-1}$, $t>0$. Its slices are complete, proper, noncompact and one-ended, with topology $\mathbb R^ P\times S^{Q-1}$; exchanging blocks gives the other cap. The radial profile is an ODE construction, not an elementary formula. It is not asserted to be convex, and no stability classification or quantitative entropy value is supplied.

The limiting slopes satisfy $B_ a\to A_*:=\sqrt{(Q-1)/(P-1)}$ as $a\downarrow 0$. For $4\leq P+Q\leq 7$, Section 5.1 describes oscillation of $B_ a$ about $A_*$ and arbitrarily many expanders at cone slopes sufficiently close to $A_*$. Uniqueness for prescribed axis radius $a$ therefore does not imply uniqueness for a prescribed cone. For $(P,Q)=(2,6)$, Theorem 18(E) and the Simoes-cone analysis produce cone-crossing profiles; the fattening criterion is discussed in Example 5.36. These are special cases of the axis-starting class already recorded on this page, not additional reading entries. The supplementary experiment matches several numerical expanders to the cone of a numerical shrinker in $\mathbb R^4$. It reports cutoff and tolerance comparisons and does not compute the entire fattened level set.

Sources: [Neto2026, AIV2002].
