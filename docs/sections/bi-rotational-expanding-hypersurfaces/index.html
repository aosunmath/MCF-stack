§ 4.16 Bi-rotational expanding hypersurfaces6BCE5569C7F9

Paper credits and chronology

Paper credits are chronological and include every author of the cited papers. They identify sources, not exclusive discovery credit. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Neto — 2026-10-19 (first arXiv submission).
6BCE5569C7F9
060795635061

Fix integers $p,q\ge 1$. In the 2026 preprint of Silva Neto, an $O(p+1)\times O(q+1)$-invariant expander in $\mathbb R^{p+q+2}$ is generated by

\[ F(s,u,v)=(x(s)u,y(s)v),\quad (u,v)\in S^ p\times S^ q, \]
\[ x'=\cos \sigma ,\quad y'=\sin \sigma ,\quad \sigma '=-\left(\frac px+\frac x2\right)\sin \sigma + \left(\frac qy+\frac y2\right)\cos \sigma . \]

The maximal nonconical profiles give complete proper noncompact hypersurfaces. Axis-starting profiles close smoothly with topology $\mathbb R^{p+1}\times S^ q$ or $\mathbb R^{q+1}\times S^ p$ and are embedded. The two-ended family has topology $\mathbb R\times S^ p\times S^ q$; each point of the open quadrant prescribes its closest point to the origin, up to orientation. Its profile has at most one loop, so embeddedness must be checked for the chosen member. Every end is asymptotically conical, with Euclidean volume growth. The Clifford minimal cone is excluded. The first ambient dimension is four, not three. The time evolution is $\sqrt t\, F$, $t>0$. The numerical meridian and projected torus orbits illustrate a selected $(p,q)=(1,1)$ solution of the stated ODE. The global assertions are attributed to the preprint, not independently verified here.

Sources: [Neto2026].
