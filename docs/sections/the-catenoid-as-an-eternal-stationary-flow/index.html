5.1 The catenoid as an eternal stationary flowC9F0102B7272

C9F0102B7272

Example 5.1.1 (Stationary catenoid flow).AC1C0D984A58 For $a>0$ the explicit immersion

\[ X(\theta ,s,t)=a(\cosh s\cos \theta ,\cosh s\sin \theta ,s), \quad \theta \in \mathbb R/2\pi \mathbb Z,\quad s\in \mathbb R,\quad t\in \mathbb R, \]

is independent of time and has $\vec H=0$. It is therefore an eternal stationary mean curvature flow in $\mathbb R^3$.

Every time slice is a complete properly embedded noncompact rotational annulus, with two ends and a waist circle of radius $a$. Its metric and curvature are

\[ g=a^2\cosh ^2s(d\theta ^2+ds^2),\qquad K=-\frac1{a^2\cosh ^4s},\qquad \int K\, dA=-4\pi . \]

The metric follows by differentiation, and the conformal curvature formula gives $K$ and its integral. The complete catenoid is unstable for compactly supported area variations: for a test function $f(s)$ independent of $\theta $, its second variation is

\[ Q(f,f)=2\pi \int _{\mathbb R}\bigl ((f')^2-2\operatorname {sech}^2s\, f^2\bigr )\, ds. \]

Taking $f$ equal to one on $[-R,R]$, zero outside $[-2R,2R]$, and $|f'|\leq C/R$ makes this negative for large $R$. Each open half is a minimal graph over $r>a$, with $z=\pm a\operatorname {arcosh}(r/a)$, so it is stable for compactly supported variations in that half. These are minimal-area stability assertions, not a classification of dynamical stability under MCF. The supplied source also records the catenoid in [CatMan11], Remark 4.4.7, printed p. 105, supplied PDF p. 116. The parametrization is exact and supports interactive rotation, scale selection, and a truncated height range with the omitted infinite ends stated.
