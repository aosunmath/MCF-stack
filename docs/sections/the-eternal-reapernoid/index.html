§ 5.10 The eternal reapernoid94DC745FE4C9

94DC745FE4C9
8EACFCA7AE0F

For every $n\geq 2$, Mramor–Payne construct an eternal embedded hypersurface flow $M_ t\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $t\in \mathbb R$, on the outside of a rotational minimal catenoid. It is $O(n)\times O(1)$ invariant, mean convex with the chosen outward normal, nonconvex, and has uniformly bounded curvature. Backward in time it converges smoothly and uniformly to that catenoid; forward in time its distance from the rotation axis tends to infinity. It is not a translator or another soliton.

The rotational graphical construction gives complete proper noncompact slices with topology $S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R$ and two ends. For $n=2$ these are annuli in $\mathbb R^3$. In that dimension the pointed forward profile limit is a line and curvature tends to zero. For $n\geq 3$, recentered meridians converge instead to a grim reaper with the same width as the higher-dimensional catenoid. These are pointed profile limits, not a claim that the unshifted surfaces converge globally to a fixed grim cylinder.

Source: [MP19], Theorem 1.3, pp. 4–5, and Section 4. The scale of the limiting catenoid is free; no explicit time-dependent profile, full uniqueness statement, or entropy value is recorded. The picture is a schematic rotational annulus, not a scaled catenoid evolving by mean curvature.
