§ 5.19 Ancient dense torus curves with irrational frequenciesE22AF7044670

E22AF7044670
3E693532AA77

Choose $m\geq 2$, amplitudes $a_ j>0$, and positive frequencies $\omega _ j$ which are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$. For $\theta ,\tau \in \mathbb R$, set

\[ C(\theta ,\tau )=\bigl (a_ j e^{-\omega _ j^2\tau }\cos (\omega _ j\theta ), a_ j e^{-\omega _ j^2\tau }\sin (\omega _ j\theta )\bigr )_{j=1}^ m, \qquad t(\tau )=-\frac12\sum _{j=1}^ m a_ j^2e^{-2\omega _ j^2\tau }. \]

The strictly increasing map $\tau \mapsto t$ maps $\mathbb R$ onto $(-\infty ,0)$. These formulas therefore define an ancient curve-shortening flow in $\mathbb R^{2m}$, of dimension one and codimension $2m-1$. They are explicit in the auxiliary time $\tau $; physical time generally requires inversion of the displayed scalar relation. Indeed, with

\[ D(\tau )=\sum _ j\omega _ j^2a_ j^2e^{-2\omega _ j^2\tau }, \qquad \frac{dt}{d\tau }=D, \]

one has $|C_\theta |^2=D$, $C_\tau =C_{\theta \theta }$, and $C_ t=C_{ss}$. This direct differentiation fixes the coefficient in the time relation.

At each finite time the curve is an injective immersion of $\mathbb R$, dense in the product of the $m$ coordinate circles. It is not an embedding: recurrence gives sequences escaping in the parameter whose images converge to a point of the curve. It is also not proper. Its constant positive speed makes its induced metric complete. Its parameter manifold is noncompact with two intrinsic ends, although its image is bounded and neither end escapes every compact set. The closure is a torus; the evolving object is a curve, not that torus as a surface. The flow has simultaneous rotational symmetry and is not homothetically self-similar because distinct coordinate radii have changing ratios.

The exact radius and curvature identities are

\[ |C|^2=-2t,\qquad |C_{ss}|^2=\frac{\sum _ j\omega _ j^4 a_ j^2e^{-2\omega _ j^2\tau }}{D(\tau )^2}. \]

Thus curvature is constant along each time slice. After dividing by $\sqrt{-2t}$, the largest-frequency coordinate pair dominates as $t\to -\infty $, and the smallest-frequency pair dominates as $t\uparrow 0$. The locally parametrized limits are great circles traced by an unbounded parameter; no finite-multiplicity convergence is asserted for the full recurrent curve.

At a fixed time, the infinite length lies in a bounded set. Consequently every positive-scale Gaussian length integral is infinite, and both $F_{0,1}$ and entropy are $+\infty $. This is a direct consequence of the parametrization, with length counted on the immersed domain. No finite Gaussian-area or locally finite length assertion is intended.

For the illustrated $\mathbb R^4$ case take $a_1=a_2=1$ and $(\omega _1,\omega _2)=(1,\sqrt2)$. An explicit physical-time formula is obtained by setting

\[ u(t)=\frac{\sqrt{1-8t}-1}{2},\qquad C(\theta ,t)=\bigl (\sqrt u\cos \theta ,\sqrt u\sin \theta , u\cos (\sqrt2\theta ),u\sin (\sqrt2\theta )\bigr ),\qquad t<0. \]

Source: [AAAW13], Section 3, equations (11)–(14), PDF pp. 8–10. The irrational-frequency case is distinct from the compact rational-frequency family in 5.16. The figure samples a finite parameter interval and projects four-dimensional geometry; apparent crossings in this projection do not establish self-intersections in $\mathbb R^4$. No stability classification is recorded.
