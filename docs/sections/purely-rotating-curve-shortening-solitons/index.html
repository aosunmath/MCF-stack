§ 5.4 Purely rotating curve-shortening solitons3A1BA479B53D

3A1BA479B53D
999F58954ACB

Let $A$ be a nonzero skew-symmetric linear map on $\mathbb R^ n$, $n\geq 2$. For a unit vector $T$ write $P_ Tw=w-\langle w,T\rangle T$. Given $C(0)=p$ and $T(0)=q$ with $|q|=1$, solve

\[ C_ s=T,\qquad T_ s=P_ T(AC). \]

The solution is unique for these initial data and exists for every $s\in \mathbb R$: $|T|=1$ and $|C(s)|\leq |p|+|s|$ prevent finite-parameter blow-up. Thus it is a complete immersed curve of codimension $n-1$. Its associated eternal flow is

\[ X(s,t)=e^{tA}C(s),\qquad t\in \mathbb R, \qquad (X_ t)^\perp =X_{\ell \ell }, \]

where $\ell $ is arclength at time $t$. The exponential is an exact evolution law for the ODE-defined profile, not a closed formula for that profile. We exclude the stationary straight lines in $\ker A$.

Both ends satisfy $|C(s)|\to \infty $ as $s\to \pm \infty $; consequently the immersion is proper and noncompact, with domain $\mathbb R$ and two ends. Its distance from the origin has a unique minimum. In the plane, $A=\omega J$ with $J(x,y)=(-y,x)$ gives the rotating spiral family. The initial data $C(0)=0$, $T(0)=(1,0)$ give the usual centrally symmetric Yin–Yang example. A logarithmic spiral is not its exact profile; the purely rotating case has different far-field behavior from the expanding rotators in 5.5.

For $\mathbb R^3$, let $A(x,y,z)=\omega (-y,x,0)$, $\omega >0$. If $T_ z(0)\ne 0$, the identity

\[ z_ s(s)=z_ s(0)\exp \left(-\int _0^ s\langle AC,T\rangle \, d\sigma \right) \]

shows that $z$ is strictly monotone. The curve is therefore embedded and is a graph over an interval of the rotation axis. This interval can be bounded or a half-line; a finite endpoint is approached exponentially in arclength. If $T_ z(0)=0$, the curve stays in a plane perpendicular to that axis. More generally, the projection to $\ker A$ lies in a line, and any nonconstant such projection is injective. No blanket embeddedness assertion is made for profiles with constant kernel projection or for invertible $A$ in even dimensions.

Source: [AAAW13], Section 4.1, equation (27), Lemmas 5.4 and 6.3, PDF pp. 11–13, 15–20, and Section 7.1, p. 23. Initial-value uniqueness is not a uniqueness theorem for prescribed ends. No general curvature bound, stability theorem, or Gaussian-area or entropy value is recorded here. The figures integrate the profile equation with $\omega =1$: one spatial example has $p=(1,0,0)$, $q=(0,0,1)$; a second shows the planar Yin–Yang profile. Both are truncated numerical ODE curves with exact rotation in the time control.

Ambient spaces: ℝ² · ℝ³ · ℝ⁴ · Higher dimensions (N ≥ 5)
Ambient space, topology, embeddedness and entropy

All profiles are complete proper curves with two ends. A nonzero kernel projection gives an embedding; the general even-dimensional case is not assigned that conclusion.
