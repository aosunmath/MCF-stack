§ 3.36 Invariant translators in the special linear group9C3485CD7693

Paper credits and chronology

Paper credits are chronological and include every author of the cited papers. They identify sources, not exclusive discovery credit. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. López–Munteanu — 2024-05-24 (first arXiv submission).
9C3485CD7693
3535C925421E

López–Munteanu classify invariant translating surfaces in $SL(2,\mathbb R)$ with its standard left-invariant metric. In Iwasawa coordinates $(x,y,\theta )$, $y>0$ and $\theta \in S^1$, the metric is

\[ g=\frac{dx^2+dy^2}{4y^2}+\left(d\theta +\frac{dx}{2y}\right)^2. \]

The invariant subgroups are the parabolic, hyperbolic and elliptic factors $N,A,K$. The driving field is selected from the Killing algebra, and is separate from the field tangent to the invariant orbits. The resulting classification contains explicit special cases and ODE-defined families, as well as minimal stationary exceptions. The source's scalar mean curvature is the average of the principal curvatures: a profile satisfying $H=\langle N,X\rangle $ evolves under trace MCF by the isometries generated by $2X$. Complete Killing motion is eternal, but does not make an incomplete profile complete. Global topology, properness and embeddedness must therefore be checked for the chosen subgroup, profile domain and circle identifications; no blanket assertion is recorded here. The visualization is an orbit-space and fiber diagram in the $\mathbb H^2(-4)$ base with its circle fiber. It does not depict the universal cover as identical to $SL(2,\mathbb R)$ or assign Euclidean metric geometry to the coordinate plot.

Sources: [LopezMunteanu2024].
