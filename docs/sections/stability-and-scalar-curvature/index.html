11.7 Stability and scalar curvatureFAABD01A7002

FAABD01A7002

In dimension three the Gauss equation rewrites the Jacobi potential in terms of scalar curvature and the intrinsic Gaussian curvature. Stability then has topological consequences. The arguments follow [ChMin], Section 24, with closedness, orientability, and the convention $R_\Sigma =2K_\Sigma $ stated explicitly.

Proposition 11.7.1 (The traced Gauss equation).3E7688AA201F For a smooth two-sided hypersurface $\Sigma ^ n\subset M^{n+1}$,

\[ R_ M=R_\Sigma +2\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )+|A|^2-H^2. \]

Here scalar curvature is twice the sum of sectional curvatures over unordered orthonormal pairs, and $H$ is the sum of principal curvatures. This is [ChMin], Lemma 24.2, pp. 61–62.

Proof

At a point diagonalize the scalar second fundamental form in an orthonormal frame $e_1,\ldots ,e_ n$, with principal curvatures $\kappa _ i$. The Gauss equation on each tangent two-plane is $K_\Sigma (e_ i,e_ j)=K_ M(e_ i,e_ j)+\kappa _ i\kappa _ j$. It follows by inserting $\nabla ^ M_ XY=\nabla ^\Sigma _ XY-h(X,Y)\nu $ in the curvature definition and taking the tangential component. Sum over $i\ne j$. The extrinsic sum is $H^2-|A|^2$, and the omitted ambient two-planes containing $\nu $ contribute $2\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )$. Rearranging proves the identity. $\square$

Corollary 11.7.2 (The Jacobi potential on a minimal surface).522E4EE726E6 For a two-sided minimal surface $\Sigma ^2\subset M^3$,

\[ q=|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu ) =\frac12(R_ M+|A|^2)-K_\Sigma . \]

Consequently its stability inequality is equivalent to

\[ \frac12\int _\Sigma (R_ M+|A|^2)\phi ^2 \leq \int _\Sigma \bigl (|\nabla \phi |^2+K_\Sigma \phi ^2\bigr ) \]

for every compactly supported smooth test function in the interior.

Proof

Put $H=0$ and $R_\Sigma =2K_\Sigma $ in Proposition 11.7.1, solve for $q$, and substitute in Definition 11.6.1. The factor two relates scalar and Gaussian curvature on a surface. $\square$

Theorem 11.7.3 (Topology and area of a stable surface in positive scalar curvature).478117A3A30D Let $\Sigma $ be a nonempty smooth closed connected orientable two-sided stable minimal surface in a Riemannian three-manifold. If $R_ M>0$ along $\Sigma $, then $\Sigma $ has genus zero. If moreover $R_ M\geq \sigma >0$ there, then

\[ \operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )\leq \frac{8\pi }{\sigma }. \]

The genus conclusion is the closed orientable form of [ChMin], Theorem 24.3, attributed there to Schoen–Yau. Both the closedness and the orientability hypotheses enter the proof.

Proof

The constant test function is admissible because $\Sigma $ is closed. Corollary 11.7.2 and Gauss–Bonnet give

\[ 0<\int _\Sigma (R_ M+|A|^2) \leq 2\int _\Sigma K_\Sigma =4\pi \chi (\Sigma )=8\pi (1-g). \]

Thus the nonnegative integer $g$ is zero. The same inequality with $R_ M\geq \sigma $ gives $\sigma \operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )\leq 8\pi $. Gauss–Bonnet and the classification of closed orientable surfaces are the topological inputs. This argument applies to an immersion as well, using its induced metric and global normal. $\square$

Proposition 11.7.4 (Rigidity of a stable torus under nonnegative scalar curvature).EBC1C1900387 Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth closed two-sided stable minimal torus in $M^3$, and suppose $R_ M\geq 0$ along $\Sigma $. Then, along $\Sigma $,

\[ A=0,\qquad R_ M=0,\qquad \operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )=0,\qquad K_\Sigma =0. \]

In particular the induced metric is flat. No splitting of an ambient neighborhood is asserted.

Proof

The constant stability test and $\chi (\Sigma )=0$ give $\int (R_ M+|A|^2)\leq 0$. Nonnegativity and continuity force $R_ M=|A|=0$. The traced Gauss equation then gives $q=\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )=-K_\Sigma $, and Gauss–Bonnet gives $Q(1,1)=-\int q=0$. For arbitrary smooth $\psi $, stability of $1+s\psi $ for both signs of small $s$ forces $Q(1,\psi )=0$. Thus $\int q\psi =0$ for every $\psi $, so $q=0$ pointwise. Hence $K_\Sigma =0$ as well. This is the equality argument in the preceding stability calculation; the polarization step is needed to obtain pointwise Ricci and Gaussian curvature conclusions. $\square$

Example 11.7.5 (A flat stable torus).785CBF422ACB In the flat product $\mathbb T^2\times \mathbb S^1$, each slice $\mathbb T^2\times \{ t\} $ is two-sided and totally geodesic, with $q=0$. Hence $Q(\phi ,\phi )=\int |\nabla \phi |^2\geq 0$. This realizes the zero-scalar-curvature case of Proposition 11.7.4; replacing positive scalar curvature by nonnegative scalar curvature does not force a sphere.

Example 11.7.6 (Why orientability is a separate hypothesis).1FEFF0FF3A61 Give $\mathbb {RP}^2$ the metric induced from the unit round sphere and consider $M=\mathbb {RP}^2\times \mathbb S^1$ with the product metric. A slice $\mathbb {RP}^2\times \{ t\} $ is a closed two-sided totally geodesic minimal surface. The product direction gives a global unit normal with $\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )=0$, so the slice is stable. The ambient scalar curvature equals $2$, but the slice is not orientable and is not a sphere. This is the possibility noted in the footnote to [ChMin], Theorem 24.3. Two-sidedness alone implies orientability only when the ambient manifold is oriented.
