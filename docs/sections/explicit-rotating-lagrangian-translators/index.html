§ 3.20 Explicit rotating Lagrangian translators5C21E6709CA8

5C21E6709CA8 An elementary subfamily of the same quadric ansatz has unbounded Lagrangian angle.
A19CAED49B0D

(Rotating translating planes) For $n\geq 2$, put $k=n-1$. The exact map

\[ X(x,s)=\left(x_1e^{is},\ldots ,x_ ke^{is},\tfrac 12|x|^2-is\right), \qquad (x,s)\in \mathbb R^ k\times \mathbb R, \]

is a complete proper embedded Lagrangian in $\mathbb C^ n$, of dimension and codimension $n$. Its translator velocity is $V=(0,\ldots ,0,k)$, so $X+tV$, $t\in \mathbb R$, is eternal. Spatial dilation by $k$ gives unit translating speed if desired.

This is the constant-radius case of Theorem G and Corollary H of [JoyceLeeTsui2010], with $\lambda _ j=-1$, $a_ j=A=1$, $u=0$, and $\alpha =k$. Directly, the metric and a lifted angle are

\[ g=\sum _ jdx_ j^2+\left(\sum _ jx_ jdx_ j\right)^2+(1+|x|^2)ds^2, \qquad \theta =ks-\pi /2. \]

Consequently

\[ \vec H=J\nabla \theta =\frac{k}{1+|x|^2}(-x_1e^{is},\ldots ,-x_ ke^{is},1)=V^\perp . \]

The last imaginary coordinate recovers $s$, proving injectivity; $|X|^2=|x|^2+|x|^4/4+s^2$ proves properness. The metric dominates the Euclidean domain metric, proving completeness. Topologically it is $\mathbb R^ n$, with one end. It has a combined rotation and imaginary-translation symmetry and is not almost calibrated. No stability or entropy value is claimed. The four-dimensional illustration samples the exact $n=2$ formula.
