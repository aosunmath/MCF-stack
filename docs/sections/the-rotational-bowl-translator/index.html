3.3 The rotational bowl translator38B2DD284E09

38B2DD284E09

Example 3.3.1 (The rotational bowl translator).ECF360A3D482 For each $n\geq 2$, the bowl is the entire graph $z=f(r)$, $r=|x|$ in $\mathbb R^ n$, moving by $z\mapsto z+t$. Its radial profile is specified by the regular ODE

\[ \frac{f''}{1+(f')^2}+\frac{n-1}{r}f'=1,\qquad f(0)=f'(0)=0,\qquad f''(0)=1/n. \]

This is an ODE characterization, not an elementary closed formula.

  • The bowl is complete, properly embedded, strictly convex, rotationally invariant, and diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^ n$. For $n\geq 2$ it has one topological end and gives an eternal flow.

  • Up to a vertical additive constant, its height has asymptotic expansion

    \[ f(r)=\frac{r^2}{2(n-1)}-\log r+C+O(r^{-1})\quad (r\to \infty ). \]

    A paraboloid is only a leading approximation, not the exact translator.

  • It is unique among entire rotationally invariant translating graphs, up to translation. In $\mathbb R^3$ it is the only complete entire translating graph, up to the natural rigid motions and normalization.

  • In $\mathbb R^3$, Brendle–Choi's theorem characterizes its flow among noncompact, strictly convex, noncollapsed ancient flows, up to scaling and ambient isometries. These are the hypotheses of the cited classification.

  • Graphical weighted stability holds. The stronger dynamical stability theorem of Clutterbuck–Schnürer–Schulze concerns continuous graphical initial data whose height difference from the bowl tends to zero at spatial infinity; the resulting height difference tends uniformly to zero as time tends to infinity.

The supplied notes [CatHIMWNotes19], Section 4 and Theorem 4.1, PDF pages 10–11, describe the construction and classification. The original ODE and asymptotic analysis is [CSS07], Section 2, Lemmas 2.1–2.2; its Theorem 1.1 gives the stated dynamical stability. The ancient classification is [CatBrendleChoi19], Theorem 1.1, printed page 36 (PDF page 2). A local construction and estimates are recorded in Theorem 20.4.3.
