§ 5.10 Compact nonconvex ancient spiral curvesF98988E964AC

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Zhang–Olson–Khan–Angenent — 2022-04-11 (first arXiv submission).
F98988E964AC
505A2B69BD4F

Zhang–Olson–Khan–Angenent construct compact embedded ancient curve-shortening flows $C_ t\subset \mathbb R^2$, $t<0$, whose distant past follows a truncated rotating Yin–Yang spiral closed by a small grim-reaper-shaped cap. They are smooth simple closed curves, hence complete and proper, with no ends. They are nonconvex in the ancient regime and are neither shrinking circles nor ancient paperclips. No assertion of nonconvexity through a later maximal continuation is needed here.

The limiting rotating model has angular speed one. Its radial expansion $\mathcal R(\theta )=\sqrt{2\theta }+O(\theta ^{-3/2})$ is asymptotic, not a formula for the constructed ancient curve. If $\Omega (t)$ is the paper's truncated spiral region and $\Lambda (t)$ is the enclosed ancient region, the Main Theorem proves

\[ |\Lambda (t)\mathbin {\triangle }\Omega (t)|\leq C_\delta |t|^{-1+\delta }\qquad (t\to -\infty ) \]

for each $\delta >0$. This is an area estimate, not an exact pointwise parametrization. Gaussian area and entropy are finite at each compact slice; no uniform ancient bound is assigned.

Source: [ZOKA23], Main Theorem and equations (1.1)–(1.3), pp. 1–3, and Appendix A. No uniqueness is claimed. The source also credits Charyyev's independent construction. The figure samples the asymptotic construction at $t=-24,-18,-12,-8$ on a common spatial scale. Its blue arms solve the rotating-soliton ODE numerically; the orange cap uses the corrected grim-reaper ansatz in Section 2. These are model snapshots, not a numerical solution of the ancient evolution equation or an illustration of extinction.

To read the motion, write $E(\theta )=(\cos \theta ,\sin \theta )$ and $J(x,y)=(-y,x)$. The two arms are

\[ Y_\pm (\theta ,t)=\mathcal R(\theta -t\pm \pi /2)E(\theta ), \qquad \theta -t\pm \pi /2>\pi /2, \]

truncated near $\theta =-t$. Here $\mathcal R$ is the Appendix A solution of

\[ \tfrac 12\mathcal R^2-\theta +\arctan (\mathcal R'/\mathcal R)=0, \qquad \theta >\pi /2, \]

with $\mathcal R\downarrow 0$ as $\theta \downarrow \pi /2$. At a fixed point of the unrotated model the arms rotate counterclockwise with speed one. The cap center instead follows $R(t)E(-t)$, where $R(t)=\mathcal R(-2t)\sim 2\sqrt{-t}$, so it moves clockwise and inward. Its leading width is $\pi /R(t)$. The inset uses $X=e^{-tJ}[(R(t),0)+R(t)^{-1}(u,v)]$; its dashed reference is the leading grim reaper

\[ G(p)=(-\arcsin (\tanh p),-\log \cosh p),\qquad p\in \mathbb R. \]

The orange curve includes the paper's normal correction $(-4t)^{-1}F(p)JG'(p)$, equations (2.19)–(2.21), and a smooth interpolation to the arms. These asymptotic formulas apply in the distant past; the figure's finite time interval carries no certified approximation-error bound.
