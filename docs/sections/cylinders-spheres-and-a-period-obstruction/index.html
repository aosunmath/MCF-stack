20.8 Cylinders, spheres and a period obstruction845C5A1F3702

845C5A1F3702

These calculations apply Section 15.9 to explicit harmonic maps. The construction is from [Hel01], pp. 19–21; the formulas below check its geometry, degeneracies, and global limitations directly.

Example 20.8.1 (An equatorial harmonic map gives a cylinder).964FA842F974 For $u(x,y)=(\cos x,\sin x,0)$ on $\mathbb R^2$, a reconstruction primitive is $B=(0,0,-y)$. The two maps

\[ X_\varepsilon (x,y)=(\varepsilon \cos x,\varepsilon \sin x,-y) \]

parametrize a unit circular cylinder, with induced metric $dx^2+dy^2$ and trace mean curvature $\varepsilon $ relative to $u$. They are covering immersions on $\mathbb R^2$ and descend to embedded cylinders on $(\mathbb R/2\pi \mathbb Z)\times \mathbb R$.

Proof

Here $u_{xx}=-u$, $u_ y=0$, and $|Du|^2=1$, so $u$ is harmonic. Since $u\times u_ x=(0,0,1)$, the reconstruction form is $-(0,0,1)dy=dB$. The formulas and metric follow by differentiation. The mean curvature follows from Theorem 15.9.4; for $\varepsilon =1$ it is the positive outward cylinder curvature. Periodicity in $x$ explains the covering, and the quotient map is injective onto the cylinder. The product metric is complete. $\square$

Example 20.8.2 (Harmonic phases and cylinder parametrizations).6BD94503E233 Let $\varphi $ be a smooth real harmonic function on a simply connected planar domain, and let $\psi $ be a harmonic conjugate, so $\psi _ x=-\varphi _ y$ and $\psi _ y=\varphi _ x$. Then $u=(\cos \varphi ,\sin \varphi ,0)$ is harmonic and

\[ X_\varepsilon =(\varepsilon \cos \varphi ,\varepsilon \sin \varphi ,-\psi ) \]

is its CMC reconstruction. Its metric is $|\nabla \varphi |^2(dx^2+dy^2)$. It is an immersion exactly where $\nabla \varphi \ne 0$; its image lies on the unit cylinder, but global injectivity is not implied.

Proof

Writing $v=(-\sin \varphi ,\cos \varphi ,0)$ gives $u_ i=v\varphi _ i$ and $\Delta u=v\Delta \varphi -u|\nabla \varphi |^2=-u|Du|^2$. Also $u\times v=e_3$, so $\alpha =e_3(\varphi _ y dx-\varphi _ xdy)=-e_3d\psi $. This proves the primitive and formula. The Cauchy–Riemann equations give $|X_ x|^2=|X_ y|^2=\varphi _ x^2+\varphi _ y^2$ and $X_ x\cdot X_ y=0$. Critical points of $\varphi $ give zero differentials; elsewhere the reconstruction theorem applies. Even without critical points, periodicity of the trigonometric functions can identify different domain points. $\square$

Example 20.8.3 (One stereographic map gives a sphere and a constant map).741A30DF8826 Put $r^2=x^2+y^2$ and

\[ u(x,y)=\frac{(2x,2y,1-r^2)}{1+r^2}. \]

This is an orientation-preserving conformal harmonic parametrization of $S^2$ minus its south pole. Its reconstruction primitive is $B=-u$, so $X_+=0$ and $X_-=-2u$. Thus one reconstruction is constant, whereas the other parametrizes a sphere of radius two with one point omitted.

Proof

Direct differentiation gives

\[ |u_ x|^2=|u_ y|^2=\frac4{(1+r^2)^2},\quad u_ x\cdot u_ y=0,\quad u\cdot (u_ x\times u_ y)=\frac4{(1+r^2)^2}, \quad \Delta u=-\frac8{(1+r^2)^2}u. \]

The last equality is the harmonic-map equation. Orientation and conformality give $u_ y=u\times u_ x$. Hence $u\times u_ y=-u_ x$ and $-u\times u_ x=-u_ y$, so $\alpha =-du$. The claimed primitives and reconstructions follow. The spherical image of $-2u$ has center zero and radius two; its chosen normal $u$ points inward, so $H=-1$, consistent with Theorem 15.9.4. No extension across infinity is needed for the assertion on the planar chart. $\square$

Example 20.8.4 (A logarithmic phase with a nonzero reconstruction period).87EFE28EE2D2 On an annulus $\{ a<r<b\} $ with $0<a<b<\infty $, the harmonic map

\[ u=(\cos \log r,\sin \log r,0) \]

has reconstruction form $\alpha =-e_3d\theta $. Its period around a positively oriented circle is $-2\pi e_3$. Consequently this reconstruction has no single-valued primitive on the annulus, although it gives cylinder parametrizations on the universal cover.

Proof

The function $\log r$ is harmonic away from zero. Locally its harmonic conjugate is $\theta $, so Example 20.8.2 gives the form. The one-form $d\theta =(-y\, dx+x\, dy)/r^2$ is globally defined on the annulus, and its circle integral is $2\pi $. Proposition 15.9.2 therefore rules out a single-valued primitive. On the cover with coordinates $(t,\theta )=(\log r,\theta )$ and real $\theta $, take $B=-\theta e_3$ and $X_\varepsilon =(\varepsilon \cos t,\varepsilon \sin t,-\theta )$. A deck transformation $\theta \mapsto \theta +2\pi $ translates the image by $-2\pi e_3$, so it does not descend as a map to $\mathbb R^3$. This is an obstruction to this construction on this domain, not a classification of all CMC immersions. $\square$
