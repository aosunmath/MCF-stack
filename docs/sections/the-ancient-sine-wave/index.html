§ 5.1 The ancient sine wave (Nakayama–Iizuka–Wadati, 1994; Angenent–You, 2018)D8ADD5B24C33

An exact eternal graphical solution of planar curve shortening is

\[ X(x,t)=(x,\operatorname {arsinh}(e^{-t}\sin x)),\qquad x\in \mathbb R,\quad t\in \mathbb R. \]

Writing its height as $u$, direct differentiation gives $u_ t=u_{xx}/(1+u_ x^2)$; this also fixes the time normalization. Spatial translations, time translations and parabolic dilations give the usual equivalent versions. Nakayama–Iizuka–Wadati's ancient sine wave is recalled in Angenent–You's introduction. Each slice is complete, proper and embedded, with parameter topology $\mathbb R$, two ends and period $2\pi $ in $x$. It is nonconvex and has infinite total absolute curvature, because each spatial period has a positive curvature integral. At each fixed finite time its slope is bounded, so it has finite Gaussian area and entropy, without a uniform-in-time value asserted here. Forward in time it approaches the axis; backward, long nearly vertical portions are joined near successive extrema. The exact graph are shown at three times. This is distinct from the finite-total-curvature grim chains.

Sources: [NakayamaIizukaWadati1994, AY18].
