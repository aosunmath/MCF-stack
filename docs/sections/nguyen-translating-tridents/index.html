Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. X. H. Nguyen — 2009 (publication year). Construction: Constructs the small-neck tridents by desingularizing a grim reaper and plane and solving the nonlinear perturbation problem. Passage: Theorem 1; Section 5, proof of Theorem 1. Reviewed text.
  2. Hoffman–Ilmanen–Martín–White — 2019-01-25 (first arXiv submission). Survey: Surveys the examples and points to their construction papers. The survey’s full author list is not a construction credit for this example. Passage: Sections 3–5, especially pp. 10–12.
  3. Hoffman–Martín–White — 2019-09-19 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Constructs tridents for every positive neck parameter by a variational method. The introduction explicitly identifies the small-neck overlap with Nguyen’s construction. Passage: Theorem 1; Sections 2–3.

Nguyen's tridents are complete periodic embedded translating surfaces in $\mathbb R^3$ obtained by desingularizing a grim-reaper cylinder and a plane. We use unit upward velocity $e_3$, so $M_ t=M_0+t e_3$ for all $t\in \mathbb R$. There is no explicit global formula for the completed translator.

  • They are noncompact and have infinite genus. A repeating row of Scherk saddle connections resolves the intersection; the intersecting input surfaces are not themselves a trident.

  • In the upward convention there is one asymptotic plane as $z\to -\infty $ and three parallel asymptotic planes as $z\to +\infty $. These describe height-direction asymptotics, not a count of topological ends of the full periodic surface.

  • Nguyen's construction applies at sufficiently small positive scales. The later semigraphical construction agrees with these examples in that range; its tridents are properly embedded and have entropy $3$ [HMWTridents22], Theorem 1 and pp. 2–3. This entropy is not identified with centered Gaussian area.

The local minimal ingredient is $\sin v=\sinh u\sinh w$, with $(u,v,w)\in \mathbb R^3$ and period $2\pi $ in $v$. Its four wings are attached to two planar wings and two grim-reaper wings. In the unit upward convention the limiting inputs are $z=-\log \cos y$, $|y|<\pi /2$, $x\in \mathbb R$, and the plane $y=0$. See [Nguyen09], Theorem 1, Sections 1.2–1.4 and Section 2 (Definitions 4–5), and [HMWTridents22], Figures 1–2. The latter figures use downward translation; here height is reflected.

The interactive picture is an original smooth deformation of the Scherk ingredient, with three periods shown. Its half-surface view exposes the saddle connections. Its neck scale is a drawing parameter: the unbalancing, cutoff matching, and final nonlinear correction in Nguyen's proof have not been computed. In particular, its fixed illustrative half-width $\pi /2$ is the limiting input width, not the width of a finite-period trident. See [HIMWNotes19], Sections 4–5, for the survey comparison with the simply connected pitchfork.
