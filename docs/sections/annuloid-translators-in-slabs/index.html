§ 3.15 Annuloid translators in slabs (Hoffman–Martín–White, 2023)93A7D23F7FA3

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Gama–Martín–Møller — 2022-09-04 (first arXiv submission). Background only: General entropy relation used for annuloids in the later construction paper; not a reference constructing this family. Analysis: Studies entropy, wing counts, and uniqueness of previously constructed translators; this is not the construction source for the family. Passage: Introduction; entropy and pitchfork uniqueness results.
  2. Hoffman–Martín–White — 2023-08-04 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Constructs complete properly embedded annular translators with the prescribed inner width and necksize. Passage: Introduction and annuloid existence theorem.

4887FC9B88DC

For every inner half-width $b\geq \pi /2$ and necksize $s>0$, Hoffman–Martín–White construct a complete properly embedded annular translator in $\mathbb R^3$. Their necksize is the distance from the $z$-axis to $M\cap \{ y=0\} $; it is not assumed to be a circular neck radius. The surface lies in a slab $|y|\leq B$, has both vertical coordinate-plane reflection symmetries, and is nonrotational. Its topology is $S^1\times \mathbb R$: genus zero and two ends.

After reflecting the source's downward translator, $M_ t=M+te_3$ for all $t\in \mathbb R$. The positive-height asymptotics are four planes $y=\pm b,\pm B$, counted with multiplicity if $b=B$; the negative-height end of the ambient space sees no surface. Four asymptotic planes in one height direction do not mean four topological ends. The outer half-width $B$ is an output of the construction, not an independent prescribed parameter. Capped and uncapped behaviors occur. No closed formula or uniqueness for fixed $(b,s)$ is supplied.

The entropy is exactly $4$, by [HMWAnnuli24], Remark 7.9, using [GMM25], Corollary 8.5. This is not a fixed-center Gaussian-area identity. Existence and the parameters are in Theorem 1.1, Definition 1.2, and Sections 7 and 22 of [HMWAnnuli24]. The displayed neck model is heuristic.
