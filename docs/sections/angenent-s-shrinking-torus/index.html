§ 2.13 Embedded rotational shrinking tori (Angenent, 1992)EB7B6435F145

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Angenent — 1992 (publication year). Construction: Proves existence of the embedded rotational shrinking torus by a shooting construction. Passage: Main Theorem and the toroidal geodesic construction.
  2. Colding–Minicozzi II — 2009-08-26 (first arXiv submission). Analysis: Studies stability and generic singularities using these shrinkers; it does not construct this catalogue family. Passage: Introduction; stability and generic-singularity theorems.
  3. Drugan–Lee–X. H. Nguyen — 2017-08-30 (first arXiv submission). Survey: Surveys earlier constructions with their original attributions; a cited or reproduced construction is not credited to the survey authors as a new construction. Passage: Introduction and survey of closed self-shrinkers with symmetry.
  4. Berchenko-Kogan — 2018-08-24 (first arXiv submission). Analysis: Numerically estimates the entropy of Angenent’s previously constructed torus, not a new construction of the torus or its cylindrical products. Passage: Introduction; numerical entropy computation.
EB7B6435F145

Angenent constructs an embedded rotational self-shrinker with topology $S^1\times S^{n-1}$ for each $n\geq 2$ in [Angenent1992]. We use the normalized profile equation from [DLN2018], Section 3.3 and equation (5.2), PDF pp. 8 and 14.

10374A017921

There is a simple closed profile $s\mapsto (x(s),r(s))$ in $r>0$ whose rotation gives

\[ X(s,\omega )=(x(s),r(s)\omega )\in \mathbb R\times \mathbb R^ n, \qquad \omega \in S^{n-1}(1). \]

For Euclidean arclength $s$ and tangent angle $\alpha $, the profile solves

\[ x'=\cos \alpha ,\qquad r'=\sin \alpha ,\qquad \alpha '=\left(\frac{n-1}{r}-\frac r2\right)\cos \alpha +\frac x2\sin \alpha . \]

Equivalently, it is a closed geodesic of $r^{2(n-1)}e^{-(x^2+r^2)/2}(dx^2+dr^2)$ on $r>0$. The theorem selects a closed orbit by shooting; the profile is not given by a closed formula. Its flow is $\sqrt{-t}\, \Sigma $, $t<0$.

  • The hypersurface is compact, embedded, complete and proper, with topology $S^1\times S^{n-1}$, rotational $O(n)$ symmetry and reflection symmetry across $x=0$. When $n=2$ it is a torus in $\mathbb R^3$.

  • It is not mean convex. Indeed, the compact embedded mean-convex classification in [CM12], Theorem 0.17, would force a sphere. It shrinks to the origin with a Type I singularity.

  • It is entropy unstable by [CM12], Theorem 0.12, which applies to this compact embedded non-spherical hypersurface. No exact entropy value or global uniqueness assertion is supplied here. Berchenko–Kogan's numerical calculation estimates its entropy as approximately $1.85122$ ([BerchenkoKogan2021], Section 5, PDF pp. 10–12). This estimate has no certified error enclosure; Section 6, Question 6.3, leaves rigorous error bounds open. The proved bound for the separately constructed variational torus is recorded in Example 2.28.

The interactive figure revolves a numerically closed solution of this ODE; its caption records the shooting residual. The profile is not circular. The variational torus in [DLN2018], Theorem 2, is a separate existence construction; that survey does not identify it with Angenent's torus.
