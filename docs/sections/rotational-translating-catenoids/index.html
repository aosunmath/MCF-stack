§ 3.3 Rotational translating catenoids (Clutterbuck–Schnürer–Schulze, 2005)FEA31DEDC853

Paper credits and chronology

Title credits include all authors of papers with a reviewed construction of this example, in chronological order. Later analysis, surveys, and citations remain listed here; they do not by themselves earn construction credit. Alternative and numerical constructions are qualified below. These credits are not an exhaustive discovery history. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Clutterbuck–Schnürer–Schulze — 2005-09-16 (first arXiv submission). Construction: Constructs the winglike solutions by solving the neck ODE and extending both branches to infinity; unlike Lemma 2.2, this is an existence construction in this paper. Passage: Section 2.2, Lemma 2.3 and its proof.
  2. Hoffman–Ilmanen–Martín–White — 2019-01-25 (first arXiv submission). Survey: Surveys the examples and points to their construction papers. The survey’s full author list is not a construction credit for this example. Passage: Sections 3–5, especially pp. 10–12.
FEA31DEDC853
72E3A9DEA98C

For every neck radius $R>0$ and $n\geq 2$, there is a rotational translating hypersurface with two ends. These are also called winglike translators. They move upward with unit speed. A convenient numerical description uses meridian arclength $s$ and tangent angle $\alpha $:

\[ r'=\cos \alpha ,\qquad z'=\sin \alpha ,\qquad \alpha '=\cos \alpha -\frac{n-1}{r}\sin \alpha , \qquad (r,z,\alpha )(0)=(R,0,\pi /2). \]

Revolution gives $(r(s)\omega ,z(s)+t)$ with $\omega \in S^{n-1}$ and $t\in \mathbb R$. The arclength system is obtained from $\vec H=e_{n+1}^{\perp }$ and is not a closed formula.

  • The surface is complete, embedded, noncompact and nonconvex, with topology $S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R$. In $\mathbb R^3$ it is an annulus, not an entire graph.

  • Outside the neck the two ends are radial graphs. Each has expansion

    \[ f_\pm (r)=\frac{r^2}{2(n-1)}-\log r+C_\pm +O(r^{-1}). \]

    In particular the two ends go to spatial infinity and the complete surface is proper.

  • The parameter controls the neck radius. The supplied survey describes the limiting double bowl with a singular point on the axis as the neck parameter tends to zero.

See [HIMWNotes19], Section 4, PDF page 10. The original construction and end expansion are [CSS07], Lemma 2.3, PDF pages 5–7 of the author preprint. No uniqueness beyond the radial initial-value construction, nor an entropy or stability classification for this non-graphical surface, is asserted here.
