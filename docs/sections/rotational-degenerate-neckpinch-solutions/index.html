§ 5.14 Rotational degenerate neckpinch solutions1E4AD3904D39

Paper credits and chronology

Paper credits are chronological and include every author of the cited papers. They identify sources, not exclusive discovery credit. Background-only citations do not determine the date of this example. A classical example may predate the references recorded here.

  1. Angenent–Velázquez — 1997 (publication year).
1E4AD3904D39
922034E946EB

(Angenent–Velázquez type-II singularities) Let $n\geq 2$, let $m\geq 3$ be odd, and let $K>0$. Choose a compact rotational target hypersurface in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, smooth away from a cusp at the origin, whose radius satisfies $u_*(x)\sim \sqrt K\, x^{m/2}$ as $x\downarrow 0$ and whose other tip closes smoothly. Angenent and Velázquez construct smooth compact rotational mean curvature flows

\[ M_ t=\{ (x,u(x,t)\omega ):a(t)\leq x\leq b(t),\ \omega \in S^{n-1}\} , \qquad t_0<t<T, \]

with smooth caps and positive radius between them. Each regular slice is an embedded sphere, hence complete and proper. The constructed flows remain close to the chosen target outside a small neighborhood of the origin as the construction interval is shortened. They are not supplied as ancient solutions or as solitons, and no closed formula for $u(x,t)$ is given.

Set $\xi _*=(2(n-1)/K)^{1/m}$. At the left tip,

\[ a(t)=-(\xi _*+o(1))(T-t)^{1/m},\qquad |\vec H|_{\rm tip}(t)=\left(\frac{\xi _*}{m}+o(1)\right)(T-t)^{1/m-1}. \]

Here the curvature coefficient is for the trace mean curvature, equivalently the magnitude $|\vec H|$ at this convex tip. Rotational smoothness makes the $n$ principal curvatures equal at the tip, so $|A|_{\rm tip}=|\vec H|_{\rm tip}/\sqrt n$. The maximum of $|A|$ is attained there; its blow-up rate is type II. The parabolic rescalings at the origin converge smoothly on compact sets to $S^{n-1}_{\sqrt{2(n-1)}}\times \mathbb R$, whereas rescaling about the tip by $A_{\max }(t)=\max _{M_ t}|A|$ yields the rotational translating bowl with tip $|A|=1$. This last normalization has translation speed $\sqrt n$; rescaling instead by $|\vec H|_{\rm tip}$ gives the speed-one bowl.

Under the intermediate scaling $x\mapsto x/(T-t)^{1/m}$ and $r\mapsto r/(T-t)^{1/2}$, the limiting profile is

\[ r=\sqrt{2(n-1)+Kx^ m},\qquad x\geq -\xi _*. \]

This last expression is an asymptotic limiting profile, not an exact formula for the evolving compact hypersurface. Source: [AV97], Introduction, items 1–5, printed pp. 16–17, supplied PDF pp. 2–3; the construction is justified in Sections 3–7. The even-$m$ modifications and the formal $m=2$ discussion are not included in this entry. The illustration selects $n=2$, $m=3$, $K=1$, and writes $s=T-t$. Its time sequence uses one spatial scale to show the left tip approaching the origin while the outer cap persists; the final cusp target is not a smooth time slice. For the profile near the origin, item 5 of the Introduction gives

\[ \frac{u(y\sqrt{s},T-s)}{\sqrt{s}} =\sqrt{2}+\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2\sqrt{2}}(y^3-6y)+o(\sqrt{s}) \]

on bounded $y$ intervals. This leading Hermite correction explains the small bulge and neck in the cylindrical region. The rotatable compact slices blend the asymptotic profiles with a numerically integrated bowl cap and a chosen outer cap: they are illustrative schematics, not a computed solution of the compact MCF initial-value problem, and their displayed times carry no certified finite-time accuracy. Separate views compare the meridians and the three blow-up limits; the intermediate view uses unequal axial and radial scaling. The construction does not classify all type-II singularities or impose global convexity on every allowed target.
