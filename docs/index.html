What is mean curvature flow?

Mean curvature flow moves a curve, surface, or higher-dimensional submanifold in the direction of its mean curvature vector. For an immersion \(X:M^n\times I\to\mathbb R^{n+1}\), the geometric evolution equation is

\[(\partial_t X)^\perp=\mathbf H.\]

Here \(\mathbf H\) is the mean curvature vector and \(\perp\) denotes projection onto the normal space. With an outward unit normal \(\nu\) and scalar mean curvature \(H=\operatorname{div}_M\nu\), we use \(\mathbf H=-H\nu\). A round sphere therefore shrinks under the flow.

The flow is the gradient flow of area: it decreases area for smooth closed submanifolds, but it can develop singularities. Special solutions provide explicit examples and models for understanding the geometry near singularities and at large scales.

What this stack collects

  • Self-shrinkers: solutions that contract by dilation, normalized by \(\mathbf H+X^\perp/2=0\).
  • Translators: solutions that move with a constant velocity \(V\), satisfying \(\mathbf H=V^\perp\).
  • Self-expanders: solutions that expand by dilation, normalized by \(\mathbf H-X^\perp/2=0\).
  • Other special solutions: ancient and eternal flows, stationary minimal submanifolds, and rotating or more general self-similar solutions.

An ancient solution exists for all times before some finite time; an eternal solution exists for all real times. These time domains do not by themselves imply self-similarity.

Following the idea of the Stacks Project, MCF-stack aims to connect examples, constructions, properties, and classification results through stable tags, cross-references, and precise source attribution. This is a growing reference, with hypotheses and proof status stated explicitly.
