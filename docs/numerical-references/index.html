Numerical references

This page credits the numerical methods, software, and visual sources used to make the figures in MCF-stack. The mathematical existence and classification references remain with each example and in the bibliography. A newly generated mesh or rendering can still owe its visual conception to another author's picture.

Visual sources and individual credits

The credits below identify documented visual influences and parametrization sources. Where a paper credits an individual illustrator, that credit is retained separately from the paper's authorship. Newly generated schematic geometry is not the source author's numerical mesh.

David L. Chopp

Chopp's Computation of Self-Similar Solutions for Mean Curvature Flow, Experimental Mathematics 3 (1994), 1–15 supplies the numerical candidates and visual references for the five Chopp entries: Figures 8–12 (cubical), 13–15 (octahedral), 16–19 (saddle), 20–23 (two-handled cone), and 26–28 (four-handled cone). The latter also appears in Angenent–Ilmanen–Chopp, A Computed Example of Nonuniqueness of Mean Curvature Flow in R3 (1995), p. 1943.

Chopp is also credited for Figure 1 in Ilmanen–White, Fattening in Mean Curvature Flow, which informs our two-ended topology drawing, and Figure 2 in Hoffman–Martín–White, a visual reference for the cubical silhouette. Our corresponding models are schematics, not recomputations of Chopp's numerical surfaces. Source review and drawing provenance.

Tom Ilmanen

The conjectural genus-eight picture credited to Ilmanen in Kapouleas–Kleene–Møller, Mean curvature self-shrinkers of high genus: Non-compact examples, Figure 1, is a visual reference for the sphere–plane configuration. Our KKM drawing uses the paper's Scherk equation and a project-specific drawing map; it is not a solved shrinker or the paper's matched initial surface. Drawing provenance.

O. Michael Melko

Melko's Visualizing Minimal Surfaces (2010), equations (16)–(19), provides the stationary Costa parametrization used for the ancient-Costa illustration. The project evaluates this parametrization and adds a truncated linearized normal mode. Those perturbed frames are not a numerical construction of the nonlinear ancient flow. Implementation and limitations.

Mario B. Schulz

The illustration for the work of Reto Buzano, Huy The Nguyen, and Mario B. Schulz follows the qualitative geometry of Section 4, Figures 3–4 of Noncompact self-shrinkers for mean curvature flow with arbitrary genus, version 2. These are visual references as well as mathematical source material. The authors produced their simulations with Kenneth Brakke's Surface Evolver. Our mesh instead samples an illustrative implicit field. BNS drawing provenance.

Schulz is individually credited for Figure 5, the genus-five numerical picture in Hoffman–Martín–White, Generating Shrinkers by Mean Curvature Flow, version 3. It informs our sphere–plane drawing and its interior view, together with Torralbo's figures below.

Schulz's Geometric Analysis Gallery, especially its self-shrinker collection, is an additional resource for these visualizations. The documented sources used for our drawings are the paper figures identified above; the gallery link does not assert that the website itself was consulted during the original implementation, or that its images or meshes were reused.

Ao Sun: supplied Chu–Sun numerical replay pages

The two supplementary Chu–Sun experiment viewers were supplied by Ao Sun with their numerical-note and archive provenance retained. Both use three.js 0.128.0; the first also uses pako 2.1.0 to decompress stored frames. These are replays of supplied computations, not numerical experiments independently reproduced by the website integration. See the integration and provenance record and the retained three.js and pako license notices. Private notes and solver archives are not exported.

Francisco Torralbo

Torralbo is credited for the numerical genus-one through genus-six illustrations in Hoffman–Martín–White, Figure 1. They inform the qualitative appearance of our sphere–plane model, alongside Schulz's Figure 5. The displayed MCF-stack surface is a topology schematic. Sphere–plane drawing provenance.

Matthias Weber

Weber is credited for Hoffman–Martín–White, Figure 7, a visual reference for our three-ended model. The source describes a conjectural qualitative genus-two picture, not a numerical shrinker approximation. Our separately generated mesh retains that distinction. Three-ended drawing provenance.

Other source-specific drawing references

Other figures use the source paper's profiles or construction diagrams. Examples include Drugan's Figure 1, Møller's Figures 1–2, Nguyen's Figure 1, and the planar-network diagrams reviewed for the expander picture. Their exact roles are recorded in the Drugan, Møller, Nguyen, and network provenance notes. The figure documentation supplies further source locators; this page does not replace the individual example references.

Drawing methods and their scope

  • Formula sampling and projection. Parametrizations are sampled into curves or triangles. Revolving a profile, taking a slice, or projecting from a higher-dimensional ambient space is identified in the caption. The mesh is a finite representation of the formula.
  • Numerical ODE profiles. SciPy integration and, where required, Brent root finding produce profiles with closing or matching conditions. DOP853 is used in several generators. The interactive Abresch–Langer viewer also has a custom period quadrature and bisection implementation. Solver settings and residuals are recorded in the relevant code and audit.
  • Numerical PDE and flow models. Selected generators use finite differences, sparse linear solves, or truncated linearized evolution. Their specific discretizations and limitations accompany the figures. A finite-domain calculation or linearized perturbation is not automatically a computed complete soliton or nonlinear ancient flow.
  • Implicit schematic meshes. Marching cubes extracts a surface from a sampled drawing field; quadric simplification reduces its triangle count. Connectivity, boundary loops, and Euler characteristic are checked where recorded. These checks describe the displayed mesh and do not establish the shrinker equation.
  • Rendering. Custom Python and JavaScript code produces SVG plates and Canvas/WebGL viewers with projection, shading, rotation, zoom, and cutaways. The supplied Chu–Sun replay pages additionally use three.js. Pillow supplies raster panels in selected plates; trimesh supplies selected mesh operations. Cutaway edges and finite display rims are explained in the captions.

The references below credit implemented algorithms and software dependencies; they are not a claim that every listed publication was read during every figure's original creation. Surface Evolver is credited for the source simulations that informed our schematics, rather than as a tool used to generate those MCF-stack meshes.

Methods and software references

Entries are sorted automatically by structured author names, then year and title. Each entry explains its role. Software documentation links were checked on 8 November 2026; this date does not identify the versions used for earlier asset generation.

[BarberDobkinHuhdanpaa1996]

C. Bradford Barber, David P. Dobkin, and Hannu Huhdanpaa. The Quickhull algorithm for convex hulls. ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software, 22(4):469–483, 1996. doi:10.1145/235815.235821.

Method behind Qhull, used through SciPy's Delaunay and ConvexHull routines in selected stacking, doubling, and other triangulated models.

[Brakke1992]

Kenneth A. Brakke. The surface evolver. Experimental Mathematics, 1(2):141–165, 1992. URL: https://www.kenbrakke.com/evolver/, doi:10.1080/10586458.1992.10504253.

Source-side software credit: Buzano, Nguyen, and Schulz used Surface Evolver for their numerical illustrations. MCF-stack's corresponding schematic is generated from its own drawing field; it does not reproduce their Evolver calculation.

[Brent1973]

Richard P. Brent. Algorithms for Minimization Without Derivatives. Prentice-Hall, 1973. URL: https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.brentq.html.

Chapters 3--4 describe the root-finding method used through scipy.optimize.brentq to select closing and matching parameters in shooting constructions. The link documents the implementation and its reference.

[FastSimplification]

fast-simplification contributors. fast-simplification: python wrapper for Fast-Quadric-Mesh-Simplification. Software documentation; accessed 8 November 2026. URL: https://pyvista.github.io/fast-simplification/.

Implementation used to reduce triangle counts for interactive figures. Credit also belongs to the wrapped Fast-Quadric-Mesh-Simplification project by sp4cerat and contributors; its repository is linked in the documentation. This is distinct from using PyVista as a renderer.

[GarlandHeckbert1997]

Michael Garland and Paul S. Heckbert. Surface simplification using quadric error metrics. In Proceedings of SIGGRAPH 1997, 209–216. 1997. URL: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~garland/quadrics/.

Underlying quadric-error idea for mesh simplification. The software actually called by the drawing generators is fast-simplification, sometimes through trimesh.

[HairerNorsettWanner1993]

Ernst Hairer, Syvert P. Nørsett, and Gerhard Wanner. Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I: Nonstiff Problems. Springer, 2 edition, 1993. doi:10.1007/978-3-540-78862-1.

Reference for numerical ODE integration, including the DOP853 method cited by SciPy. Several rotational profiles use solve_ivp with DOP853; other generators explicitly choose different solvers.

[HarrisEtAl2020]

Charles R. Harris, K. Jarrod Millman, Stéfan J. van der Walt, and others. Array programming with NumPy. Nature, 585:357–362, 2020. doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2649-2.

NumPy supplies coordinate arrays, sampling grids, vector operations, normals, and transformations throughout the numerical geometry generators.

[KhronosWebGL]

Khronos WebGL Working Group. WebGL specification, version 1.0. Web standard; accessed 8 November 2026. URL: https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/specs/latest/1.0/.

Browser graphics interface used by the project's custom interactive surface viewers, including depth testing, lighting, and cutaway rendering. This credits the rendering platform rather than a surface construction.

[LewinerEtAl2003]

Thomas Lewiner, Hélio Lopes, Antônio Wilson Vieira, and Geovan Tavares. Efficient implementation of marching cubes' cases with topological guarantees. Journal of Graphics Tools, 8(2):1–15, 2003. URL: https://thomas.lewiner.org/pub/marching_cubes_jgt.html, doi:10.1080/10867651.2003.10487582.

Marching-cubes variant used by skimage.measure.marching_cubes to extract meshes from sampled scalar fields. Its topology guarantees concern the extraction algorithm; they do not certify that an illustrative field solves a geometric PDE.

[Melko2010]

O. Michael Melko. Visualizing minimal surfaces. The Mathematica Journal, 2010. URL: https://www.mathematica-journal.com/2010/12/22/visualizing-minimal-surfaces/.

Equations (16)--(19), particularly (17), supply the stationary Costa parametrization used in the ancient-Costa illustration. MCF-stack implements its evaluation and mesh itself; the displayed normal perturbation is a separate truncated linearized model. No Mathematica package or source image is included.

[Pako]

pako contributors. pako: javascript compression library. Version 2.1.0; software project. URL: https://github.com/nodeca/pako/tree/2.1.0.

Developed by Andrey Tupitsin and Vitaly Puzrin, porting zlib by Jean-loup Gailly and Mark Adler. Used to decompress stored frame data in the first supplied Chu--Sun numerical replay; it is not a geometry solver. The upstream project distinguishes its MIT license from the zlib license of the ported core.

[Pillow]

Pillow contributors. Pillow: python imaging library fork. Software project; accessed 8 November 2026. URL: https://python-pillow.org/.

Used by selected figure generators to draw and encode raster views, including raster panels embedded in otherwise vector SVG plates.

[ThreeJS]

three.js authors. three.js: javascript 3D library. Version 0.128.0 (r128); software project. URL: https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r128.

The two supplementary Chu--Sun numerical replay pages supplied by Ao Sun use this locally hosted library for their surface rendering. Its MIT license is retained beside the bundled library. This dependency is separate from the catalogue's custom Canvas and WebGL viewers.

[Trimesh]

trimesh contributors. trimesh. Software documentation; accessed 8 November 2026. URL: https://trimesh.org/.

Used for mesh processing, normals, simplification, and combinatorial topology checks in the genus-labelled sphere--torus schematics. Such checks concern the exported triangulation, not the shrinker PDE.

[VanDerWaltEtAl2014]

Stéfan van der Walt, Johannes L. Schönberger, Juan Nunez-Iglesias, François Boulogne, Joshua D. Warner, Neil Yager, Emmanuelle Gouillart, Tony Yu, and the scikit-image contributors. scikit-image: image processing in Python. PeerJ, 2:e453, 2014. doi:10.7717/peerj.453.

Software credit for the library providing marching_cubes, in addition to the algorithm credit to Lewiner and coauthors.

[VirtanenEtAl2020]

Pauli Virtanen, Ralf Gommers, Travis E. Oliphant, and others. SciPy 1.0: fundamental algorithms for scientific computing in Python. Nature Methods, 17:261–272, 2020. doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0686-2.

SciPy supplies ODE integration, root finding, interpolation, sparse linear algebra, eigenvalue calculations, special functions, and triangulation for the generators that call those routines. A software citation does not certify a computed solution.

[WHATWGCanvas]

WHATWG. HTML standard: the canvas element. Web standard; accessed 8 November 2026. URL: https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/canvas.html.

Canvas 2D is the browser drawing interface used by the custom curve and surface viewers that project, shade, and draw geometry in JavaScript.

[W3CSVG]

World Wide Web Consortium. Scalable vector graphics (SVG) 2. Web standard; accessed 8 November 2026. URL: https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/.

SVG supplies the static, printable figure format, including labels, paths, triangles, and occasional embedded raster panels.

Code, provenance, and attribution

The figure-generation scripts, viewer code and geometry assets, and figure provenance notes distinguish source formulas from drawing choices and record numerical settings where available. The figure registry connects these assets to the catalogue. Public links point to original publications; supplied PDFs and private research files are not redistributed.

For attribution, retain the named visual source and figure locator, the numerical method or schematic construction, and the implementation credit. “Original” in older captions refers to newly generated geometry or rendering and should not be read as an absence of visual influence. A shared shape or topology does not identify surfaces produced by different mathematical constructions.

The reading site's infrastructure acknowledgments remain in the project README, with the original infrastructure license preserved.
