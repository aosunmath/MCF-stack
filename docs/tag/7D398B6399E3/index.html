Proposition 18.18.2 (Angle, reciprocal angle and their powers)7D398B6399E3

Proposition 18.18.2 (Angle, reciprocal angle and their powers).7D398B6399E3 On any oriented smooth flat-ambient flow with $w=\langle \nu ,e\rangle >0$ and $v=w^{-1}$,

\[ \mathcal Pw=|A|^2w,\qquad \mathcal Pv=-|A|^2v-2v^{-1}|\nabla v|^2. \]

For every real $p$,

\[ \mathcal P(v^ p)=-p|A|^2v^ p-p(p+1)v^{p-2}|\nabla v|^2, \qquad \mathcal P\log v=-|A|^2-v^{-2}|\nabla v|^2. \]

These are pointwise identities wherever the angle is positive, independently of compactness. The reciprocal-angle equation is [EH89], Lemma 3.1.

Proof

Proposition 15.8.3 already proves $\Delta _ M\nu =\nabla H-|A|^2\nu $. Proposition 18.3.2 gives $\partial _ t^\perp \nu =\nabla H$. Subtract and pair with the parallel vector $e$ to obtain the equation for $w$. These local identities also hold on a flat quotient and in dimension one. The scalar chain rule for $\mathcal P$ is $\mathcal P\Phi (w)=\Phi '(w)\mathcal Pw-\Phi ''(w)|\nabla w|^2$. Take $\Phi (w)=w^{-1}$ and use $|\nabla w|^2=v^{-4}|\nabla v|^2$. This proves the equation for $v$. Apply the same rule to $v^ p$; the two gradient coefficients combine to $-2p-p(p-1)=-p(p+1)$. Taking $\Phi (v)=\log v$ gives the last formula directly. No sign restriction on $p$ is needed because $v$ is positive. $\square$
