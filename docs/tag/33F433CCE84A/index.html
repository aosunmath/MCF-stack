Proposition 21.1.2 (The stress tensor and inner variations)33F433CCE84A

Proposition 21.1.2 (The stress tensor and inner variations).33F433CCE84A For a smooth harmonic map $u:\Omega \to S^{N-1}$, set

\[ S_{ij}=\tfrac 12|Du|^2\delta _{ij}-\langle u_ i,u_ j\rangle . \]

Then $\sum _ i\partial _ iS_{ij}=0$. Moreover, for the flow $\Phi _ t$ of a compactly supported domain field $X$, the derivative of the energy of $u\circ \Phi _ t^{-1}$ is $\int S_{ij}\partial _ iX_ j$. In particular every smooth harmonic map is stationary under domain variations. Compare [Hel01], Theorem 3.2; its Hamiltonian tensor is $-S$.

Proof

Differentiating and canceling mixed-derivative terms gives

\[ \sum _ i\partial _ iS_{ij}=-\langle \Delta u,u_ j\rangle =|Du|^2\langle u,u_ j\rangle =0. \]

For the variation, change variables from $\Phi _ t(x)$ to $x$ on a relatively compact domain containing the support. The Jacobian has derivative $\operatorname {div}X$, while $D\Phi _ t^{-1}$ has derivative $-DX$. Differentiating $\frac12|Du(D\Phi _ t)^{-1}|^2\det D\Phi _ t$ gives exactly the claimed integrand. Integrating the divergence-free tensor against $DX$ gives zero by integration by parts. This smooth computation does not justify using $Du\, X$ as an admissible test in a merely weak equation; that is why Definition 21.1.1 retains domain stationarity separately. $\square$
