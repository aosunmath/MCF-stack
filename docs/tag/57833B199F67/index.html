Remark 1.1.3 (The expanded variational reference)57833B199F67

Remark 1.1.3 (The expanded variational reference).57833B199F67 The first variational additions connect Pitts' book [Pitts81] to minimal hypersurfaces and stability, cycle-space min-max theory, the volume spectrum, Morse index, Willmore geometry, CMC hypersurfaces, curvature networks, and Allen–Cahn theory. They draw on the sixteen additional supplied files, with editions and roles recorded in the source inventory. Those first two batches contained twenty-one distinct works or editions in twenty-two supplied files. The later library expansion adds checked passages on compactness, geometric inequalities, multiplicity, abundance, and flow singularities, together with a larger catalogue of sources awaiting detailed study. The History chapter connects these developments to their original problems and to first-person recollections.

The current foundations include BV compactness, current operations and slicing, and the Plateau existence argument in Theorem 9.4.7. The minimal-surface chapters now connect stability to scalar curvature, the two-dimensional Bernstein theorem, and the spectral criterion for cones. The Allen–Cahn branch proves phase compactness, the perimeter lower bound, energy equipartition, and smooth and interior recovery; its full relative Gamma limit retains the boundary-approximation dependency of Theorem 16.4.7. It also develops Modica gradient bounds, diffuse energy monotonicity, geometric stability, sliding minimization, and bounded monotone-solution rigidity in dimensions two and three, with unique continuation identified as an analytic dependency. The CMC chapter distinguishes total-volume constraints from unrestricted area-minus-volume stability. The mean-curvature-flow chapter develops evolution equations, comparison, Gaussian monotonicity, entropy, rescaling, and the Brakke formulation in Definition 18.6.1. The classical obstacle chapter now includes existence, comparison, reaction measures, quadratic blow-ups, and local proofs of the Weiss and Monneau formulas and uniqueness of singular blow-ups. Its optimal regularity, regular–singular dichotomy, and singular stratification retain external proof dependencies. Free-boundary geometry now includes stationarity, boundary spectra, instability, the equatorial disk, and the critical catenoid. Harmonic Gauss maps and Hopf differentials connect CMC theory to sphere rigidity. Thin obstacles, level-set flows, finer singularity theory, free-boundary existence and regularity, and capillarity require further development. A cited theorem without a local proof remains an external dependency; a chapter in the table of contents does not mean that its subject is complete.

The Examples chapter complements the theory with explicit calculations. Proposition 20.1.10 treats a noninteger-dimensional measure model; Example 20.2.2 and its following results distinguish minimality, completeness, and stability. Theorem 20.4.3 constructs the bowl translator, while Theorem 20.5.3 constructs expanding curves. The ancient oval in Theorem 20.6.1 shows that an ancient flow need not be a soliton.
