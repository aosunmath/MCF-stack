Example 20.22.5 (An exact periodic solution converges to two interfaces)E2F3056BDF26

Example 20.22.5 (An exact periodic solution converges to two interfaces).E2F3056BDF26 Fix a circle of length $\ell $. For $0<\varepsilon <\ell /(2\pi )$ choose $a_\varepsilon $ by $T(a_\varepsilon )=\ell /\varepsilon $ and set $u_\varepsilon (x)=f_{a_\varepsilon }(x/\varepsilon )$, normalized by the increasing zero at $x=0$. These are exact critical points, and

\[ u_\varepsilon \longrightarrow \begin{cases} +1,& 0<x<\ell /2,\\ -1,& \ell /2<x<\ell \end{cases} \quad \text{in every finite }L^ p(S^1_\ell ). \]

Their energy measures converge weakly to $c_ W(\delta _0+\delta _{\ell /2})$, and their total energy tends to $2c_ W$.

Proof

The period equation implies $a_\varepsilon \to 1$. For any fixed $b\in (0,1)$, the time for $f_ a$ to rise from zero to $b$ converges, as $a\to 1$, to $\int _0^ b(2W(s))^{-1/2}ds<\infty $. On the positive half-period the profile stays above $b$ except within this uniformly bounded time of either zero, by reflection about its maximum. Multiplication by $\varepsilon $ shows that $u_\varepsilon \to 1$ uniformly on compact subintervals of $(0,\ell /2)$. The symmetry $f_ a(t+T(a)/2)=-f_ a(t)$ handles the negative half. Boundedness by one then gives every finite $L^ p$ convergence.

Change variables $x=\varepsilon t$ in the energy: the total is exactly $\mathcal E(a_\varepsilon )\to 2c_ W$ by Proposition 20.21.5. Around either zero, for every fixed $L$ the rescaled profiles converge smoothly to a heteroclinic on $[-L,L]$. Their energy on the physical interval of radius $\varepsilon L$ therefore tends to $\int _{-L}^ L\psi ^2$. Sending $L\to \infty $ shows that every fixed neighborhood of either zero carries limiting energy at least $c_ W$. Since the total limit is exactly their sum, energy outside any disjoint pair of such neighborhoods tends to zero. For a continuous test function, replace it on each small neighborhood by its value at the center and use uniform continuity. The resulting error tends to zero as the neighborhoods shrink, proving the stated weak convergence. This limit has two perimeter points of multiplicity one, even though each approximating critical point is unstable. $\square$
