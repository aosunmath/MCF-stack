Proposition 7.13.5 (The area identity on the full-rank differentiability set)64CD4DD34AB0

Proposition 7.13.5 (The area identity on the full-rank differentiability set).64CD4DD34AB0 For Lipschitz $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ and a finite-volume measurable set $A$ of full-rank differentiability points,

\[ \int _ AJ_ mDf\, dx =\int _{\mathbb R^ n}\# (A\cap f^{-1}(y))\, d\mathcal H^ m(y). \]

The multiplicity function is measurable and finite almost everywhere. This is the full-rank case of [Sim18], Theorem 3.3.

Proof

For a fixed $\varepsilon $, partition $A$ by Proposition 7.13.3. Each restriction is injective, and each image is measurable by Lemma 7.13.2. Therefore, at every target point,

\[ \# (A\cap f^{-1}(y))=\sum _\ell \chi _{f(E_\ell )}(y). \]

This proves measurability, including the value infinity when infinitely many pieces contribute. Sum Lemma 7.13.4 over the disjoint domain pieces. Monotone convergence identifies the image-measure sum with the multiplicity integral, so the same two comparison factors bound it by $\int _ AJ_ mDf$. This last integral is finite because $J_ mDf\leq (\operatorname {Lip}f)^ m$ almost everywhere and $|A|<\infty $. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ to obtain equality. Finiteness of the integral gives finiteness of the multiplicity for almost every target point. The partitions may depend on $\varepsilon $; the function being integrated does not. $\square$
