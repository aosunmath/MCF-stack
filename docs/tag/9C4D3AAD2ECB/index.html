Example 20.21.6 (All nonconstant phases on a circle)9C4D3AAD2ECB

Example 20.21.6 (All nonconstant phases on a circle).9C4D3AAD2ECB On a circle of length $\ell $, the equation $\varepsilon ^2u''=u^3-u$ has a nonconstant solution with exactly $m$ full oscillations precisely when

\[ m\in \mathbb N,\qquad 2\pi m\varepsilon <\ell . \]

Its amplitude is the unique $a\in (0,1)$ solving $T(a)=\ell /(m\varepsilon )$, and it is unique up to translation and reversal. At equality no nonconstant solution of that oscillation number exists.

Proof

Lift a solution periodically to the line and rescale its independent variable by $\varepsilon $. The bounded classification excludes heteroclinics and leaves exactly the periodic profiles. Their least period in the physical coordinate is $\varepsilon T(a)$, so fitting $m$ complete periods is equivalent to the displayed equation. Proposition 20.21.3 maps $(0,1)$ strictly increasingly onto $(2\pi ,\infty )$, proving existence and uniqueness exactly under the strict inequality. At the endpoint the amplitude would be zero, giving the constant zero rather than a new periodic profile. $\square$
