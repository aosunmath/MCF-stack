Theorem 20.25.1 (A unique bounded-slope graphical expander for a Lipschitz cone)9EC09394BFF2

Theorem 20.25.1 (A unique bounded-slope graphical expander for a Lipschitz cone).9EC09394BFF2 Let $f:\mathbb R^ n\to \mathbb R$, $n\geq 1$, be globally $L$-Lipschitz and positively homogeneous of degree one: $f(\lambda x)=\lambda f(x)$ for every $\lambda >0$. There is a unique smooth entire graph $U$ with bounded slope whose dilation flow $\sqrt t\, U(x/\sqrt t)$ converges locally uniformly to $f$ as $t\downarrow 0$ and solves graphical mean curvature flow. It satisfies

\[ \left(\delta _{ij}-\frac{U_ iU_ j}{1+|DU|^2}\right)U_{ij} =\frac{U-x\cdot DU}{2},\qquad |DU|\leq L, \qquad |U(x)-f(x)|\leq L\sqrt{2n}. \]

Its graph is properly embedded and complete. The existence input is [EH89], Theorem 4.6; the self-similarity and the stated uniqueness follow from the local comparison argument in this project.

Proof

Use Theorem 18.21.7 to obtain a smooth positive-time entire flow $u$ with initial trace $f$ and $|Du|\leq L$. For every $\lambda >0$ the parabolic rescaling

\[ u_\lambda (x,t)=\lambda ^{-1}u(\lambda x,\lambda ^2t) \]

solves the same graph equation, has the same slope bound, and has initial trace $\lambda ^{-1}f(\lambda x)=f(x)$. On each finite common time interval, Theorem 18.24.5 gives $u_\lambda =u$. Taking $U=u(\cdot ,1)$ therefore yields $u(x,t)=\sqrt t\, U(x/\sqrt t)$. Differentiate this identity and substitute into the graph equation to obtain the displayed elliptic equation with the positive expander sign. The slope estimate is inherited from the flow, and Theorem 18.25.3 at time one gives the height estimate.

An entire smooth graph is properly embedded: a compact ambient set bounds the base variable, and the graph is closed. Its induced metric dominates the Euclidean base metric. An intrinsic Cauchy sequence thus converges in the base; smoothness on a compact base neighborhood gives convergence in the graph metric. Hence the graph is complete. Finally, another profile in the stated class has a positive-time dilation flow with bounded slope and the same initial trace. Theorem 18.24.5 identifies the flows, hence their time-one profiles. Existence is not being reconstructed from the elliptic equation alone. $\square$
