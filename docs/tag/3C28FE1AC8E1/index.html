Proposition 15.11.7 (No square-integrable Jacobi field on a complete unduloid)3C28FE1AC8E1

Proposition 15.11.7 (No square-integrable Jacobi field on a complete unduloid).3C28FE1AC8E1 For a noncylindrical complete unduloid with fixed $0<a<1/H$, every smooth Jacobi field $u$ satisfying $\int |u|^2\, dA<\infty $ is zero. This conclusion does not assert that the Jacobi operator has closed range on an unweighted function space.

Proof

In the conformal coordinates of Proposition 15.11.5, $dA=r^2dt\, d\theta $ and $a\le r\le b$. Each angular Fourier coefficient $v_ k$ is therefore in $L^2(\mathbb R,dt)$ by Cauchy–Schwarz on the angular circle. For $|k|\ge 2$, it satisfies $v_ k''=q_ kv_ k$ with $q_ k=k^2-V\ge \delta >0$. Apply the following calculation to its real and imaginary parts. Choose $\eta _ T$ equal to one on $[-T,T]$, supported in $[-2T,2T]$, with $|\eta _ T'|\le C/T$. Integration by parts against $\eta _ T^2v_ k$ and Young's inequality give

\[ \frac12\int \eta _ T^2|v_ k'|^2+\int q_ k\eta _ T^2|v_ k|^2 \le 2\int |\eta _ T'|^2|v_ k|^2\le \frac{2C^2}{T^2}\| v_ k\| _2^2. \]

Letting $T\to \infty $ forces $v_ k=0$ on every bounded interval.

For the remaining frequencies, the explicit bases in Proposition 15.11.2 apply. In a first angular sector, the rotational coefficient grows at least linearly at either end, while $z'$ is periodic and bounded below positively. No nonzero combination is square-integrable. In the zero sector, put $v=-r'$. The neck field obeys $f_ a(s+jP)=f_ a(s)+jL_ a'v(s)$. Choose a subinterval of one period on which $|v|$ is bounded below positively. A nonzero coefficient of $f_ a$ then makes the squared integral over its translates grow quadratically in $|j|$; a nonzero multiple of $v$ alone has the same positive squared integral on every period. Neither is square-integrable. The changes between $s$, $t$, and area measure have uniformly bounded positive weights for this fixed unduloid. Fourier uniqueness now gives $u=0$. This proof concerns the kernel only; range and weighted mapping properties require additional analysis. $\square$
