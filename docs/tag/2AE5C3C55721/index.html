Example 14.6.4 (A cylindrical connector has a larger leading cost)2AE5C3C55721

Example 14.6.4 (A cylindrical connector has a larger leading cost).2AE5C3C55721 For $0<h<R$, remove a disk of radius $\rho \in [0,R]$ from each of the planar radius-$R$ disks at heights $\pm h$, and join the inner boundary circles by a cylinder of radius $\rho $. The resulting piecewise smooth comparison set has area

\[ 2\pi R^2+4\pi h\rho -2\pi \rho ^2. \]

The maximum along this explicit radius family is $2\pi R^2+2\pi h^2$, attained at $\rho =h$. For fixed $R$ and $h\downarrow 0$, this excess is larger by an asymptotic factor $\log (R/h)$ than the thin catenoid's excess.

Proof

The two annuli contribute $2\pi (R^2-\rho ^2)$ and the cylinder, of height $2h$, contributes $4\pi h\rho $. The excess is $2\pi h^2-2\pi (\rho -h)^2$, which has the stated maximum because $h$ lies in the allowed interval. The ratio assertion follows from Theorem 14.6.3. At the joins this comparison set has corners; the displayed calculation alone neither supplies a smooth sweepout with controlled smoothing errors nor identifies a variational width. Those additional requirements belong to the deformation theorem, not this elementary area comparison. $\square$
