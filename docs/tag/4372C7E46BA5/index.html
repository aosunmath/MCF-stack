Theorem 16.9.4 (Bounded solutions of the flat linearized equation)4372C7E46BA5

Theorem 16.9.4 (Bounded solutions of the flat linearized equation).4372C7E46BA5 Every bounded $C^2$ solution of

\[ \Delta w-W''(q(x_{d+1}))w=0 \quad \text{on }\mathbb R^{d+1} \]

is $w(y,t)=a\psi (t)$ for one constant $a$. Together with Proposition 16.8.2, this holds in every ambient dimension at least one. This is the kernel assertion in [ChAC], Appendix B, Problem 2, with its normal decay and orthogonality steps made explicit.

Proof

Take the orthogonal remainder $v$ from Proposition 16.9.3 and define $G(y)=\int _{\mathbb R}v(y,t)^2dt$. It is nonnegative, bounded and twice continuously differentiable by Lemma 16.9.2. Differentiation, the equation and integration by parts give the exact identity

\[ \Delta _ yG=2\int |D_ yv|^2dt+ 2\int \bigl (|v_ t|^2+V(t)v^2\bigr )dt. \]

Each normal slice lies in $H^1$ and is orthogonal to $\psi $. Theorem 16.8.4 therefore implies

\[ \Delta _ yG\geq 2\int |D_ yv|^2dt+3G\geq 3G. \]

Lemma 16.9.1 forces $G=0$. Continuity then gives $v=0$, proving the claim. The displayed gradient term is tangential; the inequality is derived from the exact slice energy rather than by discarding the potentially negative normal potential. $\square$
