Example 5.2.1 (A collapsing Lagrangian immersed sphere)245DFF2BED27

Example 5.2.1 (A collapsing Lagrangian immersed sphere).245DFF2BED27 For $n\geq 2$, the initial Whitney immersion in $\mathbb R^{2n}=\mathbb C^ n$ is explicitly

\[ F_0(x_1,\ldots ,x_{n+1}) =\frac{(x_1,x_1x_{n+1},\ldots ,x_ n,x_ nx_{n+1})}{1+x_{n+1}^2}, \qquad (x_1,\ldots ,x_{n+1})\in S^ n. \]

It is a compact Lagrangian immersion, of dimension and codimension $n$, with one transverse double image point at the origin coming from the two poles. It is complete and proper as an immersion of a compact domain, and it is not embedded. Its equivariance reduces the geometry to the planar initial profile

\[ \gamma _0(u)=\left(\frac{\sin u}{1+\cos ^2u}, \frac{\sin u\cos u}{1+\cos ^2u}\right), \qquad u\in \mathbb R/2\pi \mathbb Z. \]

For $n\geq 2$ the profile evolution is the equivariant equation

\[ (\partial _ t\gamma )^\perp =\vec k-(n-1)\frac{\gamma ^\perp }{|\gamma |^2}, \]

interpreted by the smooth equivariant immersion at the double point. It is not ordinary planar curve shortening.

The maximal smooth mean curvature flow $F_ t$, $t\in [0,T)$, remains Lagrangian and equivariant. It collapses to the origin in finite time, and its tangent flow there is a Lagrangian plane with multiplicity two. The Whitney sphere is not a self-similar solution. These statements are the special case stated in [CatViana18], pp. 1–3, especially the result on p. 2 and Theorem 1.3 on p. 3. The formulas above specify the initial geometry and its reduced equation, not a closed expression for $F_ t$ at positive time.
