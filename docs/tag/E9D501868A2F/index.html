Theorem 6.6.5 (Differentiation of functions relative to a Euclidean Radon measure)E9D501868A2F

Theorem 6.6.5 (Differentiation of functions relative to a Euclidean Radon measure).E9D501868A2F Let $\lambda $ be a positive Radon measure on an open set $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, and let $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\lambda )$. For $\lambda $-almost every $x\in U$,

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\lambda (B_ r(x))} \int _{B_ r(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|\, d\lambda (y)=0, \qquad \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\lambda (B_ r(x))} \int _{B_ r(x)}f\, d\lambda =f(x). \]

The denominators are positive at almost every point for all sufficiently small radii. The average conclusion applies componentwise to a finite-dimensional vector-valued function. This is [Sim18], Chapter 1, Corollary 4.10, pp. 23–24, with the Euclidean covering input isolated in Theorem 6.6.1.

Proof

First assume $U=\mathbb R^ n$ and $f\in L^1(\lambda )$. Theorem 6.6.4 gives the closed-ball conclusion. At a point where it holds, fix a sufficiently small open-ball radius $r$ and take radii $s_ k\uparrow r$. Their closed balls increase to $B_ r(x)$. Continuity from below for the denominator and the nonnegative oscillation integral shows that every uniform bound for closed-ball averages at radii below a given scale also holds for open balls below that scale. Therefore the open-ball absolute oscillation tends to zero. The absolute value of the difference between the average of $f$ and $f(x)$ is bounded by that oscillation.

For the local statement, exhaust $U$ by relatively compact open sets $V_ j$ and choose larger relatively compact open sets $W_ j$ containing their closures. Restrict $\lambda $ to $W_ j$ and extend this finite Borel measure by zero to $\mathbb R^ n$; it is a Radon measure there. Restrict $f$ to $W_ j$ and set it to zero elsewhere. Local integrability makes this a globally integrable function for the restricted measure. The whole-space result applies. At each point of $V_ j$, all sufficiently small balls lie in $W_ j$, so both integrals and denominators agree with the original ones. A countable union of exceptional null sets finishes the proof. Intersect the finitely many componentwise full-measure sets for a vector-valued function. The unproved geometric input remains Besicovitch selection, not the subsequent analytic differentiation argument. $\square$
