Example 4.6.1 (Regular expanding trees with prescribed rays)16898F334139

Example 4.6.1 (Regular expanding trees with prescribed rays).16898F334139 A regular planar network consists of smooth branches meeting only at triple junctions, with angles $120$ degrees at every junction. A self-expanding network $\Sigma \subset \mathbb R^2$ satisfies

\[ \vec k=\frac{X^\perp }{2} \]

on each open branch; its time evolution is $\Sigma _ t=\sqrt t\, \Sigma $, $t>0$, with the same angle condition. It is a network flow and is not a smooth immersed curve at its junctions. For finitely many distinct prescribed rays from the origin, connected tree-like expanders provide smoothings in this network sense. Existence for three rays, including uniqueness of the tree, and existence for more rays are recalled in [CatINS19], Section 7, printed p. 70, supplied PDF p. 32, with attribution there to Schnürer–Schulze and Mazzeo–Sáez respectively.

The branches can be described as geodesics for the conformal metric

\[ g=e^{|X|^2/2}(dX_1^2+dX_2^2), \]

with the junction balance condition. Noncompact ends approach their assigned rays with Gaussian decay. Two regular expanders with the same asymptotic rays coincide if they are in the same topological class: the comparison deformation must retain distinct junctions and the prescribed asymptotic ends, as in the source definition. This does not assert uniqueness across different network topologies. See [CatINS19], Lemmas 4.1 and 4.3 and Theorem 4.4, printed pp. 49–51, supplied PDF pp. 11–13.

The source uses $\vec k=\psi ^\perp $ and $\sqrt{2t}\, \psi $; our profile is $\Sigma =\sqrt2\, \psi $. Nontrivial networks arise from a geodesic boundary-value construction. The exact balanced special case

\[ \bigcup _{j=0}^2\{ r(\cos (2\pi j/3),\sin (2\pi j/3)):r\geq 0\} \]

is the stationary $Y$ network. It illustrates the junction condition. The accompanying general tree diagram is schematic; its branches are not numerically solved.
