Lemma 20.4.1 (Resolving the radial equation at zero)DD2B8469A5C0

Lemma 20.4.1 (Resolving the radial equation at zero).DD2B8469A5C0 There is $R>0$ and a unique smooth radial solution $u(x)=f(|x|)$ on $B_ R\subset \mathbb R^ n$ of the unit-speed translator equation with $f(0)=0$. Its derivative has the form

\[ p(r)=f'(r)=rq(r^2),\qquad q(0)=\frac1n, \]

where $q$ is real analytic near zero. In particular the apparent $1/r$ coefficient in the radial equation creates no singularity in the graph at its tip.

Proof

Put $m=n-1$ and $P(q)=q^2(1-mq)$. Multiplying $p'+mp/r=1+p^2-mp^3/r$ by $r^{n-1}$ and integrating from zero gives, after setting $p=rq(r^2)$,

\[ q(z)=\frac1n+z\int _0^1t^{n+1}P(q(zt^2))\, dt. \]

To construct a smooth solution without assuming it exists, use the Banach space of functions continuous on a small closed complex disk $|z|\leq \delta $ and holomorphic in its interior, with the supremum norm. On the closed ball $\| q-1/n\| _\infty \leq 1$, the polynomial $P$ is bounded by some $M_ n$ and is Lipschitz with some $L_ n$. The right side of the displayed integral equation maps this ball into itself when $\delta M_ n/(n+2)\leq 1$ and is a contraction when $\delta L_ n/(n+2)<1$. Its unique fixed point is holomorphic and real on the real axis, since iteration starting from $1/n$ preserves complex conjugation. Define

\[ f(r)=\frac12\int _0^{r^2}q(s)\, ds. \]

This is analytic as a function of $|x|^2$ and satisfies the ODE away from zero by differentiation of the integral identity. At zero its Hessian is $I/n$, so the graph equation holds there as well. Conversely any smooth radial solution has $p/r\to 1/n$ by Proposition 20.3.9, and it obeys the same integral equation on the real interval. The same contraction proof in the space of continuous real functions gives local uniqueness without assuming analyticity of the competitor. The only external analytic tools in this local construction are the contraction principle and uniform-limit closure of holomorphic functions. $\square$
