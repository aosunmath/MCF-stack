Proposition 14.5.2 (A corrected area ratio is monotone)06FDAA574FA5

Proposition 14.5.2 (A corrected area ratio is monotone).06FDAA574FA5 For $s>0$ define

\[ \Phi _ y(s)=\frac{A(s)}{s^2}+\frac1{2s^2}\int _{M_ s}X\cdot \vec H\, d\mu +\frac14\mathcal W(M_ s). \]

Then $\Phi _ y$ is nondecreasing and

\[ \Phi _ y(s)=\pi m+\int _{M_ s\setminus F^{-1}(y)} \left|\frac{\vec H}4+\frac{X^\perp }{r^2}\right|^2d\mu , \qquad m=\# F^{-1}(y). \]

In particular $\Phi _ y(0+)=\pi m$ and $\Phi _ y(+\infty )=\mathcal W(F)/4$. The uncorrected area ratio need not be monotone for a nonminimal surface.

Proof

Move the inner curvature-radius term in Proposition 14.5.1 to the left and add $\mathcal W(M_\sigma )/4$ to both sides. The result is $\Phi _ y(\rho )-\Phi _ y(\sigma )$ equal to the nonnegative square integral over the annulus. The graph calculation of Lemma 14.4.2 gives $A(\sigma )/\sigma ^2\to \pi m$. It also gives $X^\perp =O(r^2)$ near each preimage, whence $\sigma ^{-2}\int _{M_\sigma }X\cdot \vec H=O(\sigma ^2)$; the inner energy tends to zero. If the pole misses the image, all three terms vanish for small $\sigma $. Letting $\sigma \downarrow 0$ proves the formula for regular $s$.

For any other $s>0$, choose regular values increasing to $s$. The corresponding open sublevel sets increase to $M_ s$. Monotone convergence for the nonnegative terms, and dominated convergence for the bounded signed curvature term, yield the same formula. It makes monotonicity immediate at every radius. Finally compactness of $M$ makes both radius-denominator terms tend to zero at infinity, while the energy tends to its full value. The correction terms involving mean curvature are essential in this computation. $\square$
