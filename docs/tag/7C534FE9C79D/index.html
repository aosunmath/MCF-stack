Proposition 18.24.1 (Bounded slope gives automatic positive-time curvature control)7C534FE9C79D

Proposition 18.24.1 (Bounded slope gives automatic positive-time curvature control).7C534FE9C79D Let $u:\mathbb R^ n\times (0,T]\to \mathbb R$ be a smooth graphical flow with $v\leq V<\infty $ on the whole slab. Then

\[ |A(x,t)|^2\leq C(n)V^4/t. \]

In fact its sharp slope-based estimate is

\[ (1+2t|A(x,t)|^2)v(x,t)^2\leq V^2. \]

No curvature or spatial growth bound is assumed in either assertion. In particular they apply with $V=V_0$ to a smooth flow up to time zero with bounded initial slope, by Corollary 18.23.3.

Proof

Fix $0<\epsilon <t$. Apply Theorem 18.23.5 on a base ball centered at the chosen point and on the time interval $[\epsilon ,t]$, using time elapsed from $\epsilon $. At its center the estimate is $a(x,t)\leq C(n)V^4((t-\epsilon )^{-1}+R^{-2})$. The graph is smooth on every such compact ball. Let $R\to \infty $ and then $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. This proves the first estimate, and hence uniform curvature control on every closed positive-time slab.

Theorem 18.21.4 now applies on $[\epsilon ,t]$: its formerly assumed slab curvature bound has just been proved. Its starting slope supremum is at most $V$. Therefore $(1+2(t-\epsilon )a(x,t))v(x,t)^2\leq V^2$. Let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ for the sharp form. No convergence of the curvature at time zero is needed. This is the local-to-global procedure discussed in [EH91], Remark 3.3(i), followed by the exact time-weighted identity already in the project. $\square$
