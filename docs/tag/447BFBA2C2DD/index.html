Proposition 14.4.5 (The energy lost at an inversion pole)447BFBA2C2DD

Proposition 14.4.5 (The energy lost at an inversion pole).447BFBA2C2DD Under the closed-surface hypotheses above, inversion at $y$ gives an immersion of $M\setminus F^{-1}(y)$ with finite Willmore energy and

\[ \mathcal W(I_ y\circ F)=\mathcal W(F)-4\pi m, \qquad m=\# F^{-1}(y). \]

Thus energy is unchanged if the pole misses the image; if it lies on $m$ smooth sheets, inversion subtracts $4\pi $ per sheet. In the equality case $\mathcal W(F)=4\pi m$, the punctured inverted immersion is minimal.

Proof

Lemma 14.4.1 identifies the inverted energy with four times the square integral in Theorem 14.4.3. With no boundary that integral is $\mathcal W(F)/4-\pi m$, proving the formula and finiteness. If it vanishes, the transformed mean curvature has zero squared integral. It is smooth on the punctured domain and must vanish everywhere there. No assertion about a smooth compactification at infinity or a classification of that minimal surface is needed. $\square$
