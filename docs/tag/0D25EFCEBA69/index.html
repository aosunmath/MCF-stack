Proposition 16.8.5 (All square-integrable eigenfunctions of the normal operator)0D25EFCEBA69

Proposition 16.8.5 (All square-integrable eigenfunctions of the normal operator).0D25EFCEBA69 If a nonzero real $f\in H^2(\mathbb R)$ satisfies $\mathscr Lf=\lambda f$, then either $\lambda =0$ and $f$ is a multiple of $\psi $, or $\lambda =3/2$ and $f$ is a multiple of $\zeta $. This classifies square-integrable eigenfunctions; it is not a statement about the full continuous spectrum.

Proof

The quadratic identity gives $\lambda \geq 0$. At zero the kernel was identified in Lemma 16.8.1. Suppose $\lambda >0$ and set $g=\mathcal A f$. It is nonzero, and intertwining yields $(\mathcal A\mathcal A^*)g=\lambda g$. Set $h=\mathcal B g$. The second factorization gives

\[ -h''+2h=\lambda h. \]

These operations are justified in $L^2$: the eigen-equation and bounded derivatives of $V$ imply successively that all derivatives of $f$ lie in $L^2$, starting from $f\in H^2$ and differentiating $f''=(V-\lambda )f$.

No nonzero solution of the displayed constant-coefficient equation belongs to $L^2(\mathbb R)$. For $\lambda <2$ its solutions are linear combinations of opposite exponentials, for $\lambda =2$ affine functions, and for $\lambda >2$ sines and cosines. Each nonzero combination fails square integrability at one or both ends. Hence $h=0$, so $g=c\chi $ and the nonzero equation for $g$ forces $\lambda =3/2$. Finally $\lambda f=\mathcal A^*g$ gives the stated eigenfunction. The converse eigen-equations have already been checked. $\square$
