Corollary 14.4.9 (From the embedded theorem to all orientable positive-genus immersions)9065CAA9C92F

Corollary 14.4.9 (From the embedded theorem to all orientable positive-genus immersions).9065CAA9C92F Let $F:M^2\to \mathbb R^3$ be a smooth closed connected immersion of an orientable surface of genus at least one. Then $\mathcal W(F)\geq 2\pi ^2$. Equality requires embeddedness and, after inverse stereographic projection, a conformal image of the Clifford torus. The same statement holds for immersions in $S^3$ with energy $\mathcal W_1$. This deduction uses the external Marques–Neves theorem 14.1.2, as explained in [MNWill], p. 685.

Proof

If an immersion is not injective, Theorem 14.4.4, or Corollary 14.4.7 in the sphere, gives energy at least $8\pi >2\pi ^2$. Otherwise compactness makes it an embedding. A Euclidean embedding becomes a spherical embedding by inverse stereographic projection, with the same energy by Proposition 14.3.5. Apply Theorem 14.1.2 and its equality classification. Conversely the Clifford torus and its conformal images have that energy by the explicit calculation and conformal invariance. The deep embedded theorem is an input to this argument, not proved by the multiplicity bound. $\square$
