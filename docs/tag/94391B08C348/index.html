Example 9.6.4 (The complex cusp as an integral minimizer)94391B08C348

Example 9.6.4 (The complex cusp as an integral minimizer).94391B08C348 For $D=\{ z\in \mathbb C:|z|<1\} $ and $F(z)=(z^2,z^3)$, the current $T=F_\# \llbracket D\rrbracket $ is calibrated and mass minimizing with its prescribed outer boundary. The map is injective, is an immersion away from zero, and has area Jacobian

\[ J_2DF(z)=4|z|^2+9|z|^4. \]

Its image satisfies $w^2=v^3$ in coordinates $(v,w)\in \mathbb C^2$. The origin is not asserted to be a smooth complex submanifold point.

Proof

If two nonzero parameters have the same image, their ratio has both square and cube equal to one, so the parameters agree. Zero has only the zero preimage. The derivative $(2z,3z^2)$ vanishes exactly at zero. Its squared norm gives the displayed Jacobian by Proposition 9.6.2; Proposition 9.6.3 supplies integrality and minimization. The boundary is the smooth embedded outer curve $F(\partial D)$, since the derivative is nonzero there. The algebraic equation follows by substitution. Compare the cusp example of [Minter], p. 20; the parameter convention here interchanges the source's two coordinates. $\square$
