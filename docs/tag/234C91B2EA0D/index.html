Theorem 11.16.5 (Subcritical volume growth forces flatness)234C91B2EA0D

Theorem 11.16.5 (Subcritical volume growth forces flatness).234C91B2EA0D In the setting of Corollary 11.16.4, suppose for some $p\in [4,4+\sqrt{8/n})$ there is a sequence $R_ j\to \infty $ with

\[ R_ j^{-p}\operatorname {Vol}(\{ r<R_ j\} )\longrightarrow 0. \]

Then $A=0$ everywhere. If the immersion is complete and connected, its image is an affine hyperplane. This is the Euclidean growth implication in [SSY75], Theorem 2, p. 283; the stated sequence assumption suffices for the proof.

Proof

Use $\theta =1/2$ in Corollary 11.16.4. Every fixed compact subset lies in $\{ r<R_ j/2\} $ for all sufficiently large $j$. Its integral of $u^ p$ is therefore bounded by a quantity tending to zero. Continuity gives $u=0$ at every point. The chosen normal has zero derivative and hence is constant on a connected parameter manifold, so the image lies in an affine hyperplane. To see that it fills that hyperplane under completeness, start at one point and take a geodesic with any prescribed tangent vector. Its ambient acceleration is zero because both its intrinsic acceleration and the second fundamental form vanish. Completeness extends the geodesic to every time, giving every point on the corresponding straight line. The tangent space already equals the hyperplane's tangent space, so these lines cover the plane. $\square$
