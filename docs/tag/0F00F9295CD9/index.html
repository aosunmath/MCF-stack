Example 3.6.1 (Nonrotational entire convex translators)0F00F9295CD9

Example 3.6.1 (Nonrotational entire convex translators).0F00F9295CD9 In every dimension $n\geq 3$, Xu-Jia Wang constructs entire convex solutions $u:\mathbb R^ n\to \mathbb R$ of the unit-speed upward translator equation

\[ \left(\delta _{ij}-\frac{u_ i u_ j}{1+|Du|^2}\right)u_{ij}=1 \]

whose level sets are not spheres or round cylinders in any orthogonal coordinate system. This is an existence construction, not an explicit formula.

  • The graphs are embedded, complete, proper and diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^ n$; the last three facts also follow directly from being smooth entire graphs. Their flows exist for all $t\in \mathbb R$ by translation.

  • They are convex and break the spherical or cylindrical rotational pattern of the bowl and its Euclidean products. The corresponding statement for complete entire graphs in $\mathbb R^3$ is false: there the bowl is the only example.

  • Under spatial blow-down $u_ h(x)=h^{-1}u(\sqrt h\, x)$, Wang's theorem gives, after rotations depending on $h$, a quadratic limit of the form

    \[ \frac{x_1^2+\cdots +x_ k^2}{2(k-1)},\qquad 2\leq k\leq n. \]

    The theorem does not rule out slow rotation of the axes as $h\to \infty $. This is an asymptotic limit, not a formula for $u$.

  • The same paper also constructs nonrotational complete convex graphical translators over slab regions. No uniqueness theorem for the general higher-dimensional families is claimed here.

See [CatXJWang11], Theorems 1.2–1.3, printed pages 1187–1188 (PDF pages 3–4), using the case $\sigma =1$ of equation (1.2); the $\sigma =0$ case describes a different, level-set formulation. Any three-dimensional drawing of these higher-dimensional examples is only a projected or sectional schematic.
