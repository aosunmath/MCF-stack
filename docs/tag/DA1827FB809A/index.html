Theorem 8.1.5 (De Giorgi structure and Gauss–Green)DA1827FB809A

Theorem 8.1.5 (De Giorgi structure and Gauss–Green).DA1827FB809A Let $n\geq 2$ and let $E$ have locally finite perimeter in $U\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$. Then $\partial ^*E\cap U$ is countably $(n-1)$-rectifiable and

\[ |D\chi _ E|=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner (\partial ^*E\cap U), \qquad D\chi _ E=-\nu _ E\, \mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner (\partial ^*E\cap U). \]

Consequently, for every $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb {R}^ n)$,

\[ \int _ E\operatorname {div}X\, d\mathcal{L}^ n =\int _{\partial ^*E\cap U}X\cdot \nu _ E\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}. \]

Here $X$ is extended by zero outside $U$. The boundary measures in these identities are taken inside $U$.

The proof below separates blow-up, graph covering, and measure identification. See [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 4.4, pp. 97–100. The Gauss–Green identity follows immediately from the measure representation and Definition 8.1.1. The historical account in [Fle16], Section 3, uses the same outward-normal convention as this entry.

Proof

Theorem 8.13.6 proves countable rectifiability from the full pointwise blow-up and density estimates. Theorem 8.14.5 identifies the variation measure with Hausdorff measure on the reduced boundary. Its proof explicitly uses Theorem 6.6.5, the abstract Radon–Nikodym theorem, and Corollary 7.6.7. The remaining external inputs in these chains include Besicovitch selection, abstract Radon–Nikodym representation, Whitney extension and the Euclidean area formula; the rectifiable density step is now developed locally.

To identify the vector derivative, write $D\chi _ E=-\nu \, |D\chi _ E|$ using its measurable unit polar field. The differentiation argument in Corollary 8.14.1 gives $\nu =\nu _ E$ at almost every point for the perimeter measure. Substituting the scalar measure representation proves the second displayed identity. Finally Definition 8.1.1 yields $\int _ E\operatorname {div}X=-\int X\cdot dD\chi _ E$, which is the stated Gauss–Green formula with outward normal. This conclusion depends on the measurable set modulo null sets; no regularity of its chosen topological boundary is required. $\square$
