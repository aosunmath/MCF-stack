Lemma 20.4.2 (Linear barriers prevent finite-radius blow-up)79D2E1EDDD98

Lemma 20.4.2 (Linear barriers prevent finite-radius blow-up).79D2E1EDDD98 The tip solution extends to all $r\geq 0$ and satisfies

\[ \frac rn<p(r)<\frac r{n-1},\qquad p'(r)>0\quad (r>0). \]

Consequently its height obeys

\[ \frac{r^2}{2n}<f(r)<\frac{r^2}{2(n-1)}\quad (r>0). \]
Proof

The tip expansion in Proposition 20.3.9 gives $p=r/n+r^3/(n^3(n+2))+o(r^3)$, so the strict barriers hold initially. At a contact with $p=r/n$, the ODE gives $p'-1/n=r^2/n^3>0$, preventing a first downward crossing. At a contact with $p=r/(n-1)$, it gives $p'-1/(n-1)=-1/(n-1)<0$, preventing a first upward crossing. Thus the two barriers persist for the whole interval of existence. Between them the factor $1-(n-1)p/r$ is positive, so $p'>0$. If a finite positive endpoint of existence existed, the barriers would bound $p$ there, and the smooth ODE away from $r=0$ would continue the solution across that endpoint. This contradiction proves global extension. Integrating the strict bounds from zero gives the height bounds. Compare the continuation and barrier argument in [CSS07], proof of Lemma 2.1. $\square$
