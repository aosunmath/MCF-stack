Proposition 14.3.5 (Stereographic projection preserves the normalized energy)72FC663C3366

Proposition 14.3.5 (Stereographic projection preserves the normalized energy).72FC663C3366 Let $F:M^2\to S^ N\setminus \{ p\} $ be a smooth closed immersion in the unit sphere, and let $\pi _ p$ be stereographic projection. Then

\[ \mathcal W_0(\pi _ p\circ F)=\mathcal W_1(F) =\int _ M\left(1+\tfrac 14|\vec H_{S^ N}|^2\right)d\mu . \]

In particular, the Euclidean energy of the projection of a closed minimal surface in the sphere equals its spherical area. The identity is equation (1) of [MNWill], p. 684, in trace-vector notation.

Proof

Take $p=(0,\ldots ,0,1)$. The inverse projection is

\[ Y(x)=\left(\frac{2x}{1+|x|^2},\frac{|x|^2-1}{1+|x|^2}\right). \]

A direct derivative gives, for every $v$,

\[ |dY_ x(v)|^2=\frac{4}{(1+|x|^2)^2}|v|^2. \]

For example, the first component has derivative $2v/(1+r^2)-4x(x\cdot v)/(1+r^2)^2$ and the last has derivative $4(x\cdot v)/(1+r^2)^2$; their squared cross terms cancel. Thus projection is conformal. Compactness and avoidance of $p$ ensure a smooth conformal factor near the entire surface, so Proposition 14.3.4 applies with ambient curvatures one and zero. Minimality removes the mean-curvature term in the spherical integral. $\square$
