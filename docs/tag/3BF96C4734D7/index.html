Theorem 16.8.6 (A normal inverse after fixing translation)3BF96C4734D7

Theorem 16.8.6 (A normal inverse after fixing translation).3BF96C4734D7 For every $F\in L^2(\mathbb R)$ with $\int F\psi =0$, there is a unique $u\in H^2(\mathbb R)$ satisfying

\[ \mathscr Lu=F,\qquad \int u\psi =0. \]

With $\| u\| _{H^2}^2=\| u\| _2^2+\| u'\| _2^2+\| u''\| _2^2$, one has

\[ \| u\| _2\leq \frac23\| F\| _2,\qquad \| u\| _{H^2}\leq \sqrt7\| F\| _2. \]

Orthogonality of $F$ is necessary for any $H^2$ solution. This supplies an elementary normal-direction solvability statement for the linearized problem discussed in [ChAC], Problems 2 and 3; it is not a uniform inverse on a curved or noncompact interface.

Proof

On the closed subspace $\mathcal H=\{ u\in H^1:\int u\psi =0\} $, the gap and $V\geq -1$ give

\[ \| u\| _2^2\leq \frac23Q(u),\qquad \| u'\| _2^2\leq Q(u)+\| u\| _2^2\leq \frac53Q(u). \]

Thus $Q$ is an inner product with norm equivalent to the complete $H^1$ norm on this subspace. Hilbert representation, Lemma 9.7.1, applied to $v\mapsto \int Fv$, gives a unique $u\in \mathcal H$ with $Q(u,v)=\int Fv$ for every $v\in \mathcal H$. Both sides vanish on $\psi $, so decomposing a general $H^1$ test into its orthogonal part and its multiple of $\psi $ proves the equation weakly against all tests. Since $u''=Vu-F\in L^2$, $u\in H^2$.

Testing with $u$ gives $Q(u)\leq \| F\| _2\| u\| _2$ and hence the first estimate. It also gives $Q(u)\leq (2/3)\| F\| _2^2$, so $\| u'\| _2^2\leq (10/9)\| F\| _2^2$. Since $|V|\leq 2$, $\| u''\| _2\leq 2\| u\| _2+\| F\| _2\leq (7/3)\| F\| _2$. Adding the three squared estimates gives the constant seven. Uniqueness follows from the gap. Finally any $H^2$ solution paired with $\psi $ has $\int F\psi =Q(u,\psi )=0$, proving necessity. $\square$
