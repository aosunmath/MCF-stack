Theorem 9.13.2 (Analytic pushforward of a compact normal current)47FF81F5740B

Theorem 9.13.2 (Analytic pushforward of a compact normal current).47FF81F5740B Let $T$ be a compactly supported normal $m$-current in $\mathbb R^ N$, $m\geq 0$, and let $f:\mathbb R^ N\to \mathbb R^ Q$ be $L$-Lipschitz. For every smooth compactly supported $m$-form $\omega $, the limit

\[ f_\# T(\omega )=\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}(f_\varepsilon )_\# T(\omega ) \]

exists and defines a compactly supported normal current. It satisfies

\[ \operatorname {spt}(f_\# T)\subset f(\operatorname {spt}T), \quad \mathbf M(f_\# T)\leq L^ m\mathbf M(T), \quad \partial (f_\# T)=f_\# (\partial T). \]

For $m\geq 1$ its boundary mass is at most $L^{m-1}\mathbf M(\partial T)$. Use $L^0=1$, including $L=0$; a zero-current has zero boundary. This is the compact-support form of [Sim18], Lemma 2.36.

Proof

In dimension zero, Theorem 9.3.2 identifies $T$ with a finite signed measure. Smooth pushforward tests it against $\omega \circ f_\varepsilon $, which converges uniformly on its compact support to $\omega \circ f$. This gives the usual measure pushforward, its mass bound and support assertion. Its boundary is zero.

For $m\geq 1$, put $K=\operatorname {spt}T$. Proposition 9.3.17 applied to $f_\varepsilon ,f_\delta $ gives

\[ \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}((f_\varepsilon )_\# T-(f_\delta )_\# T) \leq \sup _ K|f_\varepsilon -f_\delta | \bigl (L^ m\mathbf M(T)+L^{m-1}\mathbf M(\partial T)\bigr ). \]

The right side tends to zero by Lemma 9.13.1. Proposition 9.3.15 makes evaluation on each fixed test form Cauchy. Define the limiting linear functional by these evaluations. Proposition 9.3.8 gives $|f_\# T(\omega )|\leq L^ m\mathbf M(T)\| \omega \| _{*,\infty }$, so the functional is a current with that mass bound.

If a test form is supported off the compact set $f(K)$, its support has positive distance from $f(K)$. Uniform convergence on $K$ makes its smooth pullback vanish near $K$ for sufficiently small parameters. Hence the limit has support contained in $f(K)$. For a test $(m-1)$-form $\eta $, Proposition 9.3.7 gives

\[ \partial f_\# T(\eta ) =\lim _\varepsilon (f_\varepsilon )_\# T(d\eta ) =\lim _\varepsilon (f_\varepsilon )_\# (\partial T)(\eta ) =f_\# (\partial T)(\eta ). \]

The construction for $\partial T$ is already valid by induction on dimension; it is a compact normal current because $\partial \partial T=0$. Its mass bound proves the boundary estimate and normality. We used limits on tests and mass bounds, not an unproved completeness assertion for the real flat norm or an integral-current compactness theorem. $\square$
