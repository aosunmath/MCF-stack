Proposition 6.4.8 (Smooth densities approximate any Radon measure locally)6D1B7D98305E

Proposition 6.4.8 (Smooth densities approximate any Radon measure locally).6D1B7D98305E Let $\mu $ be a Radon measure on $\mathbb R^ n$ and $0\leq \rho \in C_ c^\infty (B_1)$ with $\int \rho =1$. For $\varepsilon >0$ set

\[ f_\varepsilon (x)=\int \varepsilon ^{-n}\rho ((x-y)/\varepsilon )\, d\mu (y), \qquad d\mu _\varepsilon =f_\varepsilon \, dx. \]

Then $f_\varepsilon $ is nonnegative and smooth, $\mu _\varepsilon $ is Radon, and $\mu _\varepsilon \rightharpoonup \mu $ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Finite total mass of $\mu $ is not required.

Proof

On a fixed compact set of $x$ values, every contributing $y$ lies in a fixed larger compact set. Local finiteness bounds the integrals of every derivative of the smooth kernel there, justifying differentiation under the integral and proving smoothness. For $\varphi \in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$, Fubini and a change of variables give

\[ \int \varphi \, d\mu _\varepsilon =\int \left(\int \varphi (y+\varepsilon z)\rho (z)\, dz\right)d\mu (y). \]

The inner function converges uniformly to $\varphi (y)$ by uniform continuity. For $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$, all these functions have support in the compact one-neighborhood of $\operatorname {spt}\varphi $, whose $\mu $ mass is finite. This proves convergence. Smooth locally bounded densities define Radon measures. The conclusion generalizes the concentration example and the diffuse Cantor approximations in Examples 20.1.2 and 20.1.12. $\square$
