Lemma 7.6.4 (Differentiation on the union of two measurable sets)8A605F7E5FCD

Lemma 7.6.4 (Differentiation on the union of two measurable sets).8A605F7E5FCD Let $M,N\subset U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be Borel, with $U$ open, and have locally finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure in $U$, and put $\lambda =\mathcal H^ m\llcorner (M\cup N)$. At $\mathcal H^ m$-almost every $x\in M\cap N$,

\[ \frac{\mathcal H^ m(M\cap B_ r(x))}{\lambda (B_ r(x))}\to 1, \qquad \frac{\mathcal H^ m(N\cap B_ r(x))}{\lambda (B_ r(x))}\to 1, \qquad \frac{\mathcal H^ m((M\mathbin {\triangle }N)\cap B_ r(x))}{\lambda (B_ r(x))}\to 0. \]

If also $\mathcal H^ m(N\cap B_ r(x))/(\omega _ mr^ m)\to 1$ at such a point, then the symmetric-difference numerator is $o(r^ m)$ and $\lambda (B_ r(x))/(\omega _ mr^ m)\to 1$.

Proof

The union measure is locally finite and Radon. Theorem 6.6.5 applies to the bounded functions $\chi _ M$ and $\chi _ N$. At almost every point of their common part, both values are one and both averages tend to one. Their missing portions have relative measure tending to zero, and their sum is exactly the relative measure of $M\mathbin {\triangle }N$ within the union. This proves the first three limits. If the additional unit-density hypothesis holds, divide $\mathcal H^ m(N\cap B_ r)/(\omega _ mr^ m)$ by $\mathcal H^ m(N\cap B_ r)/\lambda (B_ r)$ to obtain the limit for $\lambda $. Multiplication gives the $o(r^ m)$ estimate. The union, rather than the sum of two restricted measures, avoids double-counting $M\cap N$. $\square$
