Theorem 9.3.2 (Representation of currents with locally finite mass)E73216D55EF5

Theorem 9.3.2 (Representation of currents with locally finite mass).E73216D55EF5 If $T$ has locally finite mass in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, there are a Radon measure $\| T\| $ and a measurable $m$-vector field $\xi $ such that

\[ T(\omega )=\int _ U\langle \omega ,\xi \rangle \, d\| T\| , \qquad |\xi |_{\mathrm{mass}}=1\quad \| T\| \text{-almost everywhere}, \qquad \mathbf M_ W(T)=\| T\| (W). \]

Here $|\cdot |_{\mathrm{mass}}$ is the norm on $m$-vectors dual to comass. The field $\xi $ need not be simple. The current extends uniquely to compactly supported continuous forms, and its integral representation also defines evaluation on bounded Borel forms with compact support. This is the vector-measure representation theorem, obtained from Riesz representation and polar decomposition. Compare [Sim18], Chapter 6, equations (2.8)–(2.10), with the norm convention changed by duality as explained in Remark 2.7. A local proof is supplied below from the scalar representation theorem and finite-dimensional norm duality.

Proof

Lemma 9.7.5 supplies the unique continuous extension. Apply Theorem 9.7.4 with the finite-dimensional space of $m$-forms equipped with comass. Its dual space is the space of $m$-vectors equipped with the mass norm, so its representation gives the required field and unit mass norm. Proposition 9.7.6 identifies the resulting measure with $\| T\| $. Integrating a bounded Borel form of compact support against the measurable polar field is well defined because that support has finite measure. This last operation defines the stated Borel evaluation; it is not a density assertion for the uniform norm on Borel forms. $\square$
