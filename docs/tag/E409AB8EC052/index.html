Remark 19.1.2 (De Giorgi's boundary without smoothness)E409AB8EC052

Remark 19.1.2 (De Giorgi's boundary without smoothness).E409AB8EC052 In his account of the 1950s, Fleming emphasizes De Giorgi's replacement of a smooth boundary by a reduced boundary carrying an approximate normal. A divergence theorem could then survive even when the topological boundary was unsuitable for classical differential geometry. The connection with distributional derivatives of characteristic functions brought the problem into the theory of functions of bounded variation. The modern formulation is Theorem 8.1.5; the historical account is [Fle16], Section 3, pp. 3–4. Fleming also stresses the influence of Caccoppoli's earlier work.
