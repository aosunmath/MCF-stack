Example 20.2.10 (Enneper's surface is immersed and complete but not embedded)6DA30A5B8CCE

Example 20.2.10 (Enneper's surface is immersed and complete but not embedded).6DA30A5B8CCE The polynomial map

\[ E(u,v)=\left(u-\frac{u^3}{3}+uv^2, -v+\frac{v^3}{3}-u^2v,u^2-v^2\right) \]

is a proper complete conformal minimal immersion of $\mathbb R^2$ into $\mathbb R^3$, with metric $(1+u^2+v^2)^2(du^2+dv^2)$. It is not injective: $E(\sqrt3,0)=E(-\sqrt3,0)=(0,0,3)$.

Proof

Differentiation gives $|E_ u|^2=|E_ v|^2=(1+u^2+v^2)^2$ and $\langle E_ u,E_ v\rangle =0$; each coordinate is harmonic. The positive metric factor proves immersion and Lemma 20.2.1 proves minimality. The metric dominates the Euclidean one, giving completeness. With $z=u+iv$, the first two components satisfy $E_1+iE_2=\overline z-z^3/3$, hence $|E_1+iE_2|\geq |z|^3/3-|z|$. This tends to infinity, proving properness. The two displayed parameters prove failure of injectivity. At them, $E_ u=(-2,0,\pm 2\sqrt3)$ and $E_ v=(0,-4,0)$ span different tangent planes, so the image itself has a crossing and is not an embedded surface. This is Enneper's example from [ChMin], Section 15.5, in a fixed polynomial normalization. $\square$
