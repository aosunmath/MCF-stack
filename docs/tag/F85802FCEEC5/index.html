Theorem 16.5.7 (Rigidity in the pointwise gradient estimate)F85802FCEEC5

Theorem 16.5.7 (Rigidity in the pointwise gradient estimate).F85802FCEEC5 Under the hypotheses of Theorem 16.5.4, equality at one point holds if and only if either $u\equiv \pm 1$ or

\[ u(x)=\tanh \left(\frac{e\cdot x-b}{\sqrt2}\right) \quad \text{for some }e\in \mathbb S^{n-1},\ b\in \mathbb R. \]
Proof

By the ordered contact principle of Lemma 16.7.2, a nonconstant solution has $-1<u<1$ everywhere. At an equality point $P=0$, $W(u)>0$ and hence $Du\ne 0$. The strong maximum principle used above makes $P=0$ on that regular component. A finite boundary point of the component would have $Du=0$ and $P=0$, forcing $W(u)=0$, impossible for this nonconstant solution. Thus $P=0$ throughout space.

Let $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$ and $f=q^{-1}(u)$. Since $q'>0$ and $(q')^2=2W(q)$, the equality gives $|Df|=1$. The equation for $u=q(f)$ gives $q'\Delta f=0$, so $f$ is harmonic. Bochner's identity now gives $0=\Delta |Df|^2=2|D^2f|^2$. Therefore $f=e\cdot x-b$ with $|e|=1$. Conversely the displayed layers and constant wells satisfy equality directly. This proves [ChAC], Problem 1(e), including the constant-well alternatives. $\square$
