Remark 19.1.4 (Different notions of a generalized surface)A7B67D7E0642

Remark 19.1.4 (Different notions of a generalized surface).A7B67D7E0642 The history did not produce a single universal replacement for a smooth surface. Fleming describes the influence of de Rham's currents, Whitney's geometric integration theory, and Young's generalized surfaces, before discussing Almgren's varifolds. Orientation and boundary make currents suited to one family of questions; measures recording tangent planes make varifolds suited to another. Allard's 1972 paper [Allard72] developed first-variation, compactness, and regularity tools for the latter setting. See [Fle15], Sections 2 and 8. The modern compatibility theorem of White, Theorem 10.2.3, shows how these viewpoints can interact without identifying their topologies.
