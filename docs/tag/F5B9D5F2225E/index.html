Corollary 11.16.6 (Euclidean-growth rigidity in dimensions at most five)F5B9D5F2225E

Corollary 11.16.6 (Euclidean-growth rigidity in dimensions at most five).F5B9D5F2225E Let $2\leq n\leq 5$. A complete connected smooth two-sided stable minimal immersion in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ has image an affine hyperplane if either its intrinsic balls have volume at most $CR^ n$ for all $R\geq 1$, or the immersion is proper and its extrinsic balls about a fixed center have induced volume at most $CR^ n$ for all $R\geq 1$.

Proof

Choose $p\in [4,4+\sqrt{8/n})$ with $p>n$. Such an exponent exists for $2\leq n\leq 5$. The assumed growth gives $R^{-p}\operatorname {Vol}(\{ r<R\} )\leq CR^{n-p}\to 0$, so Theorem 11.16.5 applies with the appropriate exhaustion. For $n\geq 6$, the interval has no exponent exceeding $n$, so this proof does not give the same conclusion from Euclidean growth. That limitation of the estimate is not a claim of a counterexample in dimension six. This is the dimension restriction explained after [SSY75], Theorem 2. $\square$
