Theorem 6.3.8 (Spine structure and splitting)2C1BC96081EA

Theorem 6.3.8 (Spine structure and splitting).2C1BC96081EA The spine of a nonzero stationary integral $n$-cone is a linear subspace of dimension $d\leq n$, and

\[ S(C)=\{ y:\Theta ^ n(\| C\| ,y)=\Theta ^ n(\| C\| ,0)\} . \]

The vertex has maximal density. After an orthogonal change of coordinates, $C=C_0\times \mathbb R^ d$, where $C_0$ is a stationary integral $(n-d)$-cone; for $d=n$, $C_0$ is a positive integer multiple of the origin. Source: [Minter], Lemma 1.5.

Proof

Proposition 6.9.4 identifies the maximal-density set with a linear spine of dimension at most $n$. If $d=0$, take $C_0=C$. If $1\leq d<n$, Proposition 6.9.7 gives the stationary integral transverse cone, using the locally proved rectifiable coarea theorem, Theorem 3.10.6. If $d=n$, Corollary 6.9.8 gives a positive integer multiple of the spine, with transverse factor $q\delta _0$. These statements identify the weight, tangent planes, multiplicity, and first variation of each factor. $\square$
